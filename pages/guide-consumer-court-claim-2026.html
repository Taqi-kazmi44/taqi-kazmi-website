Faulty Product or Bad Service? How to File a Claim in the Consumer Court

A shop sells you a faulty appliance and refuses to replace it, or a service you paid for is never properly delivered. You do not need an expensive civil suit: every district has a Consumer Court for exactly these disputes. This page states the exact procedure under the Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005 (Act II of 2005), verified against the official statute text.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Check that it is a consumer matter. The Act covers liability for defective products (Sections 4 to 8) and for faulty or defective services (Section 13), where you are the consumer who bought the product or hired the service for a consideration. The definition of consumer expressly includes offline and online transactions through electronic means (Section 2(c), Explanation).
  2. Keep your evidence. The seller is legally required to issue you a receipt showing the date, description of goods, quantity, price and the seller's name and address (Section 19). Keep that receipt, photographs of the defect, the warranty card and the return and refund policy the seller must disclose (Section 20).
  3. Send the manufacturer or service provider a written notice. State that the product is defective or the service is faulty, and call upon them to remedy the defect or pay damages where you have suffered damage (Section 28(1)). Send it by registered post and keep the postal receipt.
  4. The manufacturer or service provider must reply to your notice within 15 days of receiving it (Section 28(2)). Keep proof that the notice was duly delivered: Section 28(3) is explicit that no claim shall be entertained by a Consumer Court unless you have given the notice and provide proof that it was duly delivered but was not responded to.
  5. If they ignore the notice or refuse to put things right, file your claim in the District Consumer Court of the district where the seller carries on business or where the cause of action wholly or partly arose (Section 27). The claim must be filed within 30 days of the arising of the cause of action. The court may extend this for sufficient cause, but never beyond 60 days from the expiry of the warranty or guarantee period, or one year from the date of purchase if no period was specified (Section 28(4)). No court fee is charged for filing a consumer claim, and you may file it yourself or through a lawyer.
  6. On receipt of the claim the court examines it and, if admitted, forwards a copy to the defendant directing a written statement within 15 days, extendable by a further 15 days (Section 30). Both sides then get a chance at settlement at the pre-trial stage (Section 29), after which evidence is recorded and the court passes its order.
  7. If the claim is proved, the Consumer Court may order repair, replacement, refund of the price or charges paid, reasonable compensation, and actual costs including lawyer's fees (Section 31).
  8. Appeal: any person aggrieved by a final order of the Consumer Court may file an appeal in the High Court within 30 days of the order (Section 33). Warning: a claim found to be frivolous or vexatious is dismissed, and the court can fine the claimant up to Rs 10,000 for wilfully instituting a false claim (Section 35).
  9. Sindh: the same procedure applies under the Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014. A consumer must issue a written notice to the manufacturer or service provider, who must respond within 15 days; the complaint is then filed within 30 days of the cause of action (Section 29).

The key legal point

  1. The first step is not the court, it is the written notice. Skip it and the Consumer Court cannot entertain your claim, no matter how strong your evidence is. Send the notice, keep the postal proof, and the court route stays open.
  2. What this means for you: a faulty product does not have to be your loss. The law gives you a free, lawyer-optional forum, but it protects you only if you follow the notice-first sequence.
