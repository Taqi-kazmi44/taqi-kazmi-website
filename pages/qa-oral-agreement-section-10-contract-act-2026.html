Is a Verbal Agreement Legally Valid in Pakistan?

Many people believe that only a written, signed document counts as a contract. The Contract Act, 1872 says otherwise: what matters is whether the agreement meets the legal conditions for a contract, not whether it was spoken or written. But a verbal agreement is much harder to prove, and some transactions still require writing or registration under their own law.

The legal rule: Section 10, Contract Act, 1872

Section 10 provides: "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void." In other words, a verbal agreement can be a contract if there is free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, a lawful object, and nothing in law that expressly declares that kind of agreement void.

The Act does not by itself require writing

Section 10 does not impose a general requirement that a contract be in writing. A spoken offer that is accepted, with something lawful given in return, can therefore create a binding agreement. This is why a spoken promise in a business deal should never be treated as casual: it can bind the person who made it.

The exception: other laws may require writing, attestation or registration

Section 10 also states that nothing in it affects any law in force, not expressly repealed, by which any contract is required to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or any law relating to the registration of documents. So where another law requires a particular transaction to be written, attested or registered, a purely verbal deal will not be enough for that transaction.

The real difficulty: proof

The main problem with a verbal agreement is evidence. Without a written document, the person relying on the agreement must prove what was agreed, usually through witnesses or other evidence, and that is often where disputes arise. Terms that seemed clear in conversation can be remembered very differently later.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: a spoken promise can bind you, so never treat a verbal deal as casual, but always get important agreements in writing to avoid a dispute over what was actually agreed. Keep any messages, receipts or witness details that support the terms.
