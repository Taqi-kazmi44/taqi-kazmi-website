Trusts and NGO Registration (6/6): The Compliance Checklist for Every Form

Registration is the beginning, not the end. Over the last five parts, this series covered four legal forms: the trust, the society, the Section 42 company, and the welfare agency. Each form has its own law, its own registering office, and its own filing calendar. This final part puts all four together in one checklist, so that any organisation, and any donor, can check in minutes whether the legal basics are in place. Keep this post saved. It is the whole series in one page.

The Four Forms At a Glance

A trust is registered under the Sindh Trusts Act, 2020 with the Assistant Director in the Industries and Commerce Directorate. A society is registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 with the Registrar of Societies. A Section 42 company is licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan and registered as a public limited company under the Companies Act, 2017. A welfare agency is registered under the Voluntary Social Welfare Agencies (Registration and Control) Ordinance, 1961 with the Social Welfare Department. The form you chose decides who can inspect your records, what you must file, and what happens if you stop filing. There is no single NGO Act in Pakistan, and no single office that covers all four forms.

Trust: Five Checks

First, registration. Section 12 of the Sindh Trusts Act, 2020 says no trust shall be functional unless it is registered with the Assistant Director. Second, the deed. Section 3 says a trust of immovable property is not valid unless it is declared by a registered written instrument and the ownership is transferred to the trust. Third, the property. Section 15 says all movable and immovable property must be registered in the name of the trust under the Registration Act, 1908. Fourth, the trustee affidavit. Section 10(2) says each trustee accepting the trust must provide an affidavit that the trustee has read and understood the duties and liabilities of a trustee in Chapter IV of the Act. Fifth, renewal. Section 16-A, inserted by the Sindh Trusts (Amendment) Act, 2021, says a trust must apply for renewal of its registration within the thirty days before expiry. And the penalty for getting it wrong: under section 17, the Director may impose a financial penalty of up to one million rupees if a trustee violates the purpose of the trust or fails to provide information, and failure to pay can lead to imprisonment of one month to six months.

Society: Five Checks

First, seven members. Section 1 of the Societies Registration Act, 1860 requires seven or more persons. Second, the memorandum and rules. Section 2 requires the memorandum to state the name, the objects, and the names, addresses and occupations of the governing body, with a copy of the rules certified by not less than three members of the governing body. Third, the annual list. Section 4 requires a list of the names, addresses and occupations of the governing body to be filed with the Registrar once every year, on or before the fourteenth day after the annual general meeting, or in the month of January if the rules do not provide for an annual general meeting. Fourth, changing the objects. Section 12 requires two special meetings a month apart, each approving the change by three-fifths of the members. Fifth, the money. Section 14 says that on dissolution, any property remaining after debts and liabilities must go to another society, never to the members. Also remember section 16-A: the Provincial Government may supersede a governing body for up to one year, after inquiry and hearing, if it cannot discharge its duties or acts against the public interest or the interests of the members.

Section 42 Company: Five Checks

First, the licence. Section 42(6) of the Companies Act, 2017 says no association shall be registered as a company with charitable objects and conditions without a licence under section 42. Second, no dividends. Section 42(1) requires the company to apply its profits and other income to its objects and to prohibit the payment of dividends to its members. Third, filings every year. Section 42(5) makes default in filing financial statements or annual returns for two consecutive financial years a ground for revocation of the licence. Fourth, three members. Section 42(5) also makes the number of members falling below three a ground for revocation. Fifth, the consequences. Section 43 says that on revocation the company must stop all activities except recovery of money owed to it, must not solicit or receive donations, must transfer all assets after debts to another Section 42 company within ninety days, must report to the registrar within fifteen days, and must start voluntary winding up or apply for striking off within thirty days.

Welfare Agency: Five Checks

First, registration before work. Section 3 of the Voluntary Social Welfare Agencies (Registration and Control) Ordinance, 1961 says no agency shall be established or continued except in accordance with the Ordinance. Second, the separate bank account. Section 7(c) requires all moneys received to be paid into a separate account kept in the agency's name at a bank approved by the Registration Authority. Third, the annual filing. Section 7(b) requires the agency to submit its Annual Report and audited accounts to the Registration Authority and to publish them for general information. Fourth, constitution changes. Section 8 says no amendment of the constitution is valid unless approved by the Registration Authority. Fifth, the penalty. Section 14 punishes contravention or a false statement with imprisonment of up to six months, or a fine of up to two thousand rupees, or both, and no court can take cognizance except on a written complaint by the Registration Authority.

Five Rules Every Form Shares

First, the money never goes to the members. A trust must serve its lawful purpose, a society's surplus on dissolution goes to another society, a Section 42 company cannot pay dividends, and a dissolved welfare agency's assets go to another agency with similar objects. Second, audited accounts are not optional. The trust is subject to inspection and penalty under section 17, the society's accounts are part of its record, the Section 42 company files audited financial statements, and the welfare agency must maintain audited accounts and publish them. Third, file every year with the right office. A trust renews with the Assistant Director, a society files its annual list with the Registrar, a Section 42 company files with SECP, and a welfare agency files its Annual Report with the Social Welfare Department. Fourth, changing the constitution takes a procedure. A society needs two meetings a month apart, a Section 42 company has its licence conditions written into its memorandum and articles, and a welfare agency needs the Registration Authority's approval. Fifth, the records are open. The trust is subject to inspection under section 17, a society's record is maintained by the Registrar, a Section 42 company's filings are public record with SECP, and under section 13 of the 1961 Ordinance any person may inspect a welfare agency's documents at the Registration Authority's office on payment of the prescribed fee.

Before You Donate, Check Three Things

First, which form is it, and is it registered with the right office. Second, when did it last file: a trust's renewal, a society's annual list, a Section 42 company's annual return, or a welfare agency's Annual Report and audited accounts. Third, whether its accounts are audited and its money is in an account in its own name. An organisation that cannot answer these three questions is not necessarily dishonest, but it is not compliant, and a donor has every right to ask. One more check for Sindh: organisations that raise charitable funds in the province should also confirm whether the Sindh Charities Registration and Regulation Act, 2019 applies to them, because it adds a separate registration layer with the Sindh Charity Commission.

Choosing the Form: a Quick Guide

Choose a trust when a founder or a family wants to hold property for a defined purpose or beneficiaries, and is ready for registration, renewal and inspection under the 2020 Act. Choose a society when a membership body wants the simplest and oldest form, with seven or more members and an elected governing body. Choose a Section 42 company when the organisation expects large donations or institutional grants, works across provinces, and can carry full company compliance every year. Choose a welfare agency when the work falls in one of the fifteen scheduled welfare fields and the organisation depends on public subscriptions, donations or Government aid. The wrong form is not a small mistake. It decides the regulator, the filings, and the consequences of a missed deadline.

Common Mistakes Across All Four Forms

First, registering and then forgetting the annual filing, which is the single most common failure in every form. Second, mixing the organisation's money with personal money, which every form forbids in its own way. Third, changing the objects or the constitution informally, without the meeting, approval or filing the law requires. Fourth, letting the membership or the governing body fall below the legal minimum. Fifth, assuming the certificate is permanent, when every form has a power to suspend, supersede, revoke or dissolve. Sixth, quoting an old law: the Trusts Act, 1882 for a Sindh trust, or the original 2020 text of sections 7 and 9 without the 2021 amendment.

What This Means for You

If you run an organisation, work through the five checks for your form today, and fix the oldest missed filing first. If you donate, ask the three donor questions before the next donation. And if you are choosing a form, decide on the basis of who will control it, who will fund it, and how much compliance you can sustain every year, not on which form is quickest to register.
