How to File a Claim for Unpaid Wages: A Step-by-Step Guide

Your employer has stopped paying your salary or keeps cutting your wages without a lawful reason. You do not have to wait in silence. The Payment of Wages Act, 1936 gives you a dedicated forum to claim your dues, with compensation on top.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. Gather your proof. Appointment letter, salary slips, bank statements, attendance records, and any written demand you sent to the employer. Written evidence decides these claims.
  2. Know the right forum. Under Section 15(1) of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the provincial government appoints an Authority to decide claims of illegal wage deductions, delayed wages, and unpaid provident fund or gratuity dues payable under any law.
  3. File your application. You can apply yourself, through your lawyer, through an official of a registered trade union authorised in writing, or through an Inspector of the Act. If the worker has died, his heirs may apply (Section 15(2)).
  4. Mind the deadline. Apply within three years of the date the deduction was made or the date the wages fell due. The Authority can admit a late application only if you show sufficient cause for the delay (proviso to Section 15(2)).
  5. Know what the Authority can award. After hearing you and the employer, the Authority can direct the refund of the deducted amount or the payment of the delayed wages, together with compensation of up to ten times the amount deducted (Section 15(3)). No compensation is awarded for delay where the delay was caused by a bona fide dispute, an emergency, or the worker's own failure to claim the wages.
  6. Claim as a group. Workers of the same establishment whose wages for the same wage period remain unpaid can present one joint application (Section 16).
  7. Know the appeal and the bar. An appeal against the Authority's direction lies within 30 days before the Labour Court; the employer must deposit the awarded amount to appeal (Section 17). No civil court can hear a claim that could have been recovered through this forum (Section 22).

The governing law

The procedure is governed by the Payment of Wages Act, 1936 (IV of 1936), which applies to persons employed in factories and, by provincial notification, in industrial establishments. Section 5 fixes strict time limits for wage payment (before the expiry of the 7th day of the next month where fewer than 1,000 persons are employed, the 10th day otherwise), Section 7 permits only the deductions expressly authorised by the Act, and Section 15 creates the fast, dedicated claim forum with compensation. Wages must be paid in current coin or currency notes (Section 6), and any agreement giving up a right under the Act is void (Section 23).

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: unpaid salary is not a favour the employer can withhold at will. It is a legal claim you can enforce through a dedicated forum that can also award compensation, so document everything from the first delayed month.
