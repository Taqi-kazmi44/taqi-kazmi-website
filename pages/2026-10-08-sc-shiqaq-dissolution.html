SC: Marriage Can Be Dissolved on Shiqaq Even When Cruelty Is Not Proven

Supreme Court of Pakistan, five-member bench headed by Justice Shahid Waheed: a marriage can be dissolved on grounds of shiqaq (marital discord) under Section 2(ix) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, even when cruelty was not proven. Family courts cannot convert a cruelty claim into khula without the wife's voluntary consent; dower-return directions set aside. Cases: Zainab Bibi and Imran Naz Begum, reported 7 Oct 2026.

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has ruled that a marriage can be dissolved on grounds of shiqaq (marital discord) under the residuary provision of Section 2(ix) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, even where cruelty was not established.

In a 15-page judgment authored by Justice Shahid Waheed, a five-member bench (Justices Naeem Akhter Afghan, Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, Ishtiaq Ibrahim and Miangul Hassan Aurangzeb) decided the connected petitions of Zainab Bibi and Imran Naz Begum. The family courts had found cruelty unproven but dissolved both marriages through khula, without the wives' consent, and directed them to return their dower.

The Court held this conversion an error: khula proceeds from a wife's voluntary election and ordinarily carries financial consequences, so decrees of khula without the wife's clear and voluntary consent cannot be sustained. Shiqaq, by contrast, is an objectively established condition of serious matrimonial breach and does not depend on the wife surrendering dower. The dissolutions stand on the basis of shiqaq under Section 2(ix), and the dower-return directions were set aside.

The test is strict: mere incompatibility, occasional disagreement, anger, short separation or matrimonial litigation alone are not enough. The breach must be genuine, serious and continuing, the marriage must have ceased to serve its essential purposes, and reconciliation must have failed despite meaningful judicial effort.
