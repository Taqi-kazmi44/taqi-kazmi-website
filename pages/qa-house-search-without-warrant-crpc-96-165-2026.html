Can the Police Search Your Home Without a Warrant?

One of the most common questions ordinary citizens ask: can the police walk into your house and search it without showing you a warrant? The verified answer is that, generally, they cannot. A search warrant from a court is the normal rule, and the law allows only one narrow exception.

The general rule: a search warrant under Section 96 CrPC

Section 96 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 says a court may issue a search warrant where it has reason to believe that a person will not produce a document or thing when asked, where the document or thing is not known to be in any person's possession, or where a general search or inspection will serve the purposes of an inquiry, trial or other proceeding under the Code. In plain words, a search of your home normally requires a warrant issued by a court.

The only exception: Section 165 CrPC

Section 165, titled "Search by police-officer," allows the officer in charge of a police station, or a police officer making an investigation, to search without a warrant, but only on strict conditions. He must have reasonable grounds for believing that something necessary for the investigation may be found in a place within the limits of his police station, and that the thing cannot be obtained without undue delay. Before searching, he must record in writing the grounds of his belief and specify in that writing, as far as possible, the thing for which the search is to be made. The Lahore High Court reaffirmed this limit in November 2026, holding that a police officer's mere apprehension, without more, cannot justify a search without a warrant.

The safeguards: Section 103 CrPC

Section 103 requires that, before a search, the officer must call upon two or more respectable inhabitants of the locality to attend and witness the search, and the search must be made in their presence. A list of all things seized must be prepared and signed by the witnesses. The occupant of the place searched, or a person on his behalf, must in every instance be permitted to attend during the search, and a copy of the signed list must be delivered to the occupant on request. A woman can only be searched by another woman, with strict regard to decency, under Section 52 CrPC. Article 14 of the Constitution of Pakistan also protects the dignity of man and, subject to law, the privacy of the home.

If the police come to search your home

  1. Ask to see the search warrant. If there is no warrant, ask the officer to show the written grounds recorded under Section 165 CrPC and to name the thing being searched for.
  2. Insist that two respectable residents of your locality are called to witness the search under Section 103 CrPC, and stay present yourself or through a representative.
  3. Ask for a copy of the signed list of everything seized. If the search was carried out without a warrant, without written grounds or without local witnesses, raise it before the magistrate and keep a record of what happened.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: your home cannot be searched on mere suspicion. Either there is a court-issued search warrant, or the police must satisfy the strict written-grounds conditions of Section 165 CrPC, with two local witnesses present under Section 103 CrPC.
