Can a wife be denied maintenance simply because rukhsati has not taken place?

Can a wife be denied maintenance simply because rukhsati has not taken place?
The Supreme Court of Pakistan has given a clear, historic answer in Ambreen Akram vs Asad Ullah Khan.

In 2026 SCMR 1, the Court held that a wife's right to maintenance (nan-o-nafqa) attaches with the nikah itself. It is not conditional on rukhsati, consummation, or obedience. Rukhsati has no independent legal status under the law. The Court further held that if a husband refuses to pay maintenance, he must prove with clear and strong evidence that the wife has wholly and without cause brought the relationship to an end.

For law students, this judgment is a masterclass: the application of Articles 14, 25, and 35 of the Constitution to family law, gender-sensitive judicial language, and the institutional use of amicus curiae. For every citizen, the message is plain: no woman in Pakistan can be deprived of this fundamental right.

The Court also rejected the old "maintenance for obedience" concept of ihtibas, holding it against the Quranic vision of nikah built on affection and compassion.

Case: Ambreen Akram vs Asad Ullah Khan, 2026 SCMR 1. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 11 September 2025. Bench: Syed Mansoor Ali Shah and Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, JJ.
