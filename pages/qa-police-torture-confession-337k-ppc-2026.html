Can the Police Beat a Confession Out of You in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions ordinary people ask: can the police use force, beatings or torture in custody to make a person confess? The answer in Pakistani law is a clear no. The Constitution forbids it, the Pakistan Penal Code punishes it, and a confession obtained that way has no value in court.

Article 14(2) of the Constitution: no torture to extract evidence

Article 14(2) of the Constitution of Pakistan, 1973, says: no person shall be subjected to torture for the purpose of extracting evidence. This is a fundamental right. No police officer, and no other public servant, has the legal power to beat or torture a person in custody to get a statement or a confession.

Section 337-K of the Pakistan Penal Code: causing hurt to extort a confession

Section 337-K of the Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, is titled "Causing hurt to extort confession, or to compel restoration of property." It says: whoever causes hurt for the purpose of extorting from the sufferer, or any person interested in the sufferer, any confession or any information which may lead to the detection of any offence or misconduct, or for the purpose of constraining the sufferer to restore property or valuable security, or to satisfy any claim or demand, shall, in addition to the punishment of qisas, arsh or daman, as the case may be, provided for the kind of hurt caused, be punished, having regard to the nature of the hurt caused, with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years as ta'zir.

In plain words: if a police officer, or anyone else, hurts a person to force a confession out of him, that is itself a crime punishable with imprisonment up to ten years, on top of the punishment for the hurt itself.

A confession made to the police has no value in court

Article 38 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984, says that no confession made to a police officer shall be proved against a person accused of any offence. So even a statement given to the police, without any force, cannot be used against the accused. A confession only carries weight when it is made before a magistrate, under Section 164 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, with the safeguards that section requires.

The Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act, 2022

Parliament also passed a dedicated law, the Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act, 2022 (Act No. XXVIII of 2022). Section 3 of the Act says that any statement, information or confession obtained through torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment by a public official is inadmissible in any proceedings against the person who made it. In Zubaida Qureshi v. Asj etc., 2024 LHC 3636, the Lahore High Court, Bahawalpur Bench, held that the Act of 2022 is a federal law that gives a new remedy against torture by public officials, and directed the petitioner to file a complaint with the Federal Investigation Agency under the Act.

What this means for you

A confession beaten out of someone has no value in court, and the officer who tortures can be punished. If torture happens in custody, get a medical examination done at once and preserve the medical report as evidence, note the names and numbers of the officers involved, and inform the family immediately. A complaint can be made to the Federal Investigation Agency under the Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act, 2022. In an emergency, the family can also approach the High Court for habeas corpus to produce the detained person before the court.

The key legal point

  1. No. Torture to extract a confession is forbidden by Article 14(2) of the Constitution, and Section 337-K of the Pakistan Penal Code punishes causing hurt to extort a confession with imprisonment up to ten years as ta'zir, in addition to qisas, arsh or daman.
  2. Under Section 3 of the Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act, 2022, a statement or confession obtained through torture is inadmissible in any proceedings, and a complaint can be made to the Federal Investigation Agency (2024 LHC 3636).
