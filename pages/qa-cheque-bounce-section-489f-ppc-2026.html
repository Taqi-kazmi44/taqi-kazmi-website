Is a Bounced Cheque a Crime in Pakistan? The Answer Is in Section 489-F PPC

One of the most common questions business owners and ordinary people ask: if someone gives me a cheque and it bounces, is that a crime? The answer is yes, but only when the cheque was issued dishonestly. Section 489-F of the Pakistan Penal Code, 1860 makes it an offence to dishonestly issue a cheque towards repayment of a loan or fulfilment of an obligation, if that cheque is dishonoured on presentation. What Section 489-F PPC says

Whoever dishonestly issues a cheque towards repayment of a loan or fulfilment of an obligation which is dishonoured on presentation, shall be punished, unless he proves the defence written into the section. The three ingredients are:

  1. The cheque was issued dishonestly. Dishonesty is the core element: an honest mistake or a banking error, without dishonest intent, is not covered by this section.
  2. The cheque was issued towards repayment of a loan or fulfilment of an obligation. A cheque with no underlying loan or obligation does not fall under Section 489-F.
  3. The cheque was dishonoured on presentation. The offence completes when the bank refuses to honour it.

Punishment

  1. Imprisonment which may extend to three years, or fine, or both. This is the punishment stated in the section itself.

The key legal point

  1. The section itself places the burden of proof on the issuer. He escapes punishment only if he establishes that he had made arrangements with his bank to ensure that the cheque would be honoured, and that the bank was at fault in not honouring it.
  2. What this means for you: never issue a cheque unless you are sure the funds or arrangements are in place. A dishonoured cheque is not just an embarrassment, it is a criminal offence under Section 489-F PPC.
