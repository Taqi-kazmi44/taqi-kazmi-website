FCC Defines Harassment: Only Conduct of Sexual Nature Counts Under the 2010 Act

A three-judge Federal Constitutional Court bench headed by Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan set aside the Islamabad High Court's December 31, 2024 judgment in a National Bank of Pakistan workplace harassment case, holding the definition of harassment under the 2010 Act is exhaustive, not extensive.

What was reported

  1. A three-judge FCC bench headed by Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan, also comprising Justice Ali Baqar Najafi and Justice Syed Arshad Hussain Shah, decided a joint appeal by Usman Shahid and Aqeel Abbas against the IHC's December 31, 2024 judgment.
  2. The 18-page judgment, authored by Justice Najafi, set aside the IHC judgment in a National Bank of Pakistan workplace harassment case.
  3. The dispute stemmed from a complaint filed in 2012 by NBP employee Nadia Sarwar. The Federal Ombudsman had awarded the major penalty of compulsory retirement under Section 4(4)(ii)(b) of the Act in September 2013; the President set it aside in 2014 directing censure instead, and the IHC restored the Ombudsman's order in full.
  4. The FCC has now reversed that restoration, ruling on the scope of the definition of harassment itself.

The key legal point

  1. The definition of harassment under Section 2(h) of the Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010 is exhaustive, not extensive.
  2. An act, conduct or attitude must be sexual in nature to constitute actionable harassment under the Act.
  3. The Ombudsperson has no jurisdiction to inquire into disciplinary proceedings beyond this scope.
  4. The court stated: "Every sexual harassment was harassment but every harassment was not a sexual harassment."
  5. Why it matters: the Act exists to protect the honour, modesty and respect of working women by virtue of their gender, and it cannot be applied as a general disciplinary tool. Knowing exactly what the law covers helps genuine complaints get taken seriously.
