Can a Landlord Evict a Tenant Without a Court Order?

One of the most common questions ordinary citizens ask: can the owner of a rented house force the tenant out, change the locks, or cut off the utilities without going to court? No. The Punjab Rented Premises Act, 2009 lays down a fixed legal process for eviction, and self-help eviction is not part of it.

The legal rule: eviction only on listed grounds (Section 15, Punjab Rented Premises Act 2009)

Section 15, titled "Grounds for eviction", is exhaustive. A landlord may seek eviction of the tenant only if: (a) the period of tenancy has expired; (b) the tenant has failed to pay or tender the rent within thirty days after the expiry of the period stipulated in section 7; (c) the tenant has committed breach of a term or condition of the tenancy agreement; (d) the tenant has committed a violation of an obligation under section 13; (e) the tenant has used the premises for a purpose different from the purpose for which it was let out; or (f) the tenant has sub-let the premises without the prior written consent of the landlord.

If none of these grounds exists, there is no legal basis for eviction at all. Personal dislike, a better offer from another tenant, or family pressure are not grounds under the Act.

Only the Rent Tribunal can order eviction (Section 16)

Section 13(3) provides that a landlord who wants the tenant out must file an application to the Rent Tribunal, which may then direct the tenant to fulfill the obligation or order eviction. Section 16 establishes the Rent Tribunal and gives it exclusive jurisdiction over cases under this Act. In plain terms: even where a valid ground under Section 15 exists, the landlord has to go to the tribunal and get an order. Changing the locks, disconnecting electricity or water, or throwing the tenant's belongings out without an order is illegal.

What the landlord must actually do

The proper steps are: (1) give the tenant a written notice identifying the default or violation and asking for it to be remedied within a stated time, as contemplated by Section 13(2); (2) if the tenant does not comply, file an eviction application before the Rent Tribunal on one of the Section 15 grounds; (3) obtain the tribunal's eviction order. A tenant who faces threats, lockouts, or disconnection can approach the Rent Tribunal or the police against the forced action.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if you are a tenant, no one can lawfully push you out without an order of the Rent Tribunal. If you are a landlord, follow the legal process or your eviction can be challenged and set aside.
