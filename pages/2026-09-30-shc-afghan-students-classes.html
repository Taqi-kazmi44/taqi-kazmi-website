SHC Restores Afghan Medical Students to Classes

The Sindh High Court has ordered Jinnah Sindh Medical University to restore Afghan medical students' access to classes and stopped their eviction from university hostels, ruling their education must not be disrupted while their case against the PMDC return directive is pending.

Barred from classes, locked out of hostels. Now the Sindh High Court has told the university: their education will not be disrupted.

In petitions by Afghan medical students (29 in one petition, 3 in another), argued by Advocate Iftikhar Shah, the SHC ordered Jinnah Sindh Medical University to restore the students' access to classes and stopped their eviction from university hostels.

Background: the court had already suspended the PMDC directive sending Afghan students back, on September 14. Despite that protection, the university barred the petitioners from classes, disabled their biometric access and moved to evict them. The court has now made clear their education must continue until the next hearing.

Note: the PMDC had barred new admissions and placements for Afghan nationals, while the Foreign Office's position is that students with valid visas enrolled in recognised courses can complete their education. Similar interim relief has been granted by courts in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
