SBP Launches PECS: Cheques to Clear Through Images from 15 Oct 2026

The State Bank of Pakistan has decided to introduce the Pakistan Express Clearing System (PECS) to strengthen the digital payments ecosystem and make cheque clearing faster and more efficient. In a circular issued on 5 October 2026 by its Digital Innovation and Settlements Department, the central bank announced that cheques will now be cleared on the basis of captured images instead of physical instruments. The concept used is "cheque truncation" as defined in Section 2(zl) of the Payment Systems and Electronic Fund Transfers Act, 2007.

What the circular says

  1. PECS will be based on cheque truncation: instead of physically moving cheques between banks, the presenting or collecting bank captures an image of the cheque or instrument and transmits it to the clearinghouse (NIFT), which forwards it to the paying or drawing bank for processing.
  2. Implementation starts on 15 October 2026 on a pilot basis with selected clearinghouse participants. It then extends to all participants from 16 November to 31 December 2026, with full nationwide coverage by 30 April 2027.
  3. The central bank has directed all financial institutions to train relevant staff on the truncation process, comply with applicable laws including the Payment Systems and Electronic Fund Transfers Act, 2007 and the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, and create public awareness about PECS through print, electronic and social media.
  4. Participating institutions must nominate focal persons for PECS implementation and report progress to SBP, and must meet prescribed clearing timelines, customer service standards and settlement obligations throughout the rollout.

The key legal point

  1. The legal basis is Section 2(zl) of the Payment Systems and Electronic Fund Transfers Act, 2007, which defines cheque truncation: the electronic image of a cheque can stand in for the physical instrument in the clearing process. The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 continues to govern the cheque itself.
  2. For the public, this means faster clearing and settlement: no waiting while a physical cheque travels between banks, and less risk of a cheque being lost or delayed in transit.
  3. Practical note: you will still write cheques exactly as before. The change happens behind the scenes at the bank, which captures and transmits the image. Keep your cheque book safe as always, and check your account after depositing a cheque to confirm the credit.
