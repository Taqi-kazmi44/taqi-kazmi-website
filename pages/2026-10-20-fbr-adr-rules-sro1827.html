FBR ADR Rules 2026: Settle Tax Disputes in 90 Days (SRO 1827(I)/2026)

Your tax dispute with the FBR can now be settled without spending years in court. The FBR has notified revised Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) rules for tax cases: a taxpayer can now ask a three-member committee, chaired by a retired judge, to decide the dispute within 90 days.

The Federal Board of Revenue has notified revised Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) rules for tax cases through SRO 1827(I)/2026, dated 15 October 2026. The rules substitute Rule 231C of the Income Tax Rules, 2002, under Section 134A of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001.

ADR is open where the disputed tax liability is Rs 50 million or more, or where the dispute is about the admissibility of a refund, the extent of waiver of default surcharge and penalty, or other specific relief. It is closed where criminal proceedings have started.

The three-member committee must decide within 90 days of appointment, by majority, and recovery of the disputed tax stays suspended until its decision or dissolution. The committee is chaired by a retired judge of a High Court, the Federal Constitutional Court or the Supreme Court with tax or commercial experience, alongside an Inland Revenue officer not below BS-21 and the taxpayer's own nominee.

You apply in writing to the FBR in the prescribed form, nominate your own member for the committee with CNIC and contact details, and your nominee proposes three retired judges for the chairperson. The committee must be appointed within 15 days of the application.

Fees are fixed: Rs 300,000 for the chairperson and Rs 150,000 per member where the disputed tax is up to Rs 50 million, rising to Rs 500,000 and Rs 250,000 above that. An Inland Revenue officer serving on the committee gets no fee.

What this means for you: if you have a stuck refund or a heavy tax dispute, there is now a time-bound route to settle it outside court, and the FBR cannot recover the disputed amount while the committee sits.
