FBR Extends Income Tax Return Filing Deadline to 15 October 2026

The Federal Board of Revenue has extended the income tax return filing deadline for Tax Year 2026 from 30 September to 15 October 2026, through an official notification under Section 214A of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, issued on 1 October 2026. Hours earlier, a fake extension circular had circulated on social media, which FBR publicly declared fake.

What was reported

  1. FBR extended the income tax return filing deadline for Tax Year 2026 from 30 September to 15 October 2026. The extension came through an official FBR notification issued on 1 October 2026, under Section 214A of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001.
  2. The extension was granted on the request of various trade bodies and tax bar associations. It applies to every person who was required to file the return by 30 September 2026.
  3. By the original midnight deadline of 30 September, 5.767 million returns had been filed, up 45 percent from 3.98 million in the corresponding period last year, with Rs77.3 billion tax paid with the returns.
  4. FPCCI President Atif Ikram Sheikh had urged a one-month extension to 31 October 2026; FBR granted 15 days instead.
  5. FBR Chairman Rashid Mahmood Langrial cautioned, while briefing the Senate Standing Committee on Finance (chaired by Senator Saleem Mandviwalla), that retailers who fail to file under the Tax Asaan Scheme will face enforcement action within a month.

The key legal point

  1. The extension has a legal basis: Section 214A of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 empowers FBR to extend deadlines, and it was exercised through a formal notification, not an announcement. A notification is the document that matters; everything else is commentary.
  2. This matters because of what happened hours before the real notification: a document titled "Circular No. 4 of 2026-27 IR-Operations", dated 30 September 2026, claiming an extension, circulated on social media. FBR publicly declared that document fake through its official channels. Filing decisions made on the strength of a fake circular carry the same legal risk as filing late.
  3. Why it matters: tax deadlines are not flexible by nature, they are flexible only when the authority empowered by law says so in writing. Before acting on any deadline news, check FBR's own website or its verified accounts. The genuine notification text followed within hours of the fake one being debunked, and that is the only version with legal effect.
  4. Practical note: the new deadline is 15 October 2026. If you missed 30 September, you now have a lawful window, but the window does not remove the risk of late-filer consequences if you miss it again.
