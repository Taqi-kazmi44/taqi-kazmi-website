Lahore Police Proposes Making Fraud Under Section 420 PPC Non-Bailable

Lahore police's senior command has proposed amending the 166-year-old Section 420 of the Pakistan Penal Code to make cheating non-bailable, cancelled more than 200 wrongly registered Section 406 FIRs, and issued new SOPs for breach-of-trust investigations.

What was reported

  1. Lahore police's senior command has proposed amendments to the 166-year-old Section 420 of the Pakistan Penal Code (cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property) to make the offence non-bailable, following the path India took in 1974, to extend relief to fraud victims amid escalating cheating and fraud complaints.
  2. The trigger: the higher judiciary repeatedly took up FIRs lodged under Section 406 PPC (criminal breach of trust) and held that in most cases criminal breach of trust could not be established, calling it a grave miscarriage of justice resulting in a person being jailed for an offence not prima facie made out.
  3. In response, the investigation wing of Lahore police has cancelled more than 200 cases lodged under Section 406 PPC in various city police stations, and issued new Standard Operating Procedures to be followed strictly in future investigations under these sections.
  4. A senior official explained that most fraud and cheating complaints were liable to be treated under Section 420, but police had been lodging FIRs under non-bailable Section 406, causing embarrassment for the police high command when higher courts took them up. Amendments to Section 420 were proposed as an alternative way to punish fraudsters.
  5. The decisions were taken in a meeting chaired by Lahore Capital City Police Officer Bilal Siddique Kamyana, with Investigation DIG Zeshan Raza, SSP Mohammad Naveed and all divisional investigation SPs present; separate follow-up meetings were also held.
  6. Lawyer Omais Chaudhry said his firm had already proposed similar amendments to the Punjab government's law reforms committee. He noted rising fraud complaints amid massive investment in property and online businesses, ranging from land scams, fake investment schemes and business fraud to digital and cyber fraud, and that victims rarely get justice due to procedural delays, lack of expertise and poor enforcement. Under the current provisions, hardly any suspects are punished in Punjab.

The key legal point

Under the new SOPs, issued in light of Supreme Court judgments, merely not returning money or breaching a contractual obligation does not constitute an offence under Section 406 PPC. For Section 406 to apply, entrustment of the property and its dishonest misappropriation or conversion in breach of trust must be established. Section 420 PPC (cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property) is currently a bailable offence; the proposal would make it non-bailable, which would mean an accused could not claim release on bail as a matter of right.
