Muhammad Nasir v. The State, 2026 SCMR 224: Broken Chain of Safe Custody in a Narcotics Case Means Acquittal

The Supreme Court of Pakistan held that in narcotics cases the prosecution must prove an unbroken chain of safe custody from recovery to the forensic laboratory and the court.

The petitioner was convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment after 26 kg of charas was allegedly recovered from a secret cavity in his car. The question before the Supreme Court was whether the prosecution had proved the recovery and the safe custody of the case property beyond reasonable doubt.

The Court held that in narcotics cases, an unbroken chain of safe custody from the recovery of the contraband to its delivery in court must be strictly established by the prosecution. Contradictions among the prosecution witnesses about the recovery, and the failure to establish safe custody of the sample parcels, broke that chain.

The petition for leave to appeal was converted into an appeal and allowed. The conviction and the life sentence were set aside, and the petitioner was acquitted, because the prosecution had failed to prove its case beyond reasonable doubt.

What this means for you: a conviction cannot rest on seized material alone. The state must prove, step by step, that what was seized is exactly what was tested and produced in court, and any break in that chain gives the accused the benefit of the doubt.

Reference: Muhammad Nasir v. The State, 2026 SCMR 224, Supreme Court of Pakistan, bench: Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, Ishtiaq Ibrahim and Ali Baqar Najafi, JJ, decided 15 October 2025, Crl. PLA No. 926 of 2021, under section 9(c), Control of Narcotic Substances Act, 1997.
