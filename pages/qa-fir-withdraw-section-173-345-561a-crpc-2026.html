Can an FIR Be Taken Back Once It Is Registered in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask after filing a police complaint: we have settled the matter, can the FIR be withdrawn now? The verified answer is no, not by the complainant herself. Once registered, an FIR is a state document, and it can only end through three lawful routes stated in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898.

Why the complainant cannot withdraw it herself

Registration of an FIR for a cognizable offence is mandatory under Section 154 CrPC (PLD 2016 SC 581). From the moment of registration, the FIR is not a private document of the complainant, it becomes the state's case. The police station cannot simply erase or close a registered FIR at the complainant's request.

The three lawful ways an FIR can end

  1. Cancellation report: Section 173 CrPC. If the investigation finds that no offence was committed, the police submits its final report under Section 173 CrPC, and the area Magistrate cancels the FIR.
  2. Quashing: Section 561-A CrPC. The High Court can quash an FIR under Section 561-A CrPC where continuing the proceedings would be an abuse of the process of court.
  3. Compromise: Section 345 CrPC. In compoundable offences, the parties can file their compromise under Section 345 CrPC, and the court acquits the accused. For non-compoundable offences, the parties move the High Court for quashing on the basis of the compromise.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if you settled the dispute with the other side after filing an FIR, your lawyer must file the compromise in court or move the High Court for quashing. The police station alone cannot close a registered FIR.
