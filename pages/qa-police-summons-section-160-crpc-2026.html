Can the Police Call You to the Police Station?

A very common question: if the police telephone you or send a message asking you to come to the thana, do you have to go? The short answer is yes, but only in a limited way, and only if the call comes through the proper legal channel.

The rule: Section 160 CrPC

Section 160 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, titled "Police-officer's power to require attendance of witnesses", says that a police officer making an investigation may, by order in writing, require the attendance before himself of any person, being within the limits of his own or any adjoining station, who, from the information given or otherwise, appears to be acquainted with the circumstances of the case, and such person shall attend as so required.

Three limits are built into the section itself. First, the order must be in writing. A verbal telephone call or message is not a Section 160 order. Second, the person called must appear to be acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case under investigation. Third, the person must be within the limits of the officer's own police station or an adjoining police station.

This power is for witnesses, not the accused

Section 160 is a power to call witnesses for investigation. It does not authorise the police to require the attendance of an accused person with a view to his answering the charge. If you are being called, ask whether you are being called as a witness in a case under investigation, and ask to see the written order.

Who is protected: women and boys under fifteen

The proviso to Section 160 says that no male person under the age of fifteen years or woman shall be required to attend at any place other than the place in which such male person or woman resides. In plain words, a woman or a boy under fifteen cannot be made to come to the police station at all. If their statement is needed, the police must record it at their residence.

What happens if you do not attend: Section 174 PPC

If a person intentionally fails to attend after a lawful written order from a public servant, Section 174 of the Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, punishes the non-attendance with simple imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with fine which may extend to Rs. 1,500, or with both. So a proper written order under Section 160 should not be ignored, but a casual verbal call carries no such compulsion.

If the police call you to the station

  1. Ask for the written order under Section 160 and note the case or FIR number, the investigating officer's name and the reason you are being called.
  2. Check whether you are being called as a witness. If you are being treated as an accused, your rights are different, including the right to consult a lawyer of your choice under Article 10(1) of the Constitution.
  3. If you are a woman, or a boy under fifteen, you cannot be required to attend the station. Ask the police to record your statement at your residence.
  4. Keep a record of every call, message or written order, and do not hand over your mobile phone or documents without a lawful order.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: the police can call you to the thana, but only as a witness, only by a written order under Section 160 CrPC, and only if you live within that station's limits or an adjoining station. Women and boys under fifteen cannot be made to come to the station at all.
