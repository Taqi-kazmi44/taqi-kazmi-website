FBR Goes Faceless: Tax Audits and Assessments Without Meeting an Officer

From 15 October 2026, your tax audit may happen without you ever meeting a tax officer. The FBR has enforced a faceless audit and assessment framework run entirely online through the National Faceless Centre in Islamabad.

From 15 October 2026, the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) has implemented tax audits, income tax assessments and recovery proceedings through the National Faceless Centre (NFC) established in Islamabad, operating with AI-assisted case handling.

SRO 1828(I)/2026 enforces Chapter XX (Rules 233 to 245) of the Income Tax Rules, 2002, for cases designated under Section 122E of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. It covers audits under sections 177 and 214C, proceedings under section 111, and assessments under Chapter X of the Ordinance.

SRO 1825(I)/2026 enforces Chapter VIAC of the Sales Tax Rules, 2006, applying the faceless system to sales tax recovery proceedings, also from 15 October 2026.

Every case passes through three separate officers: one audits, one assesses, and one reviews for quality control (Rule 242) before any order is issued. No single officer controls a case from start to finish.

All notices carry a Digital Identification Number (Rule 238), and all replies and documents are filed electronically on the IRIS system (Rule 239). Physical meetings end. Field verification, where needed, goes through the Field Operations Wing (Rule 241), and penalty and surcharge proceedings are also handled facelessly (Rule 243).

Cases are selected by a computerised risk-based system and assigned to officers automatically. The taxpayer will not know which officer is handling the file, and the officer has no say in case allocation. The legal authority for the framework comes from the Finance Act 2026 (Section 227D).

What this means for you: if your case is selected, all replies and documents must be filed electronically on IRIS, and you will not know which officer is handling your file. Keep your tax records complete and digital.
