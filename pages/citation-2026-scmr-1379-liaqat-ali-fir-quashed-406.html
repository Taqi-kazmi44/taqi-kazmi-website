2026 SCMR 1379: FIR Under 406 PPC Quashed Where Property Dispute Was Civil

Liaqat Ali v. The State. Decided 28 January 2026 by Malik Shahzad Ahmad Khan and Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, JJ. Criminal Appeal No. 10-L of 2026.

WhatsApp inquiryTaqi Kazmi website

The facts

Liaqat Ali, a property dealer, showed a house to a complainant for mortgage. A written mortgage agreement was executed for Rs. 1,200,000, of which Rs. 700,000 was received as advance. The complainant alleged the accused neither returned the amount nor gave possession of the house, claiming his money was lying with them as "amanat" (trust), and got FIR No. 3299 dated 17.10.2025 registered under Section 406 PPC at Police Station Islampura, District Lahore. The Lahore High Court dismissed the pre-arrest bail, and the accused approached the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court's holding

The Court converted the bail petition into an appeal under Article 187 of the Constitution, set aside the orders of the Lahore High Court and the Additional Sessions Judge, and quashed the FIR:

  1. Merely mentioning the word "amanat" in the FIR does not attract Section 406 PPC where no trust was created and it was never specified how that trust was to be discharged. The ingredients of criminal breach of trust under Section 405 PPC were not attracted.
  2. The amount was paid in a property deal under a written mortgage agreement. At most it was a case of civil nature, regarding specific performance of the agreement or recovery of money, for which the civil court is the proper forum.
  3. The complainant had mala fidely set criminal machinery into motion to blackmail and pressurize the petitioner and the co-accused by converting a civil dispute into a criminal offence, which is an abuse of the process of law.
  4. Where no criminal offence is made out and there is no probability of conviction, asking the accused to first approach the trial court under Section 249-A Cr.P.C. would be a sheer abuse of process. The Supreme Court may directly convert the petition into one for quashment of the FIR.
  5. The Court relied on its earlier precedents, including Miraj Khan v. Gul Ahmad (2000 SCMR 122), where quashment of an FIR under Section 561-A Cr.P.C. was approved without first moving under Section 249-A Cr.P.C. in a similar situation.

What this means for you

  1. If someone files a criminal FIR against you over what is really a property or money dispute, the law treats that as a civil matter, and the FIR can be quashed.
  2. Writing the word "amanat" in an FIR does not by itself turn a money dispute into criminal breach of trust under Section 406 PPC.
