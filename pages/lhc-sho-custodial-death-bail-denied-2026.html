LHC Rejects Bail for SHO in Custodial Death Case

A police officer accused of killing a man in custody has been denied bail. The Lahore High Court has refused pre-arrest bail to the SHO of Mustafabad, Kasur, in the custodial death case of Rizwan, the brother of a lawyer — after the FIA found the police officials responsible for murder in custody under the Custodial Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act.

What was reported

  1. Justice Sardar Akbar Ali Dogar of the Lahore High Court dismissed the pre-arrest bail petition of SHO Hassan Imran of Mustafabad, Kasur, after hearing arguments on Friday (2 October 2026). The case concerns the death in custody of Rizwan, the brother of a lawyer, who had been arrested with four cases registered against him on the same day.
  2. The FIA investigated the matter under the Custodial Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act and found the police officials responsible for murder in custody.
  3. Five police officials were nominated: the SHO, sub-inspectors Tariq Mehmood and Muhammad Sadiq, head constable Ishtiaq and constable Yaqoob. Three are arrested; two are absconding.
  4. The case was originally registered in July 2024 and was transferred to the FIA Lahore Zone on the Lahore High Court's own orders; the inquiry was conducted by FIA Gujranwala Zone Assistant Director Naeem Akhtar.
  5. The court also dismissed the bail petition of another police official in the same case. After the order, lawyers attacked the SHO inside the High Court; court security intervened and police took him into custody.

The key legal point

  1. Officers accused of custodial death get no special relief: the Lahore High Court refused pre-arrest bail even though the accused is a serving SHO.
  2. The Custodial Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act gives the FIA the mandate to investigate custodial deaths and fix criminal responsibility on police officials.
  3. A court can deny bail in custodial death cases — the accusation of killing in custody outweighs the officer's rank.
  4. Why it matters: if a person faces abuse or death in custody, there is a clear legal route — an inquiry by the FIA under the Custodial Torture Act, and recourse to the court for denial of bail to the accused officials.
