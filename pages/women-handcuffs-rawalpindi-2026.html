Women Suspects Handcuffed for the First Time in Pakistan's History, Lawyers Protest in Rawalpindi

Women accused brought from Adiala Jail to Rawalpindi courts with both hands in handcuffs remained restrained for six to seven hours; the District Bar Association protested and announced High Court action.

WhatsApp inquiryTaqi Kazmi website

What was reported

  1. Women accused were transported from Adiala Jail to the Rawalpindi courts with both hands tightly secured in handcuffs, reportedly for the first time in Pakistan's judicial history.
  2. The women told the media they remained handcuffed for six to seven hours, from the jail to the court and back, and struggled to board the police van, use the washroom, or adjust their dupattas.
  3. They alleged that male police officials physically assisted them while boarding and leaving the van in ways that left them exposed, causing severe distress.
  4. The District Bar Association and senior lawyers protested, calling the practice unconstitutional, illegal and inhumane, and announced they would approach the High Court.
  5. Senior lawyer Shan Zaib Khan stated the order had been issued by the CPO Rawalpindi. The CPO had recently ordered proper handcuffs for all accused being produced in court, after under-trial prisoners escaped from custody.

The key legal point

These women are accused, not convicted. An accused person is presumed innocent until proven guilty, and the law requires that the basic rights and dignity of an accused person be protected during custody and court production.
