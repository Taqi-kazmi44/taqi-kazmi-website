How to File a Workplace Harassment Complaint in Pakistan: A Step-by-Step Guide

Workplace harassment is not just bad behaviour: it is a legal wrong with a fixed complaint procedure and fixed time limits. The Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010 requires every organization to run a time bound complaint system, and it protects women and men alike. A written complaint is all you need to start.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. File a written complaint with your organization's Inquiry Committee. Section 3 of the Act requires every organization to have a three member committee, with at least one woman. You may hand your complaint directly to any committee member, and that member is obligated to start the process. Keep a dated copy.
  2. You also have the option to skip the committee entirely and complain directly to the Ombudsperson. Section 8(1) gives every employee this choice.
  3. The Inquiry Committee must act within three days of receiving your complaint, and submit its findings within 30 days of the inquiry starting (Section 4). Both sides are heard, you may be accompanied by a colleague, and the law bars any adverse action or retaliation against you or your witnesses.
  4. If the accused is found guilty, the committee recommends minor or major penalties. Minor penalties include censure, withholding of promotion or increment, and recovery of compensation from the accused's pay. Major penalties include demotion, compulsory retirement, removal, dismissal, and fine, part of which can go to you as compensation.
  5. The Competent Authority must impose the recommended penalty within one week (Section 4(5)). Either side can appeal to the Ombudsperson within 30 days of the decision, and the Ombudsperson decides the appeal within 30 days (Section 6). A further representation lies to the Governor or President within 30 days (Section 9).
  6. Your employer is bound by law to display the Code of Conduct and run this system. Failure can bring a fine of Rs. 25,000 to 100,000 (Section 11).

The governing law

The procedure is governed by the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010 (Act IV of 2010). Section 3 establishes the Inquiry Committee; Section 4 lays down the inquiry procedure, the 30-day findings deadline, the minor and major penalties, and the one-week implementation rule; Section 6 provides the 30-day appeal to the Ombudsperson; Section 8(1) lets any employee complain directly to the Ombudsperson instead of the committee; Section 9 provides representation to the Governor or President; Section 11 makes the employer responsible for displaying the Code of Conduct and running the system, with fines of Rs. 25,000 to 100,000 for non-compliance; and the Schedule contains the Code of Conduct, which recognizes three manifestations of harassment: abuse of authority, creating a hostile environment, and retaliation.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: the law puts a time bound complaint system inside every workplace in Pakistan, and it protects women and men alike. Even a late report does not weaken your case, so a written complaint is all you need to start.
