FCC: Judicial Sympathy Cannot Override Remission Law

Can a life convict walk free after serving only nine years? The Federal Constitutional Court has drawn a hard line: excessive prison remissions are illegal and void, and under Rule 217(ii) of the Pakistan Prison Rules, 1978, a life convict must actually serve at least 15 years. Judicial sympathy cannot override statutory commands.

What was reported

  1. Suba Khan was convicted of murder under Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code by an additional sessions judge in Loralai on 26 May 2015 and sentenced to life imprisonment, a fine of Rs200,000 and six months' simple imprisonment in default of payment.
  2. According to the prison's nominal roll dated 17 August 2023, Suba Khan had actually served 9 years, 4 months and 19 days, while remissions of 15 years, 7 months and 18 days had been credited to his account, leaving about five months and 23 days remaining. The Balochistan High Court declared such excessive remissions illegal and void.
  3. A two-judge bench of the Federal Constitutional Court's Branch Registry Quetta, headed by Justice Rozi Khan Barrech with Justice Arshad Hussain Shah, has now upheld that verdict in a 16-page judgment.
  4. Rule 199 and Rules 212, 213, 215 and 216 of the Pakistan Prison Rules, 1978 enable remission, but Rule 217 expressly circumscribes it. Under Rule 217(ii), the cumulative effect of all remissions cannot reduce the actual period of imprisonment of a life convict to less than 15 years. This is mandatory, with no exception except lawful alteration by a competent authority.

The key legal point

  1. The judgment records the philosophy of reformation and rehabilitation but balances it against the deterrent and retributive force of sentences.
  2. The court stated plainly that judicial sympathy cannot override statutory commands, and a remission computed beyond the limits of the law confers no enforceable right.
  3. Remission is a concession within the law, not a bypass of it. For life imprisonment, at least 15 years of actual imprisonment is compulsory, and courts may not shorten that floor out of sympathy.
  4. Why it matters: anyone checking a convict's release timeline must count only actual custody against that 15-year floor. Remissions credited beyond the legal limit give the prisoner no enforceable right to release.
