Judgment Citation • 2026-10-29

2026 SCMR 1798

A delayed postmortem turned an entire murder case upside down.

If the police delay your FIR, can that delay be used against you? The Supreme Court of Pakistan has answered this clearly: no.

In Muhammad Bux alias Shahzaib v The State, 2026 SCMR 1798, the informant reported a murder within 30 minutes, and the report was entered in the Roznamcha the same night. The FIR itself, however, was registered the next afternoon. The Court held that the delay was caused by the police, not by the informant, and the victim cannot be made to suffer for the negligence of police officials.

Under Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, once information about a cognizable offence reaches the officer in charge of a police station, registration of the FIR is a mandatory duty, not a favour. The police cannot wait for funeral rites to end, or first test the credibility of the information, before registering the FIR.

The Court directed Inspectors General of Police across Pakistan to ensure prompt registration of FIRs, because delay can destroy forensic evidence and prejudice a case. Where deliberate delay by a police officer causes loss or disappearance of evidence, criminal liability under Section 201 of the Pakistan Penal Code, along with departmental proceedings, can follow, and the burden lies on the officer to explain the delay.

The Court also directed that the colonial era term "Faryaadi" be discontinued in police proceedings, because a citizen approaches the police as a matter of right, not as a supplicant seeking mercy. The correct term is "informant".

What this means for you: if you report a crime promptly, a delay by the police in writing the FIR should not weaken your case. Keep a record of when you reported it, and to whom.

Case: Muhammad Bux alias Shahzaib v The State, 2026 SCMR 1798. Supreme Court of Pakistan, Criminal Petition No. 1021 of 2021. Bench: Justice Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, Justice Salahuddin Panhwar (author) and Justice Ishtiaq Ibrahim. Heard 11 December 2025, announced 30 January 2026. Full judgment text: lawtoday.com.pk judgment PDF.
