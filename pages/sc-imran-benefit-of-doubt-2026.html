2026 SCMR 47: One Doubt Is Enough for Acquittal — Imran vs The State

A three-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Justice Athar Minallah, Justice Irfan Saadat Khan and Justice Malik Shahzad Ahmad Khan, held in Imran vs The State (Jail Petition No. 479 of 2019), reported as 2026 SCMR 47 and decided on 26 September 2025, that a single circumstance creating doubt in the prosecution case is sufficient to acquit the accused. The prosecution must prove its case beyond the shadow of doubt.

What was decided

  1. The accused, a 17-year-old boy, faced a murder charge. The Court re-examined the entire prosecution case and found that the case rested on shaky ground at several points: the motive was never proved, the witnesses were "chance witnesses" whose presence at the scene had no explanation, and the recovered pistol and the crime empties had been deposited together at the Forensic Science Laboratory.
  2. The Court held that even a single circumstance creating doubt is enough for acquittal. The benefit of doubt goes to the accused, and the prosecution's failure to remove that doubt ends the case in favour of the accused.
  3. Medical evidence was declared only supportive: it may confirm the injury, its nature and the kind of weapon used, but it cannot identify the assailant. A conviction cannot rest on medical evidence alone.
  4. Mere abscondence of an accused, by itself, is not sufficient to sustain a conviction in the absence of other reliable evidence. Running away does not prove guilt.
  5. When the recovered empties and the weapon are deposited together at the Forensic Science Laboratory, the recovery and the positive FSL report lose evidentiary value.
  6. Witnesses whose presence at the spot is unexplained (neither their land nor the place shown in the site plan) are chance witnesses, and their evidence is not worthy of reliance.

The key legal point

  1. The judgment restates the foundational criminal law rule: an accusation by the police or the media is not a conviction. Every charge must be proved in court with reliable evidence, beyond the shadow of doubt.
  2. For law students and practitioners, the case is essential reading on the standard of benefit of doubt, the supportive (not conclusive) role of medical evidence, the law on chance witnesses, and the law on abscondence.
  3. For citizens, the practical reminder is powerful: if you are accused, the prosecution carries the burden. For complainants, it is a reminder that investigation quality decides cases: unexplained witnesses, joint FSL deposits and unproved motive collapse even serious charges.
