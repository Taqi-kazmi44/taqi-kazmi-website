Is It Mandatory to Register a Rent Agreement in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions tenants and landlords ask: we have a rent agreement on stamp paper, is registration compulsory? The verified answer is yes, in two ways. One duty comes from the Registration Act, 1908, which applies everywhere in Pakistan. A second duty comes from the Punjab Rented Premises Act, 2009 for tenancies in Punjab.

The Registration Act, 1908 duty (applies everywhere)

Section 17(1)(d) of the Registration Act, 1908 makes registration compulsory for leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent. An unregistered lease cannot be received as evidence of the lease in court under Section 49 of the Act.

The Punjab Rented Premises Act, 2009 duty

  1. Written agreement required: Section 5(1) says a landlord shall not let out a premises to a tenant except by a tenancy agreement.
  2. Rent Registrar: Section 5(2) requires the landlord to present the tenancy agreement before the Rent Registrar, who enters the particulars in a register and returns the sealed original.
  3. Not a substitute: Section 5(4) makes clear that entry with the Rent Registrar does not absolve the landlord or tenant of their liability to register the tenancy agreement under the law relating to registration of documents, meaning the Registration Act, 1908 duty still stands.
  4. Price of non-compliance: under Section 9, if the tenancy does not conform to the Act, the Rent Tribunal will not entertain an application unless the landlord deposits a fine of 10 percent of the annual rent value, or 5 percent if the application is on behalf of the tenant.

The Supreme Court nuance: non-registration is curable

In Mrs. Tasneem Abbas vs Additional District Judge, Lahore (2026 SCMR 171), the Supreme Court of Pakistan held that non-registration of a tenancy with the rent registrar is a curable defect, not a fatal one. The parties must be given a chance to pay the fine and register; the case cannot be thrown out on this ground alone.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: always insist on a written rent agreement, and for any tenancy longer than a year, get it properly registered. It protects both landlord and tenant.
