Can the Police Check Your Mobile Phone Without a Warrant?

One of the most common questions ordinary citizens ask in Pakistan: can a police officer at a checkpost demand your phone and scroll through it? The courts have answered this clearly. No, not without the court's permission.

The legal rule: court permission is required

Your mobile phone's data is protected by the Constitution of Pakistan, including Article 14 (inviolability of the dignity of man) and Article 9 (security of person). The police have no legal power to scroll through your phone at a checkpost or during a routine stop and search.

In July 2023, a Lahore High Court bench comprising Justice Ali Baqar Najafi and Justice Muhammad Amjad Rafiq ruled that spying on or extracting data from the personal mobile phone of an individual or a suspect without a court's permission is unconstitutional and illegal. The court held that phone data can only be obtained with the permission of the concerned court, with strict regard to the privacy rights guaranteed under the Constitution.

Phone tapping and surveillance: also barred

The Islamabad High Court has taken the same position. In the audio-leaks case (2024), Justice Babar Sattar observed that apparently no individual, entity or agency was authorized by the federal government to intercept calls, messages or conduct surveillance of citizens without legal authorization, and that doing so without authorization is a punishable offence under the Telegraph Act, the Telecommunication Act, the Fair Trial Act and the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016. The court also ordered that police and agencies may not surveil any citizen except under warrants duly issued by a judge of the High Court.

What the law allows

Police access to electronic data is not banned in every situation. Where the police are investigating a crime, they can obtain phone data through the proper legal channel: by getting the permission or warrant of the concerned court. What the law does not allow is unilateral, warrantless scrolling through a citizen's phone at a checkpoint.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if anyone stops you at a checkpoint and demands to look through your phone, you can politely refuse. The law requires the court's permission before anyone can access your phone's data.
