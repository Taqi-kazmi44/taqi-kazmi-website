SC Grants Bail to Four Suspects in Human Placenta Trade Case

The Supreme Court has granted post-arrest bail to four accused in an alleged illegal trade in human placentas, observing that the placenta, being waste material, makes the case one of further inquiry. The FIA seized 1,241 kg of placentas and intercepted a 580 kg shipment bound for Vietnam.

On Monday, 16 November 2026, a two-member bench of the Supreme Court, headed by Justice Shahzad Ahmad Khan and comprising Justice Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, granted post-arrest bail to four accused in a case of alleged illegal trade in human placentas. Three of the accused are Chinese nationals and one is a Pakistani citizen.

The case was registered by the FIA Corporate Crime Circle in Islamabad on the complaint of Hina Kanwal, a medical officer at the Human Organ Transplant Authority. It is registered under Sections 11 and 12 of the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 2010, read with Sections 420, 34 and 109 of the Pakistan Penal Code.

According to the Deputy Attorney General, a joint FIA and HOTA team raided a house in Sector F-7/1, Islamabad, which had allegedly been converted into an unauthorised facility for collecting, processing, preserving, packaging and storing human placentas. A second raid in Sector E-11 recovered more placentas from refrigerators. In total, around 400 to 500 kilogrammes were seized, and samples were sent to PIMS for forensic examination. A 580 kg shipment, allegedly misdeclared as sheep placenta and booked for export to Hanoi, Vietnam, was intercepted at Islamabad airport. The total recovery was 1,241 kg.

The prosecution argued that the placenta falls within the definition of a human organ under Section 2(e) of the 2010 Act, that Section 11 prohibits commercial dealings in human organs and carries up to ten years in prison and a fine of Rs1 million, and that the foreign nationals could be a flight risk.

The Court observed that the placenta, being waste material, made the case one of further inquiry, and granted bail to each accused against surety bonds of Rs1 million, backed by two local sureties.

What this means for you: bail is not an acquittal. When a court says a case is one of further inquiry, it means the matter needs deeper examination before guilt can be decided. Commercial dealing in human organs is a crime under the 2010 Act, and anyone who comes across suspicious trade in medical waste should report it to the FIA or the police.
