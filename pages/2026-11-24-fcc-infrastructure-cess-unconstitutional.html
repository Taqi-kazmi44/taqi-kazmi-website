FCC Strikes Down Sindh and KP Infrastructure Cess as Unconstitutional

A tax on imports that businesses have fought for three decades is gone. The Federal Constitutional Court has struck down the provincial infrastructure development cess.

On 19 November 2026, a two-judge bench of the Federal Constitutional Court, Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and Justice Aamer Farooq, announced its decision in the infrastructure cess cases, and issued a 59-page detailed judgment authored by the Chief Justice on 20 November 2026.

The Court allowed 568 petitions filed by taxpayers and set aside the judgments of the Sindh High Court and the Peshawar High Court that had upheld the levy. The laws struck down are the Sindh Development and Maintenance of Infrastructure Cess Act, 2017, and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Infrastructure Development Cess Act, 2022, including the amendments made through the KP Finance Act, 2024.

The Court held that the cess, in its true nature, was a customs impost on the import, export and transit of goods, a subject that falls in the exclusive federal domain under Article 142(a) of the Constitution. Calling the levy a charge for infrastructure, or saying its proceeds would fund roads, does not create provincial power to impose it. Fiscal need cannot supply the missing power, the Court said, and a province cannot create a fiscal barrier inside the national trading system. The Court observed that the abolition of the Concurrent Legislative List through the 18th Amendment had expanded provincial competence but had not transferred subjects expressly retained in the federal domain.

The practical effect is wide. All charging provisions, and every dependent rule, notification, assessment and recovery measure, stand declared unconstitutional and without lawful authority. No demand, surcharge or penalty founded solely on these charges shall be enforced, and securities furnished only for these charges will be discharged on verification. Analysts estimate about Rs 42.9 billion of outstanding Sindh cess liability for 11 listed companies alone. The provinces remain free to levy constitutionally competent charges on goods manufactured and consumed within the province.

What this means for you: if you import, export or move goods across provinces, demands raised only under this cess cannot be enforced now. Keep your demand notices and recovery proceedings safe, and check whether any pending case or payment of yours is covered by this judgment.
