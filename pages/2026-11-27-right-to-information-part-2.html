Right to Information: How to File a Request in 15 Working Days

To whom do you write, in what form, and how long must you wait? The Sindh Transparency and Right to Information Act, 2016 answers all three questions, and the answer is simpler than most people expect.

Who receives your application? Section 7 requires every public body to designate and notify an officer, or an appropriate number of officers, not below the grade of BPS-16, to act as a Designated Official, with whom a request for information under the Act may be lodged. The Act required this designation within 45 days of its commencement. If no such officer is appointed, section 7(2) says the person in charge of the public body shall be deemed the Designated Official. Section 7(3) makes the Designated Official responsible for ensuring full compliance with the Act. In practice, find the designated officer of the department or office concerned. If the office has not notified one, address the application to the head of the office, who is deemed the Designated Official.

How to apply: section 8(1) says any applicant may make an application for obtaining information of the public body, and the application may be made in any form or manner to the Designated Official, and must be duly acknowledged. There is no fixed form. A simple written application, on plain paper or by email where the office accepts it, is valid. Always keep the acknowledgement, because it proves the date your application was received, and the deadlines run from that date.

What to write: section 8(2) requires the applicant to provide an adequate description of the information and the details necessary to provide the requisite information. Name the record clearly, give the period it relates to, and give any reference number, file number, or notification number you know. Vague requests are the most common reason applications are delayed. A request that says “all records of the office” is weak. A request that says “the certified copy of the work order for the repair of the road in our street, issued between January and June 2026” is strong.

The deadline: 15 working days. Section 8(3) says the Designated Official shall respond to the application as soon as possible, and in any case within fifteen working days. If the required information is not readily available, the Designated Official shall intimate the applicant and take another ten working days for responding. Count working days, not calendar days, and keep the written intimation if the officer claims the extra ten days.

Life or liberty: 2 working days. Section 8(4) says information needed to protect the life or liberty of any individual shall be provided within two working days. If the information concerns a threat to life or liberty, say so clearly in the application and state the reason.

If the officer refuses: section 8(5) says where the Designated Official decides not to provide the information, he shall intimate the applicant the reasons for such decision, along with a statement that the applicant may file an internal review. A refusal without reasons is not a proper refusal. Part 4 of this series explains the internal review and the complaint to the Sindh Information Commission.

The certified copy: section 8(6) says the information, or the copy of any public record supplied to the applicant, shall contain a certificate at the foot of it that the information is correct, or the copy is a true copy of such public record, and the certificate shall be dated, signed, and stamped by the Designated Official. Check that the certificate is present before you leave with the copy, because a certified copy is the version that counts as evidence.

The fee: section 8(7) says the public body or the Designated Official shall not charge any fee for making a request, other than the cost of reproducing or sending the information, in accordance with a centrally set schedule of costs stipulated by the Sindh Information Commission. Making the request itself is free. You pay only the reproduction or postage cost, and only at the rate the Commission has fixed.

Common mistakes: first, sending the application to the wrong office, when the record is held by a different public body. Second, writing a vague request, when section 8(2) requires an adequate description. Third, leaving without an acknowledgement, when the acknowledgement proves the date and starts the 15-day clock. Fourth, accepting a verbal refusal, when section 8(5) requires written reasons and a statement about internal review. Fifth, paying a fee for the request itself, when section 8(7) allows only the reproduction or sending cost.

What this means for you: write a short, specific application to the Designated Official of the office that holds the record, keep the acknowledgement, and expect a reply within 15 working days, or within 2 working days if life or liberty is at stake. If the officer refuses, the refusal must be in writing, with reasons. Part 3 explains the information every public body must publish on its own, without anyone asking.

Sources: Sindh Transparency and Right to Information Act, 2016 (Sindh Act No. XV of 2017), sections 7 and 8. Official text of the Act: https://www.sindhlaws.gov.pk/Setup/Publications_SindhCode/PUB-NEW-20-000002.pdf
