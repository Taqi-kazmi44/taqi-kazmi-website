How to Report Cybercrime to NCCIA Online in Pakistan

Online fraud, blackmail, harassment, or a hacked account: you do not need to suffer in silence. The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) is Pakistan's dedicated cybercrime investigation agency, and it receives complaints online, by phone, by email, and in person at Cybercrime Reporting Centres. This page states the exact reporting procedure under the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (Act No. XL of 2016).

The procedure, step by step

  1. Gather your evidence first. Save screenshots of chats, transaction records, emails, URLs, and the phone numbers or usernames involved. Keep original files unedited and back them up. Do not delete messages before preserving the evidence; investigators examine original files and their metadata.
  2. Go to the official NCCIA complaint portal at complaint.nccia.gov.pk and complete the online form: your full name, CNIC, contact details, a clear description of the incident, and your evidence. The portal issues a complaint reference number for tracking. There is no filing fee.
  3. For urgent guidance, call the 24/7 toll-free helpline at 1799. A call gives immediate advice, for example on securing a compromised account, but the formal complaint goes through the portal or in person.
  4. You can also email a written complaint with your CNIC copy to helpdesk@nccia.gov.pk, or visit your nearest NCCIA Cybercrime Reporting Centre in person (Islamabad, Lahore, Karachi, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, or Faisalabad) with your evidence and identity documents.
  5. Save the complaint reference number you receive. Use it to track your case status on the portal or in follow-ups.
  6. Overseas Pakistanis can nominate a close relative or legal representative in Pakistan, with their CNIC and contact details, to act as a focal person for the complaint.

The key legal points

  1. The substantive law is the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (Act No. XL of 2016). Cybercrime offences under PECA carry criminal penalties including imprisonment and fines, so a blackmail message or an online fraud is a legal offence, not just a nuisance.
  2. The NCCIA is the dedicated investigation agency for cybercrime in Pakistan. Use only the official portal, the 1799 helpline, or the published email; do not trust complaint numbers found through casual searches.
  3. Early reporting matters. Authorities state that faster reporting allows quicker tracing of digital footprints, and preserved evidence increases the chance of identifying the suspect.
  4. What this means for you: cybercrime is a legal offence with a dedicated reporting system, so report it early instead of staying silent.
