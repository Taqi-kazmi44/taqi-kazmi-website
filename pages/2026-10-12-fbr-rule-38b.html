FBR Draft Rule 38B: IRIS to Warn Taxpayers Before Any Penalty

FBR has proposed draft Rule 38B of the Income Tax Rules, 2002 (S.R.O. 1690(I)/2026), under which its automated system would send an advance intimation on IRIS when it detects errors in an income tax return, giving the taxpayer at least seven days to respond before any legal or penal action. The rule is still a draft, not law yet.

The Federal Board of Revenue has proposed a draft Rule 38B for the Income Tax Rules, 2002 (S.R.O. 1690(I)/2026, dated 6 October 2026). Under the draft, FBR's automated system would send an online advice or advance intimation through IRIS whenever it detects a factual or legal mistake in a taxpayer's return, before any legal or penal action begins.

What the draft proposes:
- The system, or the Officer of Inland Revenue having jurisdiction, sends the intimation on IRIS pointing out the error or discrepancy.
- The taxpayer gets not less than seven days to respond. If the deadline is missed, a reminder follows with another seven days.
- The response is recorded and placed before the officer, who then decides whether any action under the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 is needed.
- A system flag is neither an audit nor a penalty. It is a chance to clarify or correct first.

One caution: this is a DRAFT, not law yet. FBR issued it under section 237 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, inviting objections and suggestions within three days of Gazette publication. Until a final rule is notified, nothing in it binds taxpayers.

What this means for you: if there is a mistake in your return, you get an IRIS intimation first, not a fine. Respond in time and the matter can end right there.
