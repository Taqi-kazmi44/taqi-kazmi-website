How to Get a Succession Certificate from NADRA in Pakistan

When a family member passes away, their bank accounts and property do not transfer to the legal heirs automatically. Since 2021, NADRA's Succession Facilitation Units issue Succession Certificates (for movable property) and Letters of Administration (for immovable property), so heirs with an undisputed claim no longer need to spend years in a civil court. This page states the exact procedure under the Punjab Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act 2021 and the Sindh Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act 2021.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Know which document you need. A Succession Certificate is for movable assets: bank accounts, shares, vehicles. A Letter of Administration is for immovable property: house, land. (Section 3 of the Punjab Act; Tribune, 21 Jan 2021.)
  2. Choose one applicant. The legal heirs may authorize one of their own, in the prescribed form, to file the application on behalf of all of them. (Section 6(1), proviso.)
  3. Apply at any NADRA centre. NADRA has 186 Succession Facilitation Units across Pakistan, and since August 2025 you can apply at any centre regardless of where the property is located (reported by ARY News, August 2025).
  4. File these documents: the death certificate of the deceased; the list of all legal heirs with copies of their CNICs; the written authorization in the prescribed form; and details of the movable and immovable property. (Section 6(3).)
  5. NADRA publishes a public notice on its web portal and in one English and one Urdu daily newspaper of wide circulation. Anyone with a claim or objection has 14 days to come forward. (Section 6(4)-(5).)
  6. If no objection arrives, the applicant and all legal heirs complete biometric verification at any notified NADRA office in Pakistan or abroad; remote verification through the Pak ID app is also available. (Section 6(5).)
  7. NADRA issues the certificate in the names of all legal heirs, with each person's share as fixed by their personal law. It has the same legal effect as a certificate issued under the Succession Act, 1925. (Section 6(6)-(7).)
  8. If the heirs dispute the shares or heirship, there is a factual controversy: NADRA declines the application and the matter goes to the civil court under the Succession Act, 1925 (XXXIX of 1925) or other applicable law. (Section 5(b).)

The key legal points

  1. These NADRA-issued documents are not informal letters: they carry the same effect as if issued under the Succession Act, 1925, and banks, land registries and companies must act on them.
  2. A processing fee is charged under Section 9 as prescribed. In Sindh the fee has been challenged before the Sindh High Court, so confirm the current amount with NADRA before applying.
  3. What this means for you: instead of waiting years for a civil court decree, an undisputed succession can now be documented at a NADRA centre, with every heir's share clearly recorded.
