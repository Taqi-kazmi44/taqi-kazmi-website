Can the Police Keep You in Custody Beyond 24 Hours?

One of the most common questions ordinary citizens ask: how long can the police keep an arrested person in the lockup? The verified answer is 24 hours, not a minute more, unless a magistrate orders otherwise.

The 24-hour rule: Sections 60 and 61 CrPC

Section 60 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 says that a police officer making an arrest without a warrant must, without unnecessary delay, take the arrested person before a magistrate having jurisdiction or before the officer in charge of a police station. Then Section 61, titled "Person arrested not be detained more than twenty-four hours," says no police officer shall detain in custody a person arrested without warrant for longer than is reasonable in all the circumstances of the case, and that such period shall not, in the absence of a special order of a magistrate under Section 167, exceed twenty-four hours, exclusive of the time necessary for the journey from the place of arrest to the magistrate's court.

In plain words: the police must either release the person or produce them before a magistrate within 24 hours of arrest, not counting travel time to the court. Any further detention requires a special order of the magistrate under Section 167, which is the provision for police remand.

The constitutional safeguard: Article 10(2)

Article 10(2) of the Constitution of Pakistan says every person who is arrested and detained in custody shall be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours of such arrest, excluding the time necessary for the journey, and no such person shall be detained in custody beyond that period without the authority of a magistrate. So the 24-hour limit is not only in the CrPC, it is a constitutional guarantee.

If the police keep someone longer

  1. Detention beyond 24 hours without a magistrate's order under Section 167 is unlawful. Raise it before the magistrate when the person is produced.
  2. A petition for habeas corpus can be moved before the High Court against illegal detention.
  3. Women, children and other vulnerable persons have additional protections during arrest and detention, and any complaint about mistreatment in custody can be reported to the SHO, the Superintendent of Police or the 15 helpline.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if someone you know has been in police custody for more than a day without being produced before a magistrate, that detention is unlawful, and the law gives you the remedy of approaching the magistrate or the High Court.
