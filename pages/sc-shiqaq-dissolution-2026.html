SC: Marriage Can Be Dissolved on Shiqaq Even When Cruelty Is Not Proven

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has ruled that a marriage can be dissolved on grounds of shiqaq (marital discord) under the residuary provision of Section 2(ix) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, even where cruelty was not established. In a 15-page judgment authored by Justice Shahid Waheed, a five-member bench also drew a sharp legal line between khula and shiqaq: family courts cannot convert a wife's cruelty claim into a khula decree without her clear and voluntary consent.

What the judgment says

  1. The cases: two connected petitions by Zainab Bibi and Imran Naz Begum against separate Peshawar High Court orders. Both wives had sought dissolution on the ground of cruelty and claimed maintenance, dower and dowry articles. The family courts found cruelty unproven in both cases, but dissolved the marriages through khula and directed the women to return their dower. The appellate court and the Peshawar High Court largely upheld these decrees.
  2. The bench: a five-member bench headed by Justice Shahid Waheed, comprising Justices Naeem Akhter Afghan, Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, Ishtiaq Ibrahim and Miangul Hassan Aurangzeb. The 15-page judgment was authored by Justice Waheed.
  3. Khula needs the wife's own choice: the Court held that khula proceeds from a wife's voluntary election to obtain release from the marriage and ordinarily carries financial consequences. Converting the petitioners' cruelty claims into khula decrees without their clear and voluntary consent was an error, and the khula-based decrees could not be sustained.
  4. Shiqaq sustains the dissolution on a different basis: the record established a condition of shiqaq of the kind contemplated by verse 35 of Surah An-Nisa, recognised under Muslim law. Shiqaq arises from an objectively established condition of serious matrimonial breach and does not depend on the wife surrendering dower or any benefit. The dissolutions therefore stand under Section 2(ix) of the Act of 1939, not on the basis of khula, and the directions requiring the women to return or waive their dower merely as a consequence of khula were set aside; their dower entitlement will be decided under the Act of 1939.
  5. The test for shiqaq is strict: mere incompatibility, occasional disagreement, anger, short separation or matrimonial litigation alone are not enough. The Court must be satisfied that the breach is genuine, serious and continuing, that the marriage has ceased to serve its essential purposes, and that reconciliation has failed despite meaningful judicial effort.

The key legal point

  1. For an ordinary person, the meaning is this: if the rift between husband and wife is deep and continuing, and the marriage can no longer serve its purpose, the court can dissolve the marriage on shiqaq even without proof of cruelty. But khula happens only with the woman's own consent; the court cannot turn her case into khula against her will, and she cannot be forced to give up her dower through such a conversion.
  2. The judgment also protects the other side: a husband should not be judicially characterised as cruel or guilty of matrimonial misconduct without the evidentiary foundation the law requires. The law must determine rights, not aggravate human conflict.
