Sindh Irrigation and Drainage Authority v Province of Sindh, 2026 SCMR 190: Every Day of Delay Must Be Explained

The Supreme Court of Pakistan held that every day of delay in filing an appeal must be explained with specific reasons, and government departments get no preferential treatment under the Limitation Act, 1908.

The Sindh Irrigation and Drainage Authority challenged a decree of the High Court, but its appeal reached the Supreme Court after the time allowed by law had expired. The question before the Court was which limitation period applied, and whether the delay could be condoned.

The Court held that a decree issued by the High Court in its original jurisdiction attracted the 20-day limitation period under Article 151 of the Limitation Act, 1908, and the appeal was late even under the 90-day period of Article 156. Alleging that the award was void did not justify condonation: every day of delay must be explained, and government bodies receive no preferential treatment in limitation.

The Court also reaffirmed that limitation is not a mere technicality. Once the prescribed period expires, a vested right is created in favour of the other side, and delay is condoned only where the applicant satisfies the Court that there was sufficient cause for it.

What this means for you: if you have a court order to challenge, count the days from the order and file within time. A strong case on merits cannot rescue a late appeal, and a general excuse will not explain away the delay.

Reference: Sindh Irrigation and Drainage Authority v Province of Sindh and another, 2026 SCMR 190, Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 24 July 2025, bench: Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Syed Hasan Azhar Rizvi and Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, JJ, Civil Appeal No. 80-K of 2022.
