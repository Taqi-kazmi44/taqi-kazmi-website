If a court decides your case without even hearing your biggest legal question, is that judgment worth anything?
The Supreme Court says: no, it is worth nothing at all.

In Syed Saad Ali vs Federation of Pakistan, 2026 SCMR 22, contract employees of the Civil Aviation Authority challenged the vires of Regulation 21(1). The High Court decided the case without giving any finding on that central point. The Supreme Court held that such a judgment is "sub-silentio", a decision given without addressing the point at issue, and it carries no binding value. The judgment was set aside and the case sent back for rehearing.

For law students, this case cements three things: the sub-silentio doctrine, the test for vires (legislative competence and fundamental rights), and the presumption in favour of constitutionality. For every citizen, the lesson is practical: if your case is before a court and the judge has not addressed your main point, this judgment can help your lawyer argue the point again.

Case: Syed Saad Ali vs Federation of Pakistan, 2026 SCMR 22. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 12 June 2025. Bench: Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Syed Hasan Azhar Rizvi and Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, JJ.
