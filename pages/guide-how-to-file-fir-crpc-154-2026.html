How to File an FIR in Pakistan: Your Rights Under Section 154 CrPC

Many people are turned away from police stations because they do not know the exact legal procedure. Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 (Pakistan) lays down, step by step, what the officer in charge of a police station must do when information of a cognizable offence is given to him. This page states the procedure exactly as the statute and the Supreme Court describe it.

The procedure, step by step

  1. An FIR is only for COGNIZABLE offences (theft, robbery, assault, kidnapping and similar serious offences). Section 154 applies exclusively to information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence. For a non-cognizable offence, Section 155 CrPC applies: the officer enters the substance of the information in the station diary (Roznamcha) and refers the informant to the Magistrate, and no police investigation takes place without a Magistrate's order.
  2. Go to the police station of the area where the offence happened and give the information to the officer in charge (SHO), orally or in writing. You do not need to be the victim. Anyone with knowledge of the offence, a witness, a relative or any informed citizen, can lodge it.
  3. If the information is given orally, the officer must reduce it to writing himself or under his direction, and read it back to you. Check every detail carefully before signing. If you cannot read or write, a thumb impression is accepted.
  4. Every such information, whether given in writing or reduced to writing, must be signed by the person giving it, and its substance must be entered in the FIR book kept by the officer in the form prescribed by the Provincial Government. Ask for your copy of the FIR. It is handed over free of cost.
  5. Special protection for women victims: where the information is given by a woman against whom an offence under PPC Sections 336-B, 354, 354-A, 376 or 509 is alleged to have been committed or attempted, it must be recorded in the presence of a female police officer, a female family member, or any other person with her consent. If she is distressed, it must be recorded at her residence or at a convenient place of her choice. (Added to Section 154 by the Criminal Law (Amendment) (Offences Relating to Rape) Act, 2016.)
  6. If the police refuse to register the FIR, file an application under Sections 22-A and 22-B of the Code before the Ex-officio Justice of Peace, the Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge of your district (Section 25 CrPC). Under Section 22-A(6)(i), he may issue appropriate directions to the police on a complaint regarding non-registration of a criminal case. The Supreme Court confirmed in Younas Abbas v Additional Sessions Judge Chakwal (PLD 2016 SC 581) that this power is quasi-judicial in nature and is the proper remedy for a person whose FIR is refused.

The key legal point

  1. The language of Section 154 is mandatory: the officer in charge SHALL reduce the information to writing, read it over to the informant, have it signed, and enter its substance in the book. He cannot refuse registration on the ground that the offence is not made out.
  2. What this means for you: your FIR is your legal right, not a favour. Knowing the procedure means no officer can turn you away without a written reason.
