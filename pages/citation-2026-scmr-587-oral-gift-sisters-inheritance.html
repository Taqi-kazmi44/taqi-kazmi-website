2026 SCMR 587: Oral Gift Must Be Strictly Proved, Sisters' Inheritance Protected

Abdul Majeed and others v. Mst. Khalida Bibi (deceased) through LRs and others. Decided 24 June 2025 by Shahid Bilal Hassan and Miangul Hassan Aurangzeb, JJ. C.P.L.A. No. 990 of 2022.

The facts

After a man's death, his sons claimed that their father had orally gifted the disputed property to them during his lifetime, and a mutation of the gift was later entered in the revenue record. The sisters filed a suit, saying the transaction was fraudulent and designed to deprive them of their inheritance. The trial court decreed the sisters' suit; the appeal and the revision were dismissed. The brothers then sought leave to appeal in the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court's holding

The Supreme Court found no illegality in the judgments of the trial court, the appellate court and the Lahore High Court warranting interference, and refused leave to appeal. The Court restated the settled law on oral gifts:

  1. A valid gift has three essential ingredients: offer, acceptance and delivery of possession. Each must be pleaded and proved by cogent, strong, unimpeachable and confidence-inspiring evidence.
  2. No description of the offer, its acceptance, the venue, or the witnesses in whose presence it took place had been given in the written statement or in evidence. A party cannot lead evidence beyond its pleadings.
  3. An oral gift and the mutation entered on its basis are two separate transactions. The gift must be established by proving the three ingredients; the mutation must be independently proved under the procedure of the Land Revenue Act, 1967 and the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984.
  4. Where a claimed gift excludes daughters from their inheritance, the alleged donees carry a heavy burden of proof, and the claim faces the utmost scrutiny. The donees could not produce the stamp vendor, scribe, marginal witnesses, identifying witness, sub-registrar, revenue officials or the patwari, so the Court drew an adverse presumption under Article 129(g) of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984 for withholding the best available evidence.
  5. It is rare for a gift to be made without some reason like affection or reward for sincere service, and no such reason prompting the donor to exclude his daughters had been pleaded or proved.

What this means for you

  1. Your daughter or sister cannot be cut out of family property on the strength of a mere claim of an oral gift and a mutation entry. The burden is on the one claiming the gift.
