How to Register a Trust in Pakistan, Step by Step

Want to set up a trust for your family, a charity or a mosque? Since 2020, every province in Pakistan has its own Trusts Act, and registration is now compulsory. The old Trusts Act, 1882 was replaced by provincial laws, including the Punjab Trusts Act, 2020 and the Sindh Trusts Act, 2020. Here is the exact process, step by step.

Step by step

  1. Decide the purpose, name, beneficiaries and trust property. A trust can be created for any lawful purpose.
  2. Choose your trustees. They hold and manage the property for the beneficiaries, so name them and their powers in the deed.
  3. Draft the trust deed. It must state with reasonable certainty the intention to create the trust, its purpose, the beneficiaries and the trust property.
  4. Sign the deed. The author of the trust and the trustees sign the written instrument.
  5. Register the trust deed with the Sub-Registrar where the immovable property is situated. A trust of immovable property is not valid unless the deed is registered.
  6. Register the trust with the provincial regulator. In Punjab, register with the Director. In Sindh, register with the Assistant Director. In Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, the registration must be renewed every year.
  7. Transfer the trust property into the trust's name, so the trust owns it.
  8. Get the trust's NTN from FBR. If the trust is charitable, apply for approval as a non-profit organisation under section 2(36) of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. Approved trusts can claim a 100 percent tax credit under section 100C.

Why registration matters

A registered trust gives your charity or family arrangement a separate legal identity, protects the property for the beneficiaries you chose, and lets the trust open a bank account and receive donations openly. An unregistered trust of immovable property is simply not valid, so registration is not a formality, it is what makes the trust exist in the eyes of the law.

Reference

Punjab Trusts Act, 2020, sections 4, 5, 6, 14 and 15; Sindh Trusts Act, 2020, sections 3, 4 and 12; Registration Act, 1908, section 17(1)(b); Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, sections 2(36) and 100C.
