Government Moves to Overhaul Weak Audit System: SECP Amendment Bill 2026 Approved by Cabinet

Your company's audit may soon face a much tougher check. The federal cabinet has approved a Bill to overhaul Pakistan's entire audit system, acting on the Prime Minister's FBR Task Force, which described the existing framework as weak.

Cabinet approves the SECP Amendment Bill 2026

The federal cabinet has approved the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) (Amendment) Bill, 2026, on the recommendations of the Prime Minister's FBR Task Force. The task force said weak audits discourage local and foreign investment and limit access to capital markets. The government plans to introduce the legislation in parliament, along with amendments to the Audit Oversight Board (AOB) law to improve tax collection. A similar Bill was passed by the National Assembly in 2021 but was withdrawn from the Senate in 2022 for resubmission; the cabinet has now approved the revised Bill after review by three ministerial-level committees and consultations with stakeholders.

A stronger Audit Oversight Board

The Bill strengthens the Audit Oversight Board, the body that oversees auditors of public interest companies. The FBR chairman will replace the president of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Pakistan on the committee that nominates AOB board members, and no board member will be allowed to have any association with an audit firm. The AOB will carry out regular, risk-based inspections of auditors of public interest companies, and will be able to investigate complaints and references from the SECP, the State Bank of Pakistan and the FBR directly. It will also be able to file complaints about criminal offences involving falsification of accounts.

Why reform became necessary

Pakistan's existing AOB law did not comply with international standards. The International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) declared Pakistan non-compliant on independent audit oversight, and the European Union withdrew Pakistan's equivalence status for independent audit oversight. The official briefing cited Unity Foods, SE Foods and Hascol as examples of companies affected by weak audits and prospectus disclosures. The proposed system is expected to end the dominance of private auditors and strengthen Pakistan's international recognition.

What this means for you

If you run a company, are a director, or invest in listed companies, stronger audit oversight means audited accounts you can rely on more, and auditors who can be inspected and held to account. The Bill still has to be passed by parliament before it becomes law, so this is a proposal at this stage, not a change in force yet.
