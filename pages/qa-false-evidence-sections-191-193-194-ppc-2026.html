Can You Be Punished for Giving False Evidence in Court in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask about court proceedings: if a witness lies on oath, is that itself a crime? The answer is yes. Giving false evidence in court is a serious criminal offence under the Pakistan Penal Code, 1860. This page states the rule exactly as the statute does.

What Section 191 PPC says

Section 191 defines giving false evidence. Whoever, being legally bound by an oath or by an express provision of law to state the truth, makes any statement which is false, and which he either knows or believes to be false or does not believe to be true, is said to give false evidence. Section 192 covers fabricating false evidence: causing a false circumstance to exist, or making a false entry or a document containing a false statement, intending that it may appear in evidence in a judicial proceeding.

Punishment under Section 193

  1. Up to seven years imprisonment and a fine, if the false evidence is given or fabricated in any stage of a judicial proceeding.
  2. Up to three years imprisonment and a fine, if the false evidence is given or fabricated in any other case.

Section 194: false evidence in a capital case

  1. Whoever gives or fabricates false evidence intending to cause, or knowing it to be likely that he will cause, any person to be convicted of an offence which is capital by any law for the time being in force, shall be punished with imprisonment for life, or with rigorous imprisonment for a term which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to fine.
  2. If an innocent person is convicted and executed in consequence of such false evidence, the person who gives such false evidence shall be punished either with death or the punishment described above.

Section 195 and Section 196

  1. Under Section 195, giving or fabricating false evidence with intent to procure the conviction of an offence punishable with imprisonment for life or for a term of seven years or upwards is punished as a person convicted of that offence would be liable to be punished.
  2. Under Section 196, using evidence known to be false carries the same punishment as giving or fabricating false evidence.

The key legal point

  1. A statement made on oath in court is not a casual claim. Once a person is legally bound to state the truth, a deliberate lie is an offence in itself, even if no one is ultimately misled by it.
  2. What this means for you: never give a false statement in court or in an affidavit, even to help someone you know. A lie told under oath can send you to prison.
