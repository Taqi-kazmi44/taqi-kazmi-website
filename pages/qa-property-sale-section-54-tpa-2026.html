Can a House Be Bought on Stamp Paper Alone?

Many buyers in Pakistan pay the full price on the strength of a stamp paper agreement and believe the property is now theirs. The law says otherwise: an agreement to sell is a promise of a future sale, not the sale itself. Ownership passes only when the sale is completed in the manner the law prescribes.

The legal rule: Section 54, Transfer of Property Act, 1882

Section 54 defines a sale as a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid or promised or part-paid and part-promised. It then states how a sale is made: such transfer, in the case of tangible immovable property of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, or in the case of a reversion or other intangible thing, can be made only by a registered instrument. In practice, for a house or plot, that instrument is the registered sale deed executed before the sub-registrar.

An agreement to sell is not a sale

Section 54 also states that a contract for the sale of immovable property is a contract that a sale of such property shall take place on terms settled between the parties, and that it does not, of itself, create any interest in or charge on such property. A stamp paper bayana or agreement to sell therefore records the parties' commitment; it does not make the buyer the owner. It may support a claim for performance of the contract, but it is not a substitute for the registered transfer of title.

What this means for you

  1. Never treat a stamp paper agreement as proof of ownership. Always complete the registered sale deed and verify the seller's title before paying the full price.
