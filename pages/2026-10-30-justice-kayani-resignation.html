Justice Mohsin Akhtar Kayani Resigns from Lahore High Court, Citing Judicial Independence Concerns

Justice Mohsin Akhtar Kayani of the Lahore High Court resigned on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, citing personal reasons, including health concerns following a recent surgery, and "reasons of conscience". In his resignation letter to the President of Pakistan, a copy of which is available with Dawn, he said his years of judicial service were among the most meaningful of his life, and that he had disposed of more than 23,000 cases, endeavouring to uphold the Constitution, safeguard fundamental rights, and administer justice without fear or favour.

In his own words, "the changes introduced through the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments have compromised the independence and institutional integrity of the high courts". He said he had witnessed what he regarded as the executive's growing control over judicial institutions, extending to the appointment and transfer of judges, and that such interference had, in his assessment, seriously eroded public confidence in judicial independence and the fairness of the judicial process.

Justice Kayani also pointed to the ordinary litigant, who he said is the principal stakeholder in the justice system, yet whose suffering continues to deepen. He had previously served as the senior puisne judge of the Islamabad High Court and was expecting elevation as its chief justice, but after his transfer to the Lahore High Court he lost seniority and was placed 10th on that court's seniority list, according to Dawn. His resignation comes years before his scheduled retirement date of February 10, 2032. He said he intends to return to legal practice to continue serving the people, protecting their rights, and promoting legal awareness.

What this means for you: judicial independence is not an abstract debate for judges alone. When appointments and transfers are seen as influenced from outside the judiciary, it is the ordinary person waiting for a decision who loses confidence that the case will be decided on its merits alone. The judge's views are his own stated reasons in his resignation letter, not a court finding.
