2026 SCMR 87

Twenty packets were tested together as one batch.

Twenty packets were tested together as one batch.
The Supreme Court said: the method itself was wrong.

In Irshad Khan vs The State, 2026 SCMR 87, a narcotics case, the FSL conducted a collective test of 20 packets of opium and 3 packets of chars, and never prepared a separate report for each packet. The seal affixed had no connection with any officer or witness. The Court applied the maxim "communi observantia non est recedendum", meaning where the law prescribes a method, that method must be followed. The collective report destroyed the credibility of the entire forensic evidence. The accused was acquitted.

For law students, this case teaches the FSL protocol in narcotics cases, the difference between collective and separate analysis, the importance of the seal and monogram, and the application of the communi observantia maxim. For citizens, the lesson is sobering: the price of a forensic error is paid by an innocent person with his freedom, which is why following procedure is the guarantee of justice.

Case: Irshad Khan vs The State, 2026 SCMR 87. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 12 August 2025. Bench: Jamal Khan Mandokhail, Musarrat Hilali and Shakeel Ahmad, JJ.
