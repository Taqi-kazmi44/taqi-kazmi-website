Senate Panel Asks Why Election Appeals Should Move from Supreme Court to Constitutional Court

Your election appeal may soon be heard by a different court altogether. The Senate is now asking why election appeals should move from the Supreme Court to the new Federal Constitutional Court.

According to Dawn, the Senate Standing Committee on Law and Justice has sought an explanation from the Ministry of Law and Justice and the Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) over the proposed transfer of appeals under the Elections Act, 2017, from the Supreme Court to the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC).

The committee, chaired by Senator Farooq Hamid Naek, was examining the Elections (Amendment) Bill, 2026, a private member's bill moved by Senator Khalil Tahir on his own behalf and on behalf of Senator Mohammad Abdul Qadir during the Senate sitting on 26 October 2026. The proposed legislation seeks to bring the Elections Act, 2017 in line with the constitutional framework introduced through the Constitution (27th Amendment) Act, 2025, under which the Federal Constitutional Court was established.

During the deliberations, Senator Naek referred to Sections 9 and 155 of the Elections Act and observed that appeals arising under these provisions are statutory in nature, distinct from appeals stemming from proceedings under Article 199 of the Constitution. He questioned what constitutional provision or legal justification warranted transferring such appeals to the Federal Constitutional Court, particularly when they originated under an ordinary statute rather than constitutional proceedings.

In view of the support extended by the Ministry of Law and Justice and the ECP to the proposed legislation, the committee chairman asked both institutions to explain the reasoning behind changing the existing appellate forum. He also sought clarification on the implications of the proposed amendment for the existing mechanism of hearing election-related appeals. Meanwhile, the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs informed the committee that the National Assembly has already passed a separate private member's bill dealing with the same subject. The committee directed that a copy of the bill passed by the National Assembly, along with the report of its relevant standing committee, be presented before it at the next meeting for consideration.

What this means for you: if you challenge an election result, the court that hears your appeal could change, and that choice affects how quickly and by whom your case is decided. The question before the Senate committee is whether the Constitution actually permits that shift.
