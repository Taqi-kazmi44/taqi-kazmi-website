2026 SCMR 1: Wife's Right to Maintenance Begins with the Nikah

Can a husband refuse maintenance because rukhsati has not taken place yet? The Supreme Court of Pakistan says no: a wife's right to maintenance begins with the nikah itself.

In Ambreen Akram v. Asad Ullah Khan, 2026 SCMR 1, the Supreme Court of Pakistan delivered a clear, historic ruling on maintenance (nan-o-nafqa). The Court was asked whether a wife can be denied maintenance simply because rukhsati has not taken place.

The Court held that a wife's right to maintenance flows unconditionally from a valid marriage. It is not contingent on rukhsati, consummation, or obedience. Rukhsati has no independent legal status under the law, and conditioning maintenance on it violates the constitutional guarantees of dignity and equality (Articles 14, 25, and 35 of the Constitution).

The Court also rejected the old "maintenance for obedience" concept of ihtibas, holding it to be against the Quranic vision of nikah built on affection and compassion.

On the burden of proof, the Court held that if a husband refuses to pay maintenance, he must prove with clear and strong evidence that the wife has wholly and without cause brought the relationship to an end. Silence or a bare denial is not enough.

What this means for you: if you are a wife whose husband refuses support before rukhsati, the law stands with you, and the family court can grant you maintenance. The nikah certificate alone is the foundation of your claim.

Full reference: Ambreen Akram v. Asad Ullah Khan, 2026 SCMR 1. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 11 September 2025. Bench: Justice Syed Mansoor Ali Shah and Justice Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi.
