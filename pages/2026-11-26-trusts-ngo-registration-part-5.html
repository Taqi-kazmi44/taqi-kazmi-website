Trusts and NGO Registration (5/6): Registering a Welfare Agency under the 1961 Ordinance

A group of neighbours collects donations for a free clinic, a shelter for the elderly, or a school for street children. Before a single rupee is collected, the law has a rule that many welfare workers never hear: no welfare agency may be established or continued without registration. The law that governs it is the Voluntary Social Welfare Agencies (Registration and Control) Ordinance, 1961. This part explains what counts as a welfare agency, how registration works, and what happens if the rules are ignored.

What Counts As a Welfare Agency

Section 2(f) of the Ordinance defines a voluntary social welfare agency as an organisation, association or undertaking established by persons of their own free will for the purpose of rendering welfare services in any one or more of the fields mentioned in the Schedule, and depending for its resources on public subscriptions, donations or Government aid. The Schedule lists fifteen fields: child welfare, youth welfare, women's welfare, welfare of the physically and mentally handicapped, family planning, recreational programmes intended to keep people away from anti-social activities, social education, welfare and rehabilitation of released prisoners, welfare of juvenile delinquents, welfare of the socially handicapped, welfare of beggars and destitutes, welfare and rehabilitation of patients, welfare of the aged and infirm, training in social work, and coordination of social welfare agencies. Two tests must both be met: the work must fall in a scheduled field, and the money must come from the public, donations or Government aid. An agency includes any branch of the agency, so a branch in another city is covered by the same law.

Registration is Compulsory

Section 3 is one sentence long and leaves no room for doubt: no agency shall be established or continued except in accordance with the provisions of this Ordinance. This is the strictest of the four forms in this series. A trust that is not registered is non-functional under section 12 of the Sindh Trusts Act, 2020, and a society exists only after the Registrar certifies it, but the 1961 Ordinance goes further and prohibits even continuing an unregistered agency. Collecting donations for welfare work without registration is not a paperwork delay. It is a contravention of the Ordinance, and section 14 punishes it.

How to Apply

Section 4 sets out the application. Any person intending to establish an agency, or to continue an agency already in existence, must apply to the Registration Authority in the prescribed form, on payment of the prescribed fee, accompanied by a copy of the constitution of the agency and such other documents as may be prescribed. Section 2(e) defines the Registration Authority as an officer authorised by the Provincial Government, by notification in the official Gazette, to exercise the powers of the Registration Authority. In Sindh, welfare agencies register through the Social Welfare Department, Government of Sindh. The Registration Authority may make such inquiries as it considers necessary, and then either grant the application or reject it for reasons to be recorded in writing. If it grants the application, it issues a certificate of registration in the prescribed form, and it maintains a register of all certificates issued. Confirm the current prescribed form and fee at the time of filing, because the Ordinance leaves both to the rules and the Provincial Government.

New Agencies and Existing Agencies

Section 5 draws a sharp line. An agency not in existence when the Ordinance came into force can be established only after a certificate of registration has been issued. An agency already in existence cannot be continued for more than six months from the date the Ordinance came into force, unless an application for registration was made within thirty days of that date. If the application is rejected, the agency may still be continued for thirty days from the date of rejection, or, if an appeal is filed, until the appeal is dismissed. The message is consistent throughout the Ordinance: registration comes first, work comes after.

If the Application is Rejected

Section 6 gives the applicant an appeal. If the Registration Authority rejects an application for registration, the applicant may, within thirty days from the date of the order, prefer an appeal to the Provincial Government, and the order passed by the Provincial Government is final and must be given effect to by the Registration Authority. The thirty-day limit is strict, so a rejected applicant should act at once, not after months of informal follow-up.

The Yearly Duties of a Registered Agency

Section 7 imposes four continuing conditions on every registered agency. First, it must maintain audited accounts in the manner laid down by the Registration Authority. Second, it must, at such time and in such manner as may be prescribed, submit its Annual Report and audited accounts to the Registration Authority and publish the same for general information. Third, it must pay all moneys received by it into a separate account kept in its name at such bank or banks as may be approved by the Registration Authority. Fourth, it must furnish to the Registration Authority such particulars about accounts and other records as the Authority may from time to time require. The Registration Authority, or any officer duly authorised by it, may at all reasonable times inspect the books of account and other records of the agency, the securities, cash and other properties held by the agency, and all documents relating to them. The separate bank account is the rule donors should remember: welfare money must never pass through a personal account.

Changing the Constitution

Section 8 says no amendment of the constitution of a registered agency is valid unless it has been approved by the Registration Authority. A copy of the amendment must be forwarded to the Authority, and if it is satisfied that the amendment is not contrary to the Ordinance or the rules, it may approve it. Where it approves an amendment, it issues a copy of the amendment certified by it, which is conclusive evidence that the amendment is duly approved. Changing the objects, the name, or the governing structure without this approval has no legal effect.

Suspension of the Governing Body

Section 9 gives the Registration Authority a strong control power. If, after such inquiries as it thinks fit, it is satisfied that a registered agency has been responsible for any irregularity in respect of its funds, or for any maladministration in the conduct of its affairs, or has failed to comply with the Ordinance or the rules, it may by order in writing suspend the governing body. It then appoints an administrator, or a caretaker body of not more than five persons, with all the authority and powers of the governing body. Every suspension order must be placed before a Board of not more than five persons constituted by the Provincial Government, which may order reinstatement, or dissolution and reconstitution of the governing body. The governing body against whom a dissolution and reconstitution order is made may appeal to the Provincial Government within thirty days, and the decision of the Provincial Government is final and cannot be called in question in any court.

Dissolution: the Money Never Goes to the Members

Section 10 lets the Registration Authority report an agency to the Provincial Government if it has reason to believe the agency is acting against its constitution, contrary to the Ordinance or the rules, or in a manner prejudicial to the interests of the public. After giving the agency such opportunity of being heard as it thinks fit, the Authority reports, and the Provincial Government, if satisfied, may order the agency dissolved from a specified date. Section 11 covers voluntary dissolution: no registered agency can be dissolved by its governing body or members on their own. Not less than three-fifths of the members may apply to the Provincial Government for an order of dissolution. Section 12 sets out the consequences: on dissolution the registration stands cancelled, the Provincial Government may freeze the agency's money, securities and assets, appoint a competent person to wind up its affairs, and order any money, securities and assets remaining after all debts and liabilities are satisfied to be paid or transferred to another agency with similar objects. As with a society and a Section 42 company, the assets never go to the members.

Anyone Can Check the Record

Section 13 is the donor's tool. Any person may, on payment of the prescribed fee, inspect at the office of the Registration Authority any document relating to a registered agency, or obtain a copy of or an extract from any such document. Before donating to a welfare organisation, a donor can check its registration, its constitution, and its filed reports. A genuine agency has nothing to hide from this inspection.

Penalties

Section 14 punishes any person who contravenes the Ordinance or any rule or order made under it, or who makes any false statement or false representation in an application for registration or in any report or statement submitted to the Registration Authority or published for general information. The punishment is imprisonment for up to six months, or a fine of up to two thousand rupees, or both. Where the offender is a company, body corporate or association of persons, every director, manager, secretary and other officer is deemed guilty unless he proves the offence was committed without his knowledge or consent. No court can take cognizance of an offence under the Ordinance except on a written complaint by the Registration Authority or an officer authorised by it. Section 17 adds that the Provincial Government may, by Gazette notification, exempt any agency or class of agencies from all or any provisions of the Ordinance.

Common Mistakes

First, collecting donations before registration, when section 3 prohibits establishing or continuing an agency without it. Second, keeping welfare money in a personal bank account, when section 7(c) requires a separate account in the agency's name at an approved bank. Third, skipping the Annual Report and audited accounts, or filing them but never publishing them, when section 7(b) requires both filing and publication. Fourth, amending the constitution without approval, when section 8 makes an unapproved amendment invalid. Fifth, treating the governing body as permanent, when section 9 allows suspension for fund irregularity or maladministration. Sixth, assuming a rejected application can be refiled at leisure, when section 6 gives only thirty days to appeal.

What This Means for You

Before you start a welfare organisation, fix three things: a constitution that fits a scheduled field of welfare work, an application to the Registration Authority with the prescribed fee, and a separate bank account in the agency's name. Then keep the audited accounts, the Annual Report and the publication up to date every year, because the Ordinance controls agencies after registration just as strictly as before it.
