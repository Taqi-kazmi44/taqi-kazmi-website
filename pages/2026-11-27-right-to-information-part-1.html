Right to Information: What the Right Is and Who Can Ask

You pay taxes, you follow the rules, and then a government office refuses to tell you how your money was spent. The law says you do not have to accept that.

The Sindh Transparency and Right to Information Act, 2016 (Sindh Act No. XV of 2017) was passed by the Provincial Assembly of Sindh on 13 March 2017, assented to by the Governor on 8 April 2017, and published in the Gazette on 12 April 2017. Section 1 says it extends to the whole of the Province of Sindh and came into force at once. It repealed the earlier Sindh Freedom of Information Act, 2006 (section 23). A separate federal law, the Right of Access to Information Act, 2017, covers federal bodies. This series covers the Sindh Act.

Who can ask? Section 2(a) defines an applicant as a citizen of Pakistan or a legal person registered or incorporated in Pakistan. Any citizen can ask. You do not need to be a lawyer, a journalist, or an affected party, and you do not need to explain why you want the information.

What counts as information? Section 2(g) defines information very widely. It means any information held by a public body, including any memo, book, design, map, contract, representation, pamphlet, brochure, order, notification, document, plan, letter, report, accounts statement, project proposal, photograph, audio, video, drawing, film, any instrument prepared through electronic process, machine-readable document, and any other documentary material, regardless of its physical form. A contract, a budget, a notification, a report, or a photograph can all be information under this Act.

Which bodies are covered? Section 2(i) defines a public body to include a department, attached department, or autonomous or semi-autonomous body of the Government, a company of the Government, or a special institution created by the Government; a local government constituted under the Sindh Local Government Act, 2013; the Secretariat of the Governor of Sindh; any Court, Tribunal, Office, Board, Commission, Council, or other body functioning in the province of Sindh; the Provincial Assembly of Sindh; a statutory body established under a provincial law; a non-government organisation substantially financed by the Government or a local government; and the Secretariat of the Chief Minister, Sindh. In practice, this covers most of the provincial government a citizen deals with every day.

What the right includes: section 2(k) says the right to information includes the right to inspect any work or document, to take a certified copy of a document, and to obtain a copy of information in electronic form. Section 4 gives every applicant the right, in the prescribed manner, to acquire information or a record held by any public body. Section 3 requires every public body to endeavour at all times to maintain transparency in the discharge of its functions, and section 5 requires every public body to ensure that all records it holds are properly maintained so it can comply with the Act.

Some information is published without asking: section 6 requires every public body to proactively disclose and publish fifteen categories of information, including its organisation, functions and duties, the powers of its officers, its rules and notifications, its decision-making processes, its budget and actual expenditure, and a directory of its officers and employees with their qualifications and remuneration. Part 3 of this series explains proactive disclosure in detail.

Common mistakes: first, assuming information is a favour the office grants, when Article 19A and section 4 make it a right. Second, believing only journalists or lawyers can ask, when section 2(a) gives the right to every citizen. Third, asking a federal body under the Sindh Act, when the Sindh Act covers Sindh public bodies and a separate federal law covers federal bodies.

What this means for you: if a Sindh government office holds a record, you can ask for it in writing, inspect it, or take a certified copy. You do not need a reason, and you do not need a lawyer. Part 2 explains exactly how to file the request, who receives it, and the deadlines the office must meet.
