SC: Fraud in a Registered Sale Deed Must Be Proved by the Claimant

A two-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Justice Jamal Khan Mandokhail and Justice Naeem Akhtar Afghan, ruled in CPLA No.4211/2023 that anyone who claims a registered sale deed was executed through fraud, forgery or misrepresentation must prove that allegation with clear and reliable evidence. Mere general allegations, or simply denying the genuineness of the registered document, do not shift the initial burden of proof to the other side. The judgment was reported on 2 October 2026.

What was decided

  1. The Court converted the civil petition for leave to appeal (CPLA No.4211/2023) into an appeal and allowed it. It set aside the Lahore High Court judgment dated 26 September 2023 and the appellate court judgment dated 12 January 2012, and restored the trial court judgment dated 29 September 2011.
  2. The dispute concerned a sale deed registered on 21 August 2000. The plaintiffs alleged the deed was the product of fraud, forgery or misrepresentation, but could not produce reliable evidence to prove the allegation.
  3. The petitioner, defending the registered deed, produced both attesting witnesses. The Court found their testimony consistent and reliable.
  4. Even a mere denial by the signatory of a registered sale deed is not enough to shift the burden of proof onto the party relying on the registered document.

The key legal point

  1. The Court relied on Article 117 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984: the burden of proving a fact lies on the person who asserts it. Fraud, being a serious allegation, must be established through clear and convincing evidence; vague or general claims of fraud carry no weight.
  2. This matters for every property buyer and seller: a registered document carries a presumption of correctness in court. If someone challenges your registered sale deed by crying fraud, the burden of proving fraud rests on the challenger, not on you.
  3. Practical note: the judgment does not say fraud in sale deeds can never be proved, only that the allegation must be backed by evidence. Before buying property, verify the chain of title, get the registered document examined, and keep your own records. If you allege fraud, preserve and present your evidence from the start.
