2026 SCMR 274

A government employee was dismissed without being allowed to cross-examine the witnesses against him.

A government employee was dismissed without being allowed to cross-examine the witnesses against him.
The Supreme Court declared that dismissal void.

In Ain-ud-Din vs Acting Sector Commander NHMP, Shahi Bag Kalat Quetta, 2026 SCMR 274, the Court held that under Rule 8(2) of the NH&MP (Efficiency and Discipline) Rules, 2016, read with Article 10-A of the Constitution, the right to cross-examine is a vested and inalienable right of the accused in departmental proceedings. It is not waived by his failure to specifically request it. The Inquiry Officer must afford the opportunity, and if the accused declines, written reasons must be recorded.

Since the petitioner had been denied cross-examination of the prosecution witnesses, the Court set aside both his dismissal from service and the Federal Service Tribunal's judgment, directed a de novo inquiry within two months, and held that he stood reinstated pending the inquiry.

For law students, the concepts to master are Article 10-A (due process), the distinction between vested and waivable rights, and the inquiry officer's duty to record reasons. For government employees facing departmental proceedings, the message is clear: you cannot be dismissed without a fair chance to question the witnesses against you.

Case: Ain-ud-Din vs Acting Sector Commander NHMP, Shahi Bag Kalat Quetta, 2026 SCMR 274. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 23 October 2025. Bench: Syed Mansoor Ali Shah and Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, JJ. Docket: Civil Petition No. 1531 of 2025.
