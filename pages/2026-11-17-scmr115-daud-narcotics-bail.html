Muhammad Daud v The State, 2026 SCMR 115: Only Your Own Quantity Counts for Bail

The Supreme Court of Pakistan held that at the bail stage, each accused is answerable only for the quantity of narcotics recovered from him individually. A collective recovery cannot be fastened on every accused unless the prosecution shows each one knew about the others' possession.

In Muhammad Daud and another v The State and another, the total recovery was 3.2 kilograms of methamphetamine, but only about 1 kilogram was recovered from each accused. The case was under section 9(c) of the Control of Narcotic Substances Act, 1997. The question before the Supreme Court was whether the whole 3.2 kilograms could be attributed to every accused at the bail stage.

The Court held that it could not. At the bail stage, each accused is attributed only the quantity recovered from him individually. On 1 kilogram, the maximum sentence is seven years under the table in section 9(2) of the Act, so the case does not fall within the prohibitory clause of section 497 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898. The total collective recovery cannot be attributed to every accused absent confidence-inspiring material showing conscious knowledge of the others' possession.

The Court also held that bail is the rule where the prohibitory clause does not apply, absent exceptional circumstances such as repetition of the offence or absconding. A bare apprehension, such as one accused being a foreign national, is not enough to refuse bail. The petitions were converted into appeals and allowed, and bail was granted.

What this means for you: in a narcotics case, the quantity that matters for your bail is the quantity recovered from you, not the total found with a group. The prosecution must prove your own link to the drugs, and a bare fear that you might abscond is not enough to keep you in jail while the trial runs.

Reference: Muhammad Daud and another v The State and another, 2026 SCMR 115, Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 19 June 2025, bench: Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, Aamer Farooq and Ali Baqar Najafi, JJ, Criminal Petitions for Leave to Appeal Nos. 537 and 559 of 2025.
