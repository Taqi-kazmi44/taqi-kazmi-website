2026 SCMR 77: Unproved Information Is Not False Information

A two-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Justice Irfan Saadat Khan and Justice Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, held in Ali Adnan Sheikha vs I.G. Police Sindh and others, reported as 2026 SCMR 77 and decided on 5 September 2025 (Cr.P.L.A. No. 147-K of 2025), that information given to a public servant with a bona fide belief in its truth is "not proved", not "false". It cannot attract Section 182 of the Pakistan Penal Code unless its falsity is proved through judicial proceedings.

What was decided

  1. Information given to a public servant with a bona fide belief in its truth is "not proved", not "false", and cannot attract Section 182 PPC unless its falsity is proved through judicial proceedings. Proceedings under Section 182 can only be initiated by the public servant before whom the false information was laid and which led to criminal proceedings found false through the judicial process.
  2. Failure to prove allegations before the judicial forum does not make the information "false" so as to invoke penal provisions like Section 182 PPC. In the absence of any judicial finding that the information was false, Section 182 is not attracted.
  3. The Court added that, under Section 195 CrPC, a magistrate can take cognisance of an offence under Section 182 PPC only on a written complaint from that specific public servant or a superior authority, not on anyone's direction. Any other interpretation would multiply criminal litigation out of the same proceedings and open the floodgates for frivolous cases wherever a complainant could not succeed.
  4. The petitioner had sought a direction to register a case under Section 182 against two persons for allegedly lodging a false FIR and giving false evidence some 20 years earlier. The Court upheld the Sindh High Court and Sessions Court orders refusing that direction, dismissed the appeal, and imposed a cost of Rs 25,000 on the petitioner.

The key legal point

  1. For ordinary people, the protection is real: if you filed a genuine complaint and it could not be proved, that failure alone cannot turn into a fresh Section 182 case against you. "Not proved" and "false" are two different things in the eyes of the law.
  2. For law students, this case is essential reading on the scope of Section 182 PPC, the difference between "false" and "not proved" information, and the framework of Sections 173, 190 and 195 CrPC that controls who may complain.
  3. Practical note: the protection is for bona fide information. Deliberately false information, proved false through the judicial process, can still attract Section 182, but only on the complaint of the public servant who received it.
