NA Passes Bill to Turn State Life into a Company: What It Means for Policyholders

On Thursday, October 22, the National Assembly passed the Life Insurance (Nationalisation) (Amendment) Bill, 2026, beginning the corporatisation of the State Life Insurance Corporation into State Life Insurance Company Limited under the Companies Act, 2017.

On Thursday, October 22, 2026, the National Assembly passed the Life Insurance (Nationalisation) (Amendment) Bill, 2026 by a majority vote, after clause-by-clause consideration. The bill was moved by the Minister of State for Finance and Railways, Bilal Azhar Kayani.

The bill provides that the federal government will incorporate a new company, State Life Insurance Company Limited, under the Companies Act, 2017. Its chief executive officer and board of directors will be appointed under the State-Owned Enterprises Act, 2023 and the Companies Act, 2017.

Once the company is incorporated, the government will issue a vesting order in the official gazette. On a specified date, all assets, liabilities, contracts, policies, proceedings and undertakings of the existing State Life Insurance Corporation will transfer to the new company, and the corporation will stand dissolved.

The bill also creates a legal framework for possible future privatisation or divestment, subject to federal government approval and applicable laws. The government has clarified that the amendments themselves are not a decision to privatise State Life.

According to the statement of objects and reasons, the aim is to remove the structural and operational constraints of the current statutory framework, which dates back to the Life Insurance (Nationalisation) Order, 1972, and to bring State Life in line with modern corporate governance standards, improving efficiency and transparency.

What this means for you: your State Life policy is not cancelled. The bill transfers existing policies to the new company, so policyholders continue under the same contracts. But the corporate form makes future privatisation legally possible, which is why this bill matters.

In the same session, the House referred the Privatisation Commission (Amendment) Bill, 2026 to the relevant committee and laid the OGRA (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 (No. V of 2026) before the House under Article 89(2) of the Constitution.
