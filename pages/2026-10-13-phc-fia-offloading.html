PHC Declares Off-Loading of Student from International Flight Illegal

The Peshawar High Court (Justice Syed Arshad Ali and Justice Babar Sattar) has declared that the FIA has no lawful authority to interfere with the international travel of a Pakistani citizen holding valid travel documents unless authorised by law, ruling that the off-loading of a student bound for Turkiye at Peshawar airport on 10 August 2026 was illegal.

In a detailed 10-page judgment, a Peshawar High Court bench of Justice Syed Arshad Ali and Justice Babar Sattar declared that the FIA has no lawful authority to interfere with the international travel of a Pakistani citizen holding valid travel documents, unless the interference is authorised by law and based on relevant material within the scope of that authority.

The case: student Yaseen Khan had secured admission to a four-year computer engineering programme at Istanbul Topkapi University, obtained a Turkish study visa and bought his ticket, but was off-loaded from his Turkiye-bound flight by immigration officials at Peshawar's Bacha Khan International Airport on 10 August 2026. The officials questioned his low grades and study gap and doubted his capacity to pursue the course. The court held that judging academic suitability falls beyond the FIA's lawful remit, since no statute gives it that power.

Key points from the judgment:
- A valid passport and visa do not exempt a passenger from lawful immigration scrutiny, but scrutiny cannot be enlarged into an unrestricted power.
- The mere existence of an administrative SOP or internal practice cannot confer a power which the parent statute does not provide.
- Unlawful off-loading may expose the immigration officer to legal consequences, including a civil action for recovery of compensation, and the court may award compensation in appropriate cases.
- Copies of the judgment were sent to the federal interior secretary and the FIA director general for information, compliance and guidance to immigration authorities.

What this means for you: if you hold a valid passport and visa, an immigration officer cannot stop you from boarding on vague grounds or personal assessments. Your right to travel is constitutionally protected, and any restraint must be anchored in law.
