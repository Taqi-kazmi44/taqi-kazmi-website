Is Stealing Electricity a Crime in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask: is taking electricity without paying for it, by tampering with the meter or taking a direct connection, actually a crime? The answer is yes. It is a criminal offence under the Electricity Act, 1910. This page states the rule exactly as the statute does.

What Section 39 of the Electricity Act, 1910 says

Section 39 (Theft of energy) provides that whoever dishonestly abstracts, consumes or uses energy shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both. The section adds that the existence of any device, contrivance or artificial means for such abstraction, consumption or use is prima facie evidence of the offence.

What counts as dishonest abstraction

The Explanation to Section 39 says a person is deemed to have dishonestly abstracted, consumed or used energy if he is found to have tampered or interfered with the wiring connections of any meter or measuring apparatus installed by the licensee; or to have prevented the meter from duly registering the amount of energy supplied, for example by drilling a hole in its casing or jamming its mechanism; or to have connected his installations directly to the licensee's works without passing through a meter or other measuring apparatus, without the written consent of the licensee. A direct connection from the line, commonly called a kunda, therefore falls squarely within the offence.

Section 39A: devices and artificial means

Section 39A is a separate offence. Whoever installs or uses any device, contrivance or artificial means for the dishonest abstraction, consumption or use of energy, whether he derives any benefit from it or not, is punishable with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both. If such a device is proved to exist on a premises, it is presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the person in control of the premises committed the offence.

Helping someone steal electricity is also an offence

Under both Section 39(2) and Section 39A(2), whoever abets or conspires in the commission of these acts is deemed to have committed the offence himself.

The bill for stolen energy: Section 26A

Section 26A allows the licensee to charge the consumer for the amount of energy deemed to have been dishonestly abstracted, consumed or used, for the period during which the meter or measuring apparatus was tampered with or prevented from registering the supply. So apart from the criminal punishment, the stolen energy itself can be billed.

The key legal point

  1. Yes, stealing electricity is a crime in Pakistan: up to three years imprisonment, or fine, or both, under Section 39 of the Electricity Act, 1910.
  2. What this means for you: if your meter is faulty or your bill seems wrong, get it checked and fixed through the proper complaint channel instead of tampering with it, because a tampered meter is treated as theft in law.
