FCC: Judicial Sympathy Cannot Override Remission Law; Life Convict Must Serve 15 Actual Years

The Federal Constitutional Court has upheld the Balochistan High Court's verdict declaring excessive prison remissions illegal: under Rule 217(ii) of the Pakistan Prison Rules, 1978, a life convict must actually serve at least 15 years, and judicial sympathy cannot override statutory commands.

Suba Khan was convicted of murder under Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code by an additional sessions judge in Loralai on 26 May 2015 and sentenced to life imprisonment, a fine of Rs200,000 and six months' simple imprisonment in default of payment.

According to the prison's nominal roll dated 17 August 2023, Suba Khan had actually served 9 years, 4 months and 19 days, while remissions of 15 years, 7 months and 18 days had been credited to his account, leaving about five months and 23 days remaining. The Balochistan High Court declared such excessive remissions illegal and void.

A two-judge bench of the Federal Constitutional Court's Branch Registry Quetta, headed by Justice Rozi Khan Barrech with Justice Arshad Hussain Shah, has now upheld that verdict in a 16-page judgment.

The court held that Rule 199 and Rules 212, 213, 215 and 216 of the Pakistan Prison Rules, 1978 enable remission, but Rule 217 expressly circumscribes it: under Rule 217(ii), the cumulative effect of all remissions cannot reduce the actual period of imprisonment of a life convict to less than 15 years. This is mandatory, with no exception except lawful alteration by a competent authority.

The judgment records the philosophy of reformation and rehabilitation but balances it against the deterrent and retributive force of sentences. The court stated plainly that judicial sympathy cannot override statutory commands, and a remission computed beyond the limits of the law confers no enforceable right.

Why it matters: remission is a concession within the law, not a bypass of it. For life imprisonment, at least 15 years of actual imprisonment is compulsory, and courts may not shorten that floor out of sympathy. Anyone checking a convict's release timeline must count only actual custody against that 15-year floor.

Reference: Federal Constitutional Court, two-judge bench of Branch Registry Quetta (Justice Rozi Khan Barrech, Justice Arshad Hussain Shah), reported by Dawn, 1 October 2026: https://www.dawn.com/news/2033879
