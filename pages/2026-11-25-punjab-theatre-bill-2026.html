Punjab Theatre Bill 2026: New Law Threatens Artistic Freedom

Your stage, your script, your voice. Punjab has just passed a law that could decide what artists are allowed to perform.

On Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the Punjab Assembly passed the Punjab Dramatic and Theatrical Performances Bill 2026, along with seven other bills, after rejecting all opposition amendments. The bill replaces the Dramatic Performances Act of 1876, a law nearly 150 years old.

Under the new bill, no theatrical or dramatic performance can take place at a public venue without a licence from the information and culture secretary. It introduces mandatory artist registration, venue licensing, licensing fees, script scrutiny charges and artist registration fees. Unauthorised stage productions can attract up to six months in jail and fines reaching Rs 1 million. An appeal lies through the administrative hierarchy up to the Punjab chief secretary.

Artists are pushing back. At a roundtable convened by the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan in Lahore, theatre practitioners said the bill was never circulated among stakeholders and they learned of it through the media. They questioned vague terms in the bill such as vulgarity, obscenity and disaffection, and warned that compulsory registration, venue licensing and pre-performance script scrutiny would put artistic expression under executive control. Some called it even more repressive than the 1876 Act it replaces.

Ajoka Executive Director Shahid Nadeem said the bill was not circulated among stakeholders before its passage. Filmmaker Kanwal Khoosat said compulsory registration would constrain gender and political expression. HRCP Secretary General Harris Khalique said artistic expression should not be subjected to a registration and licensing regime, and called for the bill's repeal.

The participants urged the Governor of Punjab to withhold assent and return the bill to the Assembly for further deliberation, saying it must not violate Article 19 of the Constitution, which guarantees freedom of speech and expression, or Pakistan's international obligations to protect free expression.

What this means for you: the bill has been passed by the Assembly but it is not law yet. It becomes law only after the governor's assent. If you are an artist, a theatre worker or simply someone who watches stage plays, this law will decide what can be performed in Punjab and who gets to decide.
