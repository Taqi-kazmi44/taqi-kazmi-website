SC: Distant Relatives' DNA Cannot Deny Paternity or Inheritance

Can your inheritance be challenged by a DNA test done on your distant cousins? The Supreme Court of Pakistan has said no. A three-member bench upheld the Peshawar High Court and dismissed a petition that tried to use DNA tests of extended family members to disprove the heirs' lineage.

What was reported

  1. A three-member bench of the Supreme Court (Justice Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Justice Irfan Saadat Khan and Justice Shahid Bilal Hassan) upheld the Peshawar High Court's Abbottabad bench and dismissed the petition of Barkat Khan and others, who claimed the plaintiffs were not the real heirs of Muhammad Ayub Khan (son of Arsala Khan, died 10 October 2006).
  2. The plaintiffs (Banaras Khan and others) filed their suit on 14 December 2007, claiming to be the legal heirs of Muhammad Ayub Khan. The defendants, who joined the case on 15 March 2008, alleged the plaintiffs were in fact the offspring of Faris Khan (son of Haibat Khan).
  3. The litigation travelled a long road: the trial court dismissed the suit on 13 July 2011, the appellate court reversed that decision on 11 December 2012, and the Peshawar High Court dismissed the revision on 22 February 2021.
  4. The petitioners relied on a DNA test conducted on extended family members, arguing it disproved the plaintiffs' lineage. The court rejected that argument: a claim to a biological father's paternity or inheritance cannot be dismissed solely on DNA tests from indirect or distant relatives. Without a direct sample from the deceased father, the report lacks conclusive weight and offers only statistical probabilities, not definitive proof.

The key legal point

  1. The court invoked Article 128 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984: a child's birth during a valid marriage is conclusive proof of legitimacy.
  2. Indirect genetic analysis gives statistical probabilities only. It cannot substitute for the direct sample that would be needed for a conclusive result.
  3. The documentary record supported the plaintiffs throughout: NADRA records, the Union Council Dhamtour death certificate, grave photographs and marriage agreements all consistently listed Arsala Khan as the father of Muhammad Ayub Khan.
  4. Why it matters: in an inheritance dispute, documentary evidence (NADRA, Union Council records) and the presumption of legitimacy under Article 128 carry real weight. A DNA report from distant relatives alone cannot decide the case.
