LHC Rules Child Maintenance Cannot Be Reduced by Parents

A child's maintenance is a right, not a favour. The Lahore High Court has ruled that maintenance (nan-nufqa) is an absolute legal right of minor children — it cannot be reduced, waived or surrendered, not even with the mutual consent of both parents.

What was reported

  1. Justice Usman Ghani Rashid Cheema of the Lahore High Court issued a six-page judgment on 3 October 2026 while deciding a petition filed by Shazia Noreen, a mother with custody of two minor children. The mother had earlier signed an affidavit agreeing to a reduced maintenance amount under a mutual parental agreement fixing the child's monthly maintenance at Rs 30,000.
  2. The court set that agreement aside, accepted the petition, and remanded the case back to the family court to decide the matter afresh after evaluating the father's financial status and capability. The court observed that minor children are not our past, but our present.
  3. Before reaching the High Court, the children had appealed before the Sessions Court in Sargodha. The High Court held that a family court is not bound by a settlement between parents if it affects the child's welfare or legal rights.
  4. Before fixing maintenance, the court must independently assess the child's needs, standard of living and overall welfare, along with the father's financial capacity and social status.

The key legal point

  1. Maintenance (nan-nufqa) is an absolute legal right of minor children. It cannot be reduced, waived or surrendered — even with the mutual consent of both parents.
  2. Neither the parents nor a guardian has the authority to give up this right. A mother acting as guardian cannot waive her child's maintenance through an agreement with the father.
  3. A family court is not bound by a parental settlement that affects the child's welfare or legal rights; it must assess the child's needs independently.
  4. Why it matters: if after talaq or khula a child's nan-nufqa was reduced, or a muahida fixed a low maintenance amount, that agreement will not survive in court. The child's nafqah is the child's own right — the parents cannot end or reduce it.
