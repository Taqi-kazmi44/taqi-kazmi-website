Is Posting Someone's Photo Online to Damage Their Reputation a Crime in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask: is it a crime to post someone's photo or personal information on social media to ruin their reputation? Yes. The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (PECA) makes this a specific criminal offence.

The legal rule: offences against dignity (Section 20, PECA 2016)

Section 20 of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (Act No. XL of 2016) creates the offence titled "offences against dignity of a natural person". It says: whoever intentionally and publicly exhibits or displays or transmits any information through any information system, which he knows to be false, and intimidates or harms the reputation or privacy of a natural person, is guilty of an offence. Publishing someone's photo or personal information on social media to harm their reputation or privacy falls within this provision.

The section also gives the victim a civil remedy. Under subsection (2), any aggrieved person (or their guardian, where the person is a minor) may apply to the Authority for removal, destruction of, or blocking access to that information, and the Authority must pass reasonable orders on that application.

The punishment

The punishment fixed by Section 20(1) is imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years, or a fine which may extend to one million rupees, or both.

What the offence requires

Three things must be present: (1) the act is intentional and public, done through an information system (for example a social media platform or messaging app); (2) the person knows the information to be false; and (3) it intimidates the person, or harms their reputation or their privacy. Where the content targets modesty (for example private or explicit images), Section 21 of the same Act may apply instead.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if someone posts your photo or false claims about you online to harm your dignity or reputation, the law treats it as a cyber crime. Preserve the evidence (screenshots, links, dates), report it to the NCCIA (National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency) through its online complaint portal or helpline 1799, and you may also apply under Section 20(2) for removal or blocking of the content.
