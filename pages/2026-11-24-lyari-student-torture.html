Lyari: Student Tortured on Video as Five Arrested, Including Son of Slain Gang Commander

A student was abducted, stripped and tortured in Lyari, and the assault was filmed and shared online. Five men have been arrested.

The incident took place in Lyari, Karachi, within the jurisdiction of Baghdadi police station. The victim, a student identified as Ghulam Nabi, was allegedly abducted by the suspects, stripped and subjected to brutal torture. The suspects also recorded the assault and later circulated the video on social media.

Action taken so far: police, in a joint operation with intelligence agencies, arrested five suspects. They have been identified as Sharif, son of slain Lyari gang war commander Noor, also known as Baba Ladla, Shahzaib, son of Rasheed Ali, Ghanzafar Ali, son of Hashim, Faraz, son of Qasim, and Uzair, son of Ishaq. The suspects were produced before a court, which granted police three-day physical remand for further investigation. A case has been registered at Baghdadi police station under Sections 365, 324, 342, 337-F(1) and 34 of the Pakistan Penal Code.

What the law says: Section 365 of the Pakistan Penal Code covers kidnapping or abduction, and carries imprisonment of up to seven years and a fine. Section 324 covers an attempt to commit murder, and carries imprisonment of up to ten years and a fine. Section 342 covers wrongful confinement, and carries imprisonment of up to one year or a fine. Section 34 applies where several persons act with a common intention. The allegations against the arrested men are yet to be proved in court, and they remain innocent until proven guilty. An arrest is not a verdict, and a viral video is evidence that must still be proved before a court.

What this means for you: if you witness such violence, or if the police refuse to register your case, you can file a written complaint at the police station, approach the SSP or DPO of the district, or file an application under Section 22-A of the Code of Criminal Procedure before the Justice of Peace for registration of an FIR. You can also approach the Sindh Human Rights Commission or the National Commission for Human Rights. When a video of an assault circulates, save a copy and report it to the police at once, because that recording can become the strongest evidence in the case.
