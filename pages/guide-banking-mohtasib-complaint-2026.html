How to File a Complaint with the Banking Mohtasib Pakistan: A Step-by-Step Guide

When your bank will not fix a wrong deduction, a delayed refund, or poor handling of your complaint, the branch manager's desk is not the end of the road. The Banking Mohtasib Pakistan is a free, time bound forum above the bank, created under the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, and you do not need a lawyer to use it.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. Complain to your bank FIRST, in writing. Send it to the bank's Complaint Management Unit by email, letter, or the bank's own portal. Describe the issue with dates and evidence, say clearly what you want fixed, and state that you intend to approach the Banking Mohtasib if it is not resolved. Keep a dated copy and the complaint reference number.
  2. The bank must respond within 30 days. This is Section 82D of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, as amended in 2024 and published in the Gazette of Pakistan (the period was 45 days before the amendment).
  3. If the bank does not reply within 30 days, or its reply is unsatisfactory, file your complaint with the Banking Mohtasib within the next 30 days. The complaint is made on solemn affirmation or oath: fill the prescribed form, sign it, and attach a CNIC copy, your correspondence with the bank, and all supporting documents. You can file online or send it to the Banking Mohtasib Pakistan, Shaheen Complex, 5th Floor, M.R. Kiyani Road, Karachi. The Mohtasib may condone delay in filing if you show valid grounds.
  4. The service is completely free, and you do not need a lawyer. The Mohtasib hears complaints about banking malpractice, violation of banking laws and regulations, excessive delays and inefficiency, poor service, discriminatory actions, and harassment in loan recovery. For public sector banks it can additionally hear corruption, nepotism and maladministration. Only the aggrieved person or their lawful attorney can file.
  5. Know the limits. The Mohtasib cannot decide bank policy matters like the schedule of charges or loan policies, cannot order a bank to sanction a loan or waive or reduce mark up, cannot hear bank employees' service matters, and cannot take a case that has already been decided by a court or the State Bank, or is pending before any court.
  6. If you disagree with the decision, you can file a representation to the President of Pakistan within 30 days, under the Federal Ombudsman Institutional Reforms Act, 2013.

The governing law

The procedure is governed by Section 82D of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962. Sub-section (1) requires the complaint to be made on solemn affirmation or oath in writing, addressed to the Banking Mohtasib, setting out the full particulars of the transaction complained of and the name and address of the complainant. Sub-section (2), as substituted by the 2024 amendment, requires the complainant to first request the banking company to redress the grievance; if the bank fails to respond or gives an unsatisfactory reply within 30 days, the complaint may be filed within a further 30 days, with power in the Mohtasib to condone delay on valid grounds. Sub-section (3) lets the Mohtasib adopt any procedure he considers appropriate for investigation, with no order against a bank without notice and a hearing. Sub-section (4) bars stay orders and complaints in matters pending before a court. Under the Federal Ombudsman Institutional Reforms Act, 2013, a person aggrieved by a decision, order, findings or recommendations of the Banking Mohtasib may file a representation to the President within 30 days.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: an unresolved bank deduction, a delayed refund, or rude mishandling of your complaint does not have to end at the branch manager's desk. The law gives you a free, time bound forum above the bank, and even a late filing can be accepted if your reason is valid.
