Is Threatening Someone a Crime in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions ordinary citizens ask: is it a crime to threaten someone, for example to scare them into paying money or dropping a case? Yes. The Pakistan Penal Code treats it as a specific offence called criminal intimidation.

The legal rule: threatening is criminal intimidation (Section 503 PPC)

Section 503 of the Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, defines the offence. Whoever threatens another person with injury to their person, reputation or property, or to the person or reputation of someone that person cares about, with the intent to cause alarm to that person, or to cause that person to do something they are not legally bound to do (or to stop doing something they are legally entitled to do) as the means of avoiding the execution of the threat, commits criminal intimidation.

The section gives a clear illustration: if A, for the purpose of inducing B to desist from prosecuting a civil suit, threatens to burn B's house, A is guilty of criminal intimidation. So a threat used as pressure, for example "withdraw the case or I will harm you", falls squarely within this offence. Words alone, without a real threat of injury, do not qualify: the section's own ingredients require a threat of injury to person, reputation or property made with the stated intent.

The punishment (Section 506 PPC)

Section 506 fixes the punishment. Whoever commits criminal intimidation is punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to two years, or with fine, or with both.

The punishment is heavier in serious cases. If the threat is to cause death or grievous hurt, to cause the destruction of any property by fire, to cause an offence punishable with death or imprisonment for life or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to seven years, or to impute unchastity to a woman, the offender is punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, or with fine, or with both.

What counts as a threat

Three things must be present: (1) a threat of injury to the person's body, reputation or property, or to someone they care about; (2) the intent to cause alarm, or to force them to do something they are not bound to do, or to stop them from doing something they are entitled to do; and (3) the threat is used as the means of getting that result. A threat does not have to be carried out to count as the offence.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if someone threatens your life, your reputation or your property to scare you or force your hand, for example to make you pay money or drop a complaint, you can report it to the police as criminal intimidation under Sections 503 and 506 of the Pakistan Penal Code. Keep any evidence of the threat, such as messages or witnesses.
