Is Peaceful Protest Your Legal Right in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask: can you take to the streets to raise your voice, or can the police simply stop you? The short answer is yes, peaceful protest is your legal right, but it is not an unlimited right. This page states the rule exactly as the Constitution and the law state it.

Article 16: the constitutional guarantee

Article 16 of the Constitution of Pakistan, 1973, under the heading "Freedom of assembly", provides: "Every citizen shall have the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to any reasonable restrictions imposed by law in the interest of public order." The right belongs to every citizen, and it has two conditions built into it: the assembly must be peaceful, and it must be without arms.

The right is not unlimited: three lawful limits

First, the gathering must stay peaceful and unarmed. A violent or armed gathering is outside the protection of Article 16 altogether. Second, the administration may temporarily restrict a gathering where public order is at risk, for example through an order under Section 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, under which a District Magistrate may direct persons to abstain from an act where there is an apprehended danger to public tranquillity, a riot or an affray. Third, in Islamabad, the Peaceful Assembly and Public Order Act, 2024 (Act No. XIX of 2024) applies: the organiser of an assembly must apply in writing to the District Magistrate at least seven days before the assembly, and the District Magistrate shall not permit any assembly outside a designated area.

What this means for you

You can raise your voice peacefully, but follow the lawful process in your city, keep the gathering peaceful and unarmed, and respect any lawful restriction in force at the time. A peaceful assembly that follows the law is protected; blocking roads, damaging property or carrying weapons takes the gathering outside that protection and exposes the participants to action under the ordinary criminal law.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: peaceful protest is a fundamental right under Article 16 of the Constitution, but it is a right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to reasonable restrictions in the interest of public order.
  2. Check the rules for your city before you gather. In Islamabad, permission must be sought at least seven days in advance and only a designated area may be used. Elsewhere, a Section 144 order may temporarily restrict a gathering where public order is at risk.
