Bahawalpur: Sub-Inspector Arrested for Slapping a Citizen During a Hotel Check

A routine hotel verification visit ended with the police officer himself in custody. What does the law actually provide when an officer raises a hand on a citizen, and what can an ordinary person do about it?

A video from a private hotel in Bahawalpur went viral on social media: Sub-Inspector Awais Arshad, in charge of the Lari Adda police post, slapped a citizen during a verification check. The guest was staying at the hotel with his wife, and the incident was captured on the hotel's CCTV cameras.

The affected citizen also alleged that the sub-inspector had demanded a bribe and subjected him to violence during questioning. That allegation has not been independently verified, and police have not yet made the sections of the registered case public. The investigation is under way.

On the notice of Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz Sharif, an FIR was registered against the officer. He was arrested, suspended from service, and issued a departmental charge sheet for disciplinary proceedings. Suspension, it should be remembered, is not a punishment. It is an interim step, and the final outcome will be decided by the criminal trial and the departmental inquiry.

What the law says about such conduct: using criminal force against a person is punishable under Section 352 of the Pakistan Penal Code with imprisonment of up to three months, or a fine of up to Rs 1,500, or both. Article 156 of the Police Order 2002 separately punishes a police officer who, without lawful authority or reasonable cause, enters or searches a place, or vexatiously and unnecessarily detains, searches or arrests any person, with imprisonment of up to five years and a fine on conviction. Article 14 of the Constitution of Pakistan declares the dignity of every person inviolable.

What this means for you: if a police officer misuses his authority against you, report it at the police station, complain to the District Police Officer, and preserve any video or CCTV evidence. If the police refuse to register your FIR, you can apply under Section 22-A CrPC before the Justice of Peace.
