The edges of the wound were blackened. Does that prove the shot was fired from close range?
The Supreme Court said: not necessarily.

In Qaiser vs The State, 2026 SCMR 163, the Court clarified that blackening of wound margins can result from a distant shot as well, from a hot bullet touching the skin, or even through clothing. Blackening alone, without burning or tattooing, does not prove close-range firing. But in this case the conviction was maintained, because the medical evidence fully corroborated the witnesses, the witnesses were found reliable, and the accused was arrested at the scene soon after the incident.

For law students, this case defines the relationship between medical and ocular evidence, and how forensic findings must be interpreted. For citizens, it shows that forensic science now plays a decisive role in court decisions.

Case: Qaiser vs The State, 2026 SCMR 163. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 22 September 2025. Bench: Athar Minallah, Irfan Saadat Khan and Malik Shahzad Ahmad Khan, JJ.
