How to Register Your Tenant with the Police: A Step-by-Step Guide

You have found a tenant and signed the rent agreement. One legal step remains, and most landlords in Pakistan never take it. The law requires you to inform the police about your tenant, and ignoring it is a criminal offence. This guide walks you through the exact procedure.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. Check the tenant's identity first. Under Section 5 of the Punjab Information of Temporary Residents Act 2015, you must satisfy yourself about the tenant's identity on the basis of his computerized national identity card or passport before handing over possession. Take a copy of it.
  2. Keep an attested copy of the rent agreement ready. Written proof of the tenancy makes the submission smooth and protects you in any future dispute.
  3. Submit the tenant's information to the nearest police station: CNIC copies of both the landlord and the tenant, a recent photograph of the tenant, the property address, and the rent agreement copy. In Punjab you can also register online on the Punjab Police official portal (punjabpolice.gov.pk) under public services, tenant registration, using a mobile OTP and entering the landlord, tenant and property details.
  4. Remember that one submission covers all of you. Under Section 3(3), if the landlord, the tenant or the property dealer provides the information to the police, the others are not liable for non-provision.
  5. Mind the deadline. In Punjab the information must reach the police within 15 days of delivery of possession (Section 3(1)). In Sindh, under the Sindh Information of Temporary Residents Act 2015, the deadline is 48 hours.
  6. Keep the stamped or acknowledged copy with you as proof of compliance.

The governing law

The procedure is governed by the Punjab Information of Temporary Residents Act 2015: Section 3 imposes the information duty on the property dealer, landlord and tenant; Section 5 requires the CNIC or passport identity check and obliges the landlord to give a copy of the CNIC to the police at the earliest; Section 6 requires the police to maintain a database of tenants and guests; Section 11 punishes knowing contravention with imprisonment up to six months and a fine not less than Rs.10,000 and not more than Rs.100,000; and Section 12 makes the offence cognizable and non-bailable, tried summarily by a Magistrate of the first class. The Sindh Information of Temporary Residents Act 2015 mirrors this scheme with a 48-hour deadline for tenants and a penalty of up to six months imprisonment and a fine of Rs.10,000 to Rs.45,000.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: if you have rented out any house, shop or room and never informed the police, do it now. Tenant verification is not a courtesy. It is a statutory duty, and compliance protects you if your tenant is ever involved in a crime.
