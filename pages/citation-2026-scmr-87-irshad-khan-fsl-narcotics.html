2026 SCMR 87: Collective FSL Report Cannot Prove Narcotics, Accused Acquitted

Irshad Khan v. The State. Decided 12 August 2025 by Jamal Khan Mandokhail, Musarrat Hilali and Shakeel Ahmad, JJ. Cr.P.L.A. No. 219-P of 2023.

WhatsApp inquiryTaqi Kazmi website

The facts

In a narcotics case, the prosecution recovered 20 packets of opium and 3 packets of chars. Instead of testing each packet separately, the Forensic Science Laboratory conducted one collective analysis of all the parcels together and never prepared a separate chemical report for each packet. The substance was sealed with a monogram ("MN") that had no connection to any officer or witness, and that fact remained unexplained on the record.

The Supreme Court's holding

The Court held that the procedural violations destroyed the credibility of the forensic evidence, and the accused was acquitted:

  1. Under the maxim communi observantia non est recedendum, where the law prescribes a specific procedure it must be followed accordingly; any deviation renders the act questionable.
  2. In narcotics cases, a collective FSL analysis of all recovered parcels, without separate testing of each sample and individual reports, violates Supreme Court principles and destroys the credibility of the chemical examination.
  3. A collective forensic report raises serious doubts about the representative nature of the samples actually sent for chemical analysis, since it says nothing about what was tested from each packet.
  4. Where the recovered substance was sealed with a monogram unrelated to any officer or witness and that fact remains unexplained, combined with procedural violations and the absence of credible forensic evidence, the accused is entitled to acquittal.

What this means for you

  1. In narcotics cases, the forensic procedure decides the outcome: every recovered parcel must be tested separately, with its own report.
  2. If the FSL takes a shortcut and tests everything in one batch, the prosecution's scientific evidence can collapse, even where the recovery is otherwise shown.
