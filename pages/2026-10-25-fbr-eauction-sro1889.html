FBR E-Auction of Seized Goods: SRO 1889(I)/2026 Explained

Seized goods in Pakistan will now be auctioned online, and any citizen with a CNIC can bid. The Federal Board of Revenue has introduced a new electronic auction system for seized and confiscated goods, replacing the old manual process with an open online platform.

The Federal Board of Revenue has introduced an electronic auction system for seized and confiscated goods through SRO 1889(I)/2026, notified on 22 October 2026. The notification adds a new chapter to the Sales Tax Rules, 2006, and the system covers confiscated goods and seized goods cleared for disposal under an adjudication order.

Any person holding a valid CNIC or NICOP can register electronically on the FBR auction module by paying a one-time registration fee of not less than Rs25,000. Each registered bidder receives a unique identification number, and the electronic platform handles registration, listings, bidding, approvals, payments, deliveries and record-keeping.

Goods remain open for online bidding for 72 hours from receipt of the first bid. The system displays the highest valid bid and keeps an electronic record of every offer, and the authorised officer must process the highest offer within 72 hours of the close of bidding.

The reserve price of each lot is fixed on the recommendation of a three-member valuation committee of Inland Revenue officers: an additional commissioner, a deputy commissioner and an assistant commissioner. Bids below the reserve price are ordinarily not accepted, though the Commissioner Inland Revenue may approve a bid down to 80 percent of the reserve price for recorded reasons, and the Chief Commissioner may approve lower bids. If goods remain unsold in three consecutive auctions, the committee may reduce the reserve price by up to 10 percent at a time.

Goods under a court stay order cannot be auctioned until the stay ends or is withdrawn. The custodian must upload the inventory, photographs and documents of the goods and arrange physical inspection for interested bidders.

If you win, you must deposit the full bid amount along with applicable taxes, duties and charges within seven days of acceptance, through electronic payment. Goods are released only after full payment is verified.

What this means for you: confiscated goods will now be sold through a transparent online process instead of closed manual auctions, and ordinary citizens can take part.
