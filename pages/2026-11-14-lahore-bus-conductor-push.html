Lahore: Bus Conductor Sacked for Pushing Woman Off Bus After Video Goes Viral

A woman trying to board a bus in Lahore was pushed off the vehicle twice by the conductor, falling onto a parked motorcycle. The video went viral, the conductor has lost his job, and the Punjab Chief Minister has directed the police to take further action.

According to Dawn, the incident took place at a bus terminal in Lahore on Friday, 13 November 2026. A video recorded by passersby shows the woman trying to board the bus when the conductor pushed her. She tried again, and the conductor pushed her off the vehicle. This time, she fell onto a parked motorcycle. The video was shared on social media and drew public outrage, with users demanding action against the conductor and the transport company, Faisal Movers Group.

Action taken so far: Faisal Movers Group sacked the conductor, Ali Rizwan, who was deployed at BRT Lahore, with immediate effect on disciplinary grounds. The termination letter, dated 13 November, was shared by the Punjab Minister for Transport and Mass Transit, Bilal Akbar Khan, on X. The minister said the Transport Department condemns the conductor's behaviour and apologised for it. Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz also took notice, saying dismissal from service was not sufficient. She directed the police to take further action and said the conductor should be apprehended, taken to the woman's home and made to apologise publicly. Directives have been issued to DIG Faisal Kamran. No FIR or arrest has been publicly reported yet.

What the law says: pushing a person off a bus is assault and criminal force. Section 352 of the Pakistan Penal Code, punishment for assault or criminal force otherwise than on grave provocation, carries imprisonment of up to three months, a fine of up to Rs1,500, or both. Where force is used against a woman with intent to outrage her modesty, Section 354 applies, with imprisonment of up to two years, a fine, or both. Sacking is an employer's disciplinary action, not a punishment under the law. Only a court can punish the offence after a proper trial. The allegations must be proved in court; the accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

What this means for you: if you face such behaviour on public transport, report it at the nearest police station, note the bus number and the company, keep the video as evidence, and file a complaint with the transport company and the District Police Officer. If police refuse to register an FIR, you can approach the District Police Officer, or file an application under Section 22-A of the Code of Criminal Procedure before the Justice of the Peace.
