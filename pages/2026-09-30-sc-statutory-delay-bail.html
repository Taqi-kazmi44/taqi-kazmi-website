Supreme Court: Courts Must Examine Who Caused Trial Delay for Statutory Bail

In Chaudhry Shahzaib Shamim vs the State, a three-member bench granted statutory bail under the third proviso to Section 497(1) CrPC, holding that trial delay caused by the jail's failure to produce the undertrial cannot be attributed to the accused; courts must examine whether the delay was occasioned by an act or omission of the accused or anyone acting on his behalf.

Undertrial for over a year, but the jail never produced him for trial. The Supreme Court has ruled: the system cannot punish you for its own failure.

In Chaudhry Shahzaib Shamim vs the State and another, a three-member bench headed by Justice Jamal Khan Mandokhail (comprising Justice Naeem Akhter Afghan and Justice Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi) granted bail to an accused booked under Section 489-F PPC (dishonoured cheque) in FIR No. 83/2025, Police Station Sihala, Islamabad.

The trial court and the Islamabad High Court had both refused bail, saying the delay was not the prosecution's fault because the accused was never produced from jail. The Supreme Court examined the trial court's diary sheets and found that neither the accused nor his counsel ever sought an adjournment. The trial stood still only because jail authorities failed to produce him despite production warrants.

The Court laid down the rule: for statutory bail under the third proviso to Section 497(1) CrPC, courts must examine whether the delay was occasioned by an act or omission of the accused or anyone acting on his behalf. Delay caused by the system's failure cannot defeat the accused's right to bail.

Aam aadmi ke liye iska matlab: agar aap ek saal se zyada se jail mein hain, trial mukammal nahi hua, aur der ki wajah aap nahi balke jail ya adalti intezamiya hai, to adalat ko zamanat deni hogi. Apne haqooq janiye.
