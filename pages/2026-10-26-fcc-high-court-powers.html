FCC Rules: High Courts Cannot Dictate Officer Postings

Can a court decide where a government officer should be posted? The Federal Constitutional Court has now answered that question, and its answer draws a clear line between judging the law and running the administration.

In a 10-page judgment authored by Justice Rozi Khan Barrech, a two-member bench comprising Justice Barrech and Justice Syed Arshad Hussain Shah set aside a Sindh High Court order of June 30, 2026, concerning police officer Munir Samo. The High Court had directed that Samo be removed from the Special Branch at once, kept in Karachi for one year, barred from operational or field duties, and subjected to disciplinary proceedings.

The Federal Constitutional Court held that judicial review under Article 199 of the Constitution exists to correct unlawful actions and to ensure compliance with the law, not to take over administrative decision-making. The inquiry conducted on the High Court's own directions had not established Samo's involvement in the raid in question, and his role as a group officer was limited to issuing intelligence reports. The inquiry officer had not recommended any action against him at that stage.

The Court stressed that adverse action against a public servant must be supported by the record and the inquiry findings, not by speculation or assumptions. The Constitution rests on a division of powers: the legislature makes laws, the executive implements them, and the judiciary interprets the law. No institution may encroach on the domain assigned to another.

What this means for you: a court can strike down an unlawful order, but it cannot ordinarily write the transfer order itself.
