Can a Person Under 18 Make a Binding Contract in Pakistan?

Many people ask whether an agreement signed by a person under 18 can be enforced. The law is clear: a minor is not competent to contract, so the agreement is void from the very beginning, not merely cancellable later.

The legal rule: Sections 10 and 11, Contract Act, 1872

Section 10 provides that all agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not expressly declared to be void. Section 11 provides that every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject. A minor therefore fails the competence requirement, so no contract comes into existence.

The leading authority: Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903) 30 IA 114

The Privy Council held that an agreement with a minor is void ab initio, that is, void from the very beginning, not merely voidable at the minor's option. It cannot be enforced against the minor, and it does not become valid simply because the minor later turns 18. Age of majority is generally 18 years, or 21 years where a court has appointed a guardian.

The narrow exception: Section 68, Contract Act, 1872

Under Section 68, if a person incapable of entering into a contract, or anyone whom he is legally bound to support, is supplied by another person with necessaries suited to his condition in life, the supplier is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of such incapable person. That is a claim against property for necessaries, not enforcement of the minor's contract itself.

What this means for you

  1. Before signing any agreement, check that both parties are legally competent to contract. An agreement signed by a minor offers no enforceable protection.
