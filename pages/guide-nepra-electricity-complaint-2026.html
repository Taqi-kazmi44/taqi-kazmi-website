How to File a Complaint Against Your Electricity Company (NEPRA)

A wrong electricity bill is not something you have to accept silently. NEPRA, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority, runs a formal complaint system that every consumer in Pakistan can use, free of charge, for problems with their electricity company (DISCO), including K-Electric in Karachi.

What you can complain about

You can raise a complaint for overbilling, a wrong meter reading, estimated bills, voltage fluctuation, repeated outages and faulty lines. As a useful check: NEPRA's tariffs apply to a maximum billing period of 31 days. If your bill charges you for 35 or 37 days in a single month, that is a billing violation you can challenge.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. Collect your evidence. Take a photo of your meter reading, keep copies of the disputed bills, and note the dates and times of outages or faults.
  2. Complain to your electricity company first. Call Helpline 118 (free, available in seven languages) or your DISCO's customer care, and always get a complaint number. The system automatically escalates the complaint if it is not resolved within NEPRA's set timeframe.
  3. If the company does not fix the problem, escalate to NEPRA. Download the "NEPRA Asaan Approach" mobile app, which lets you file complaints about outages, fluctuations, line faults and billing discrepancies with real-time tracking.
  4. You can also file through NEPRA's online complaint management system or approach NEPRA's Consumer Affairs Division.
  5. The whole process is free of charge. Keep your complaint reference number and track it until it is closed.

The governing law

Consumer complaints against distribution companies are governed by the NEPRA Complaint Handling and Dispute Resolution Procedure Rules, 2015, together with the NEPRA Consumer Service Manual, which applies to all consumers served by distribution companies.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: never throw away a wrong bill. Complain first, get a complaint number, and if the company ignores you, NEPRA is one free complaint away.
