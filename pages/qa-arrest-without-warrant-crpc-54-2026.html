Can Police Arrest Without a Warrant? The Answer Is in Section 54 CrPC

One of the most common questions ordinary people ask: can the police arrest someone without a warrant? The short answer is yes, but only in the specific cases listed in the law. Section 54 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 (Pakistan) gives a police officer the power to arrest without an order from a Magistrate and without a warrant, provided one of the listed grounds exists. This page states each ground exactly as the statute does.

What Section 54 CrPC says

A police officer may, without an order from a Magistrate and without a warrant, arrest:

  1. Any person who has been concerned in any cognizable offence, or against whom a reasonable complaint has been made, or credible information has been received, or a reasonable suspicion exists of his having been so concerned. This is the most-used ground: an FIR-level (cognizable) offence is enough; no warrant is needed.
  2. Any person having in his possession, without lawful excuse, any implement of house-breaking; or any person in whose possession anything is found which may reasonably be suspected to be stolen property, and who may reasonably be suspected of having committed an offence with reference to such thing.
  3. Any person who has been proclaimed as an offender under the Code or by order of the Provincial Government; and any person reasonably suspected of being a deserter from the armed forces of Pakistan.
  4. Any person who obstructs a police officer while in the execution of his duty, or who has escaped or attempts to escape from lawful custody; and any released convict committing a breach of any rule made under Section 565(3).
  5. Any person for whose arrest a requisition has been received from another police officer, provided the requisition specifies the person and the offence or cause, and the arrest could lawfully be made without a warrant by the officer who issued the requisition.

The key legal point

  1. A warrant is not needed for every arrest, but the officer must have one of these legal grounds. An arrest made with none of the Section 54 grounds is unlawful and can be challenged before a Magistrate.
  2. What this means for you: if you are ever arrested, ask the officer on what ground the arrest is being made. A lawful arrest needs a legal reason under the statute, not just suspicion.
