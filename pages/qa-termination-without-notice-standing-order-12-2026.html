Can Your Employer Fire You Without Notice in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions workers ask: can the boss simply tell you to leave today, with no notice and no reason? The short answer is no, not if you are a permanent workman. The law requires notice, or pay instead of notice, and a written order that states the reason. This page states the rule exactly as the law states it.

Standing Order 12: one month's notice is mandatory

Standing Order 12(1) of the Industrial and Commercial Employment (Standing Orders) Ordinance, 1968 (Ordinance VI of 1968) provides: "For terminating employment of a permanent workman, for any reason other than misconduct, one month's notice shall be given either by the employer or the workman. One month's wages calculated on the basis of average wages earned by the workman during the last three months shall be paid in lieu of notice." In simple words, an employer who wants to end a permanent workman's job for any reason other than misconduct must either give one month's written notice or pay one month's wages instead. The rule cuts both ways: a permanent workman who resigns must also give one month's notice.

Termination must be in writing, with the reason stated

Standing Order 12(3) adds a second protection: the services of a workman shall not be terminated, nor shall a workman be removed, retrenched, discharged or dismissed from service, except by an order in writing which shall explicitly state the reason for the action taken. A verbal order to leave, or a termination letter with no reason, does not meet this requirement. The Supreme Court has reaffirmed this rule, holding that no workman can be removed without a written order stating the reason.

Who is not covered by the notice rule

Standing Order 12(2) is clear that the notice rule does not apply to everyone. No temporary workman, whether monthly-rated, weekly-rated, daily-rated or piece-rated, and no probationer or badli, is entitled to any notice if the employer terminates their services. The Ordinance itself applies to industrial and commercial establishments where twenty or more workmen are employed. Similar notice provisions exist in the provincial laws, including the Sindh Terms of Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 2015.

What this means for you

If you are a permanent worker and you were dismissed without a month's notice, without a month's pay in lieu of notice, or without a written order stating the reason, the termination does not follow the law, and you can challenge it before the Labour Court. Keep your appointment letter, salary slips and the termination letter, if any, because they are the record on which your claim will rest.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: a permanent workman cannot be dismissed without one month's notice, or one month's wages in lieu of notice, and the termination must be by a written order stating the reason (Standing Order 12, Industrial and Commercial Employment (Standing Orders) Ordinance, 1968).
  2. Temporary workers, probationers and badlis are not entitled to this notice, and misconduct cases follow a separate inquiry procedure under Standing Order 15.
