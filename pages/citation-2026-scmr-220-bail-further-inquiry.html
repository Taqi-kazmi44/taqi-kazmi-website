2026 SCMR 220: Doubtful Recovery Means Bail, Not Custody

Can the police keep you in jail when their own forensic evidence does not add up? The Supreme Court of Pakistan says no: custody needs proof, not suspicion.

In Sahad v. Mst. Afsheen, 2026 SCMR 220, the Supreme Court of Pakistan granted bail in a case where the prosecution's own forensic evidence failed to connect the accused to the crime. The empties recovered from the crime scene did not match the weapon allegedly recovered from the accused, which rendered the recovery doubtful.

The eyewitness account was only a general narration that attributed no specific role to the accused. The Court held that such a case falls within the ambit of further inquiry under section 497(2) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, and allowed bail.

Three principles emerge from the ruling. First, if the forensic evidence does not connect the accused to the crime, the recovery is treated as doubtful. Second, a vague, general statement that assigns no specific role to the accused cannot justify keeping him in custody. Third, where the case needs further inquiry, bail is the rule, not a favour.

What this means for you: nobody can be kept behind bars on a shaky case. If the evidence itself raises questions, the law gives the accused the benefit of bail until trial.

Full reference: Sahad v. Mst. Afsheen, 2026 SCMR 220. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 18 August 2025. Bench: Justice Amin-ud-Din Khan and Justice Syed Hasan Azhar Rizvi.
