LHC Acquits Muhammad Ashraf: FIR Delay and Weak Evidence End a Life Sentence

A life sentence for murder has been set aside. The Lahore High Court held that the prosecution could not explain the delay in registering the FIR, could not prove that witnesses were present at the crime scene, and relied on a late post-mortem examination.

Justice Farooq Haider allowed the appeal of Muhammad Ashraf, setting aside the life sentence and the Rs500,000 fine imposed by the trial court in 2021. The case had been registered by Mandi Bahauddin police in 2019 over the murder of Sikandar Firdous.

Three weaknesses proved fatal to the prosecution's case. First, the prosecution failed to give a convincing explanation for the delay in registering the FIR. Second, it could not establish the presence of its witnesses at the scene of the crime. Third, the post-mortem examination was conducted late, and the police prepared the relevant documents after a delay.

The court also found the complainant's own account doubtful. She said she was travelling on a motorcycle with her son to visit her daughter when Ashraf allegedly signalled them to stop outside his house, took her son inside and shot him dead. The court observed that her daughter's home was situated before the accused's house along the route in the village, which raised questions about the sequence of events she described.

Finding these weaknesses in the prosecution's case, the High Court set aside the conviction and sentence.

What this means for you: in a criminal trial, the prosecution must prove its case beyond reasonable doubt. An unexplained FIR delay, late medical evidence, and doubtful witness presence can each become a ground for acquittal.
