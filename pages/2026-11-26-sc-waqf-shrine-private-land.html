Supreme Court: Shrine on Private Land Is Not Waqf Property

Your land is your land, even if a shrine stands on it. The Supreme Court has said so in clear words.

On Tuesday, 24 November 2026, a two-judge bench of the Supreme Court, headed by Justice Shahid Waheed and comprising Justice Naeem Akhter Afghan, declared that building a shrine over a grave and visits by devotees do not automatically turn private land into waqf property.

The case concerned Darbar Hazrat Syed Shabir Hussain Shah in Hafizabad. The land, five kanals and three marlas, was bought by Muhammad Hussain from Rab Nawaz through a registered sale deed dated 2 March 1988. He built his house and a tube-well on it, and later raised a shrine over the grave of a family member, Syed Shabir Hussain Shah, who was buried there in 1993. On 19 November 2003, the chief administrator of Auqaf, Punjab, issued a notification taking over the administration, control and management of the shrine. The Lahore High Court had decided in 2008 that the land was not waqf property.

The Supreme Court upheld the Lahore High Court and declared the 2003 notification to have no legal effect. The court directed the Auqaf department to restore the administration, control and possession of the land to Muhammad Hussain within 30 days, and the revenue authorities to correct the record of rights. The sums already received by the Auqaf department and credited to the Auqaf Fund will not be reopened.

The court framed three questions: whether the land was waqf property under Section 2(e) of the Punjab Waqf Properties Ordinance, 1979; whether the 2003 notification was issued in conformity with Section 7 of the Ordinance; and whether the Lahore High Court was right in treating the absence of a deed of dedication as decisive. The court held that Article 24(1) of the Constitution provides that no person shall be deprived of his property save in accordance with law, Article 23 guarantees the right to hold property, and Article 4 guarantees the right to be dealt with in accordance with law. Taking over the administration, control and management of land deprives its owner of its use and enjoyment, and such a step is lawful only if the conditions on which the statute grants the power are strictly met. A shrine on privately owned land, even if visited by the public and receiving offerings, does not become waqf property without the owner's consent.

What this means for you: if your family land has a shrine or a grave on it, mere visits by people and offerings in a cash box do not hand your land to Auqaf. The department needs a valid dedication and a lawful process, and you can challenge a takeover in court.
