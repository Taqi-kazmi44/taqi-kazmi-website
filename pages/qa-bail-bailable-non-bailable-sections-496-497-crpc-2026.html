Can Bail Be Granted in Every Criminal Case?

One of the most common criminal-law questions: after an arrest, can the accused always get bail? The law of Pakistan divides offences into two classes, bailable and non-bailable, and the answer depends on which class the FIR's sections fall into.

The legal rule: Sections 496 and 497 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898

Section 496 deals with bailable offences. It says that when any person other than a person accused of a non-bailable offence is arrested or detained without warrant by an officer in charge of a police station, or appears or is brought before a court, and is prepared to give bail, such person shall be released on bail. In other words, in a bailable offence, bail is the accused person's right, not a favour of the police or the court.

Non-bailable offences: bail is the court's discretion

Section 497(1) deals with non-bailable offences. It says that a person accused of a non-bailable offence may be released on bail, but he shall not be so released if there appear reasonable grounds for believing that he has been guilty of an offence punishable with death or imprisonment for life or imprisonment for ten years. The law does, however, allow the court to grant bail to a person under the age of sixteen, a woman, or a sick or infirm person accused of such an offence. So in serious cases bail is the exception, not the rule, and it depends on the court's satisfaction.

Which offences are bailable and which are not?

The Second Schedule of the Code of Criminal Procedure lists every offence and marks it as bailable or non-bailable. That is why the practical question in any case is always the same: which sections are written in the FIR, and how are those sections classified in the Second Schedule? The classification decides whether Section 496 (bail as a right) or Section 497 (bail as the court's discretion) applies.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: the sections written in the FIR decide which bail rule applies to the case, so always check whether the offence is listed as bailable or non-bailable in the Second Schedule of the CrPC. In a bailable offence the police or the court must release the accused on bail once bail is offered; in a non-bailable offence the accused must convince the court.
