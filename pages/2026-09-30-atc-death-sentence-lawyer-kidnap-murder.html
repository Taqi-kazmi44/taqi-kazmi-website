Death Sentence for Kidnap and Murder of Karachi Lawyer

ATC-1 Karachi sentences Awais Ali Hashmi to death on two counts for the 2020 kidnapping and murder of 70-year-old advocate Syed Farhat Ali Askari over a Rs4 crore ransom demand, holding that ransom-murder spreading panic and fear amounts to terrorism under Section 6 of the Anti-Terrorism Act, 1997; three co-accused acquitted as a co-accused's confession to police needs independent corroboration.

Justice for a 70-year-old lawyer, six years after his kidnapping. An Anti-Terrorism Court in Karachi has sentenced the killer to death.

Awais Ali Hashmi abducted advocate Syed Farhat Ali Askari in 2020, demanded Rs4 crore from his wife, and when the ransom was not paid, strangled him and threw his body, in a suitcase, into a nullah in Surjani Town.

The court held that kidnapping for ransom followed by murder, which spreads panic and fear among the public and the legal fraternity, squarely falls within "terrorism" under Section 6 of the Anti-Terrorism Act, 1997. He was sentenced to hang till death on two counts with a total fine of Rs500,000.

Three co-accused (Faizan, Iftikhar and Saeed) were acquitted. The court reiterated an important rule of evidence: a co-accused's confessional statement to the police cannot be used as substantive evidence against others; it needs independent corroboration.

The case was registered at Shahrah-i-Noor Jahan police station under Sections 302 (murder), 365-A (kidnapping for ransom) and 34 of the Pakistan Penal Code, read with Section 7 of the ATA, 1997.
