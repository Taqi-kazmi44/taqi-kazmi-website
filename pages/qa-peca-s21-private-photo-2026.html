Is Sharing Someone's Private Photo or Video Without Consent a Crime in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask: can someone post your private photo or video online without your permission? The short answer is yes, it is a crime. Section 21 of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 makes it an offence to share a person's sexually explicit photo or video, or to intimidate or blackmail them with such material. This page states the law exactly as the Act states it.

What Section 21 of PECA, 2016 says

Section 21, headed Offences against Modesty of a Natural Person and Minor, provides that whoever intentionally and publicly exhibits or displays or transmits any information which superimposes a photograph of a natural person's face over any sexually explicit image or video, includes a photograph or video of a natural person in sexually explicit conduct, intimidates a natural person with any sexual act or any sexually explicit image or video, or cultivates, entices or induces a natural person to engage in a sexually explicit act, through an information system, to harm a natural person or his reputation, or to take revenge, or to create hatred or to blackmail, shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years or with fine which may extend to five million rupees or with both.

The punishment is higher where the victim is a minor

Where the victim is a minor, the punishment under Section 21 extends to imprisonment for up to seven years. The section is aimed at the non-consensual sharing of intimate images and at blackmail with such material, including morphed or fake intimate images used to threaten a person.

How the courts have applied it: 2023 YLR 1377

The Peshawar High Court dealt with Section 21 in Naveed v. The State, 2023 YLR 1377, where the accused had threatened and blackmailed a woman with her pictures over WhatsApp. The trial court convicted him under Sections 21 and 24 of PECA and sentenced him to three years' rigorous imprisonment under Section 21 with a fine. The High Court examined the forensic evidence from the phone on which the pictures were received. The case shows that a threat or blackmail message backed by private pictures is treated as a Section 21 offence, not as a private dispute.

The victim can also ask for the content to be removed

Under Section 21(3) of the Act, the aggrieved person may apply to the Authority for removal, destruction of or blocking access to such information. This remedy runs alongside the criminal case, so a victim does not have to wait for a conviction before asking for the photo or video to be taken down. Complaints under PECA are investigated by the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA).

An important limit: not every photo is a Section 21 offence

Section 21 covers sexually explicit images and videos, and intimidation or blackmail with such material. An ordinary photograph shared without consent is not automatically an offence under Section 21. Where the shared material is false and damages a person's reputation, Section 20 of PECA, which covers offences against the dignity of a natural person, may apply instead. The exact section depends on what was shared and why.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: a private photo or video shared without your consent is not a joke or a prank. It is a criminal offence under Section 21 of PECA, 2016, and you can seek both removal of the content and punishment of the person who shared it.
  2. Save the messages, screenshots and links as evidence, do not delete them, and report the matter to the NCCIA. Never pay a blackmailer: paying does not make the material go away.
