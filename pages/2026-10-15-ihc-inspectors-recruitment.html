IHC Makes Islamabad Police Inspector Recruitment Subject to Court Decision

Direct recruitment of police inspectors in Islamabad is now legally uncertain. The Islamabad High Court has ordered that every step of the ongoing direct recruitment of inspectors in the Islamabad Capital Territory Police will remain subject to the court's final decision on whether such recruitment is lawful at all.

The petition was filed by Arshad Ali and 39 other serving sub-inspectors of the ICT Police. They challenge S.R.O. 737(I)/2026 dated 29 April 2026, issued by the Ministry of Interior and Narcotics Control, and the consequent advertisement dated 11 August 2026 issued by the Federal Public Service Commission, both concerning initial recruitment to the ordinary rank of Inspector in the ICT Police.

Their counsel, Barrister Adil Aziz Qazi, argued that direct recruitment to the rank of Inspector violates Article 7(3) of the Police Order, 2002, which does not provide for direct recruitment to that rank. He submitted that the Police Order, 2002 is effective and operative in the ICT, as the IHC itself held in Tasleema Bibi v. SSP Islamabad (PLD 2019 Islamabad 524). He further relied on a 1 November 2022 judgment in WP No.3403/2017 (Taimoor Ali Khan, etc. v. Federation of Pakistan, etc.) directing effective implementation of the Police Order, 2002.

Justice Ayyaz Shaukat issued notices to the Interior Secretary, the Inspector General of Police, the Additional Inspector General, the Chief Commissioner Islamabad, the Deputy Secretary (Interior) and the Public Service Commission, directing report and para-wise comments within two weeks. Notices were also ordered for the Attorney General for Pakistan and the Advocate General, Islamabad.

The court fixed the next hearing for 28 October 2026 and directed that any steps taken in the recruitment process shall be subject to the final outcome of the petition.

What this means for you: if you applied for direct inspector recruitment in the ICT Police, the process continues for now, but any appointment made can be undone if the court strikes the process down. Watch the 28 October hearing closely.
