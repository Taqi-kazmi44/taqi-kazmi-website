2026 SCMR 60

A delayed postmortem turned an entire murder case upside down.

A delayed postmortem turned an entire murder case upside down.
The Supreme Court treated the delay itself as evidence of a deeper problem.

In Mushtaq Ahmed vs The State, 2026 SCMR 60, the Court held that an unexplained delay in the postmortem suggests the incident was not witnessed at all, and that the time was used to prepare false witnesses. All the witnesses were relatives of the complainant. There were material contradictions on motive. And a conviction could not be maintained on the very evidence on which the co-accused had already been acquitted.

For law students, the learning points are clear: the effect of delay in postmortem, the distinction between interested and independent witnesses, material contradictions on motive, and the benefit of a co-accused's acquittal. For citizens, the principle is simple: failures in investigation ultimately go in favour of the accused, and that is what justice demands.

Case: Mushtaq Ahmed vs The State, 2026 SCMR 60. Supreme Court of Pakistan, decided 18 June 2025. Bench: Athar Minallah, Irfan Saadat Khan and Malik Shahzad Ahmad Khan, JJ.
