LHC Notices on Plea Against Camera-Equipped Smart Glasses

Lahore High Court issues notices to the federal government, PTA and commerce ministry on a public interest petition challenging the unregulated import and sale of camera and mic-equipped smart glasses, citing privacy, dignity and national security under Articles 9 and 14 of the Constitution; replies due by October 8.

Someone wearing ordinary glasses could be secretly recording you right now. The Lahore High Court has just stepped into this.

On Monday, Chief Justice Aalia Neelum issued notices to the federal government, the PTA, the commerce ministry and other authorities on a public interest petition challenging the unregulated import and sale of camera and mic-equipped smart glasses in Pakistan. The petition was filed by citizen Husnainul Abideen through Advocate Shahram Saqi.

The case as pleaded:
1. Smart glasses like Ray-Ban Meta look like ordinary eyewear but can secretly photograph, record and even live-stream anyone within the wearer's view.
2. This makes them fundamentally different from a mobile phone, and violates Articles 9 and 14 of the Constitution, which protect liberty, dignity and privacy.
3. Though not officially marketed in Pakistan, the glasses are openly sold online and in Lahore shops, including Hafeez Centre.
4. The counsel cited regulatory concerns in Ireland, Italy and Germany, and bans on such glasses in courts in England and Wales and New York.

The petition seeks a declaration that unrestricted use of these glasses is unconstitutional, plus directions to prohibit imports, review PTA approvals, bar the glasses in courts, airports and government offices, stop customs clearance of consignments, and make the cybercrime agency disclose related complaints. Replies are due by October 8.

Aam aadmi ke liye iska matlab: technology qanoon se tez badal rahi hai, aur aapki privacy Articles 9 aur 14 ke tehet qanooni haq hai. Agar koi aapki razamandi ke baghair aapki recording kare, to qanoon aapke saath hai. Apne haqooq janiye.
