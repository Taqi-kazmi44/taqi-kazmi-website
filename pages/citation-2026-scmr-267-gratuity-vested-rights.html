2026 SCMR 267: Gratuity, Provident Fund and Pension Are Vested Rights

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has held that gratuity, provident fund and pension are vested rights of employees. An employer cannot withhold them by pleading financial difficulty. This page breaks down the judgment.

The judgment, step by step

  1. The case is M. Asghar Janjua v. Federation of Pakistan, Civil Petitions for Leave to Appeal Nos. 573-K to 575-K of 2023.
  2. The Supreme Court decided it on 2 September 2025, per Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Athar Minallah and Salahuddin Panhwar, JJ.
  3. The holding: gratuity, provident fund and pension are vested rights of employees. Once earned through service, they cannot be taken away at the employer's discretion.
  4. The case concerned employees of Pakistan Steel Mills. The employer's financial-crunch plea was rejected as a ground to withhold retirement benefits.
  5. What this means for you: your retirement benefits are your legal right, not a favor; no financial-crisis excuse can erase them.

The key legal points

  1. Vested-rights principle: gratuity, provident fund and pension belong to the employee by right of service.
  2. Full reference: 2026 SCMR 267; M. Asghar Janjua v. Federation of Pakistan; C.P.L.As. 573-K to 575-K of 2023; decided 2 September 2025; Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Athar Minallah and Salahuddin Panhwar, JJ.
  3. The financial-difficulty defence does not defeat vested retirement benefits.
