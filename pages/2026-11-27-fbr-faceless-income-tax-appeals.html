FBR Notifies Faceless Income Tax Appeals Rules (SRO 2276(I)/2026)

Your tax appeal will soon be decided by an officer you will never see, meet or even name.

The Federal Board of Revenue has notified SRO 2276(I)/2026 on 24 November 2026, inserting Chapter XIXA, Rules 245A to 245M, into the Income Tax Rules, 2002. The rules cover first appeals under Section 127 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, processed through the Centre under Section 129A. A draft was published as SRO 2136(I)/2026 on 18 November 2026, six days before the final notification.

Under the new system, appeals will be filed through the FBR IRIS portal and given a unique identification number. Each appeal will be assigned to an Appellate Unit or Commissioner (Appeals) by a random, dynamic, automated algorithm. The identity of the Commissioner and the unit will be hidden from the taxpayer, the taxpayer's representative and the department, including in the final order.

There will be no in-person appearance. Hearings will be held by video conference or a secure digital link and will be recorded and archived. The Commissioner's face will be hidden and voice electronically altered. A taxpayer may choose a decision on written submissions alone, and taxpayers without internet access can join hearings from dedicated facilities at the nearest Field Operations Wing unit.

Every order, notice and direction will carry a Digital Identification Number and will be authenticated electronically, without the Commissioner's name, designation or signature. Final orders must state the amount of tax upheld. The orders are exempt from quality control review under Rule 242, and mistakes apparent from the record can be corrected electronically under Section 221.

The rules also insulate the deciding officer. An official who worked on the audit, assessment or quality control of a case, or who passed the order under appeal, cannot hear it. The Director General, the Chief Commissioner and other Centre authorities are barred from directing Commissioners on how to hear or decide appeals. A Commissioner with a personal or financial interest in the case must declare it electronically and withdraw.

Applications for stay of recovery, condonation of delay, additional evidence and new grounds of appeal will also be handled electronically. Before deciding a stay, the Commissioner must hear the respondent electronically, and the order will go to all parties the same day.

One important point: the rules are notified but not yet in force. They will take effect from a date the FBR announces in the Gazette, and different categories of appeal can start on different dates. The FBR has not yet announced the commencement date or which categories will move first.

The same faceless framework already exists for sales tax and federal excise duty appeals, through SRO 2134(I)/2026 of 18 November 2026. A tax litigation task force counted about 85,480 tax cases pending as of April 2026, including 66,747 before tribunals, 17,410 in the High Courts and 1,322 in the Supreme Court. These rules do not reach those pending cases.

What this means for you: if you file an income tax appeal after the system starts, you will argue it online, you will not know who decides it, and the order you receive will carry only a number. The appeal can still be taken further to the Appellate Tribunal under Section 131.
