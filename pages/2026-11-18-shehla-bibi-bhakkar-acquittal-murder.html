Bhakkar: Woman Shot Dead With Father Hours After Court Acquittal

A court acquitted her. On her way home, she was shot dead with her father. Shehla Bibi was acquitted of murder charges in Bhakkar district on 17 November 2026, and opponents allegedly opened fire on her family near Cinema Morr as they returned home.

According to Dawn, Shehla Bibi was accused in a murder case reported to the Darya Khan police in Bhakkar district, from Basti Sial Nasheb, Lak Kalan. On Tuesday, 17 November 2026, a court acquitted her of the murder charges. She was returning home with her father and brother on a motorcycle when their opponents allegedly opened fire on them near Cinema Morr.

Action taken so far: Shehla Bibi and her father died on the spot, and her brother was seriously injured. A police team shifted the bodies and the injured youth to hospital. The police have detained one of the suspects and are investigating the matter. The sections under which the police have registered this case have not been publicly reported.

What the law says: an acquittal means the court found the accused not guilty. Being attacked after an acquittal is not justice, it is revenge, and the law treats it as a fresh crime. A killing like this attracts Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code, which deals with qatl-i-amd and carries the punishment of death or imprisonment for life. An attack that injures a person attracts Section 324, attempt to commit qatl-i-amd, punishable with imprisonment of up to ten years. These are the provisions that apply to such an attack; the exact sections in the FIR will be confirmed when the police record is made public. The allegations must be proved in court; the accused are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

What this means for you: the family of the victims can pursue the case as complainants, engage a lawyer to follow the investigation and the trial, and ask the District Police Officer to ensure progress and protection. If the police fail to act, the family can approach the Justice of Peace under Section 22-A of the Code of Criminal Procedure. No family should have to fear for its life after a court has spoken in its favour.
