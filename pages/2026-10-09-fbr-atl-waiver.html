FBR Draft: Late Filers Can Skip the Rs25,000 ATL Surcharge With a 6-Month Property Freeze

FBR's draft S.R.O. 1691(I)/2026 (6 Oct 2026) proposes that late income tax return filers can join the Active Taxpayers' List without the Rs25,000 surcharge if they submit Form ATL-U on IRIS agreeing not to buy property for six months. Breach withdraws the relief from the breach date; ATL status then follows Rule 81B(2A). Still a draft, not law yet. Verified (Daily Pakistan / Business Recorder, 7 Oct 2026).

The Federal Board of Revenue issued draft amendments to the Income Tax Rules, 2002 through S.R.O. 1691(I)/2026 on 6 October 2026. Under the proposal, an individual who files the latest tax year's return after the due date (or extended due date) can be included in the Active Taxpayers' List without paying the Rs25,000 surcharge, provided he submits an undertaking in the new Form ATL-U (to be added as Part XV of the First Schedule) through IRIS.

The undertaking declares that for six months from the date shown on the IRIS electronic acknowledgement, the taxpayer will not purchase, acquire or otherwise obtain ownership or beneficial interest in any property. FBR may verify compliance using information from property registries, government agencies, financial institutions and other lawful sources. If a breach is established, after giving the taxpayer an electronic opportunity to explain, the surcharge-free relief stands withdrawn from the date of breach, and the taxpayer's ATL status will then be governed by Rule 81B(2A), the existing rule for late filers.

Important: this is still a DRAFT, not law yet. FBR has given stakeholders seven days from 6 October to submit objections or suggestions before the proposal is finalized. Alongside it, FBR issued S.R.O. 1690(I)/2026 (procedure for electronic scrutiny and intimation of issues detected by the automated system) and S.R.O. 1689(I)/2026 (income tax return form for non-resident ship owners, charterers or authorised shipping agents).
