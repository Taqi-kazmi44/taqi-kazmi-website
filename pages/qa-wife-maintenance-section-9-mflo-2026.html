Can a Wife Claim Maintenance From Her Husband in Pakistan?

One of the most common questions people ask: can a wife claim maintenance (kharcha) from her husband? The short answer is yes. Section 9 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961, gives a wife a clear legal route when her husband fails to maintain her adequately, and she does not have to wait for divorce to use it. This page states the rule exactly as the statute does.

What Section 9 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961, says

Section 9 (Maintenance) provides that if any husband fails to maintain his wife adequately, or where there are more wives than one, fails to maintain them equitably, the wife, or all or any of the wives, may in addition to seeking any other legal remedy available, apply to the Chairman who shall constitute an Arbitration Council to determine the matter, and the Arbitration Council may issue a certificate specifying the amount which shall be paid as maintenance by the husband.

Two routes: Arbitration Council or Family Court

The wife has two routes. First, the Section 9 route: apply to the Chairman of the Union Council, who forms an Arbitration Council that fixes the maintenance amount. Second, she may file a suit for maintenance directly in the Family Court, which has exclusive jurisdiction over maintenance claims under Section 5 and Part I of the Schedule to the Family Courts Act, 1964. Either decision may be challenged only as the law prescribes: the Section 9 certificate may be revised by the Collector concerned, whose decision is final.

Recovery: unpaid maintenance is recoverable as land revenue

Under Section 9(3) of the Ordinance, any amount payable as maintenance, if not paid in due time, is recoverable as arrears of land revenue. The right is not limited to future maintenance: the courts have held that a wife cannot be deprived of past maintenance otherwise permissible to her (PLJ 1991 Lahore 101).

Two years without maintenance is a ground for dissolution

If a husband neglects or fails to provide for his wife's maintenance for a period of two years, that is itself a ground on which a wife may seek dissolution of her marriage under Section 2(ii) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939.

The key legal point

  1. Maintenance is a legal right, not a favour. A wife does not have to wait for divorce to claim it.
  2. Apply to the Chairman of the Union Council under Section 9 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961, or file a suit for maintenance in the Family Court.
