How to File a Complaint with the Federal Tax Ombudsman in Pakistan

If a tax officer delays your refund, harasses you with repeated notices, or passes an order that is arbitrary or contrary to law, you do not have to accept it as the cost of dealing with the tax department. Pakistan has a free, independent forum for this problem: the Federal Tax Ombudsman.

Step by step

  1. Identify the maladministration. This covers a decision, act or omission that is contrary to law or established procedure, perverse, arbitrary, unjust or biased, or based on corrupt or improper motives. It also covers neglect, delay and inefficiency, repeated notices, wilful errors in refunds, and deliberate withholding of a refund already determined.
  2. Check that the Forum can hear your case. It cannot investigate a matter already sub judice before a court, tribunal or board, or a matter about assessment, tax liability, classification or valuation, or interpretation of tax law, where an appeal, review or revision is available. If your remedy is an appeal, use the appeal first.
  3. Write your complaint on solemn affirmation or oath, addressed to the Federal Tax Ombudsman, as the aggrieved person or through your legal representative. Anonymous complaints are not entertained, so sign it with your full particulars.
  4. File within six months from the day you first had notice of the matter. A late complaint is entertained only if special circumstances make it proper in the interest of justice, so do not rely on an extension.
  5. Lodge it in person at the Ombudsman's office, hand it to the Ombudsman in person, or send it to the office by any other means of communication. Attach the tax order or notice, your refund record, and your correspondence with the department.
  6. The Ombudsman issues notice to the Secretary of the Revenue Division and to the officer complained against, calling for a reply. The investigation is conducted in private.
  7. If maladministration is found, the Ombudsman communicates the finding with a recommendation to the Revenue Division within sixty days of receiving the complaint. If you or the Revenue Division are aggrieved by a recommendation, a representation can be made to the President within thirty days.

Reference

The Establishment of the Office of Federal Tax Ombudsman Ordinance, 2000 (XXXV of 2000), sections 2(3), 9, 10, 11 and 32. Official website: https://fto.gov.pk/aboutfto.aspx. Full text of the Ordinance: http://nasirlawsite.com/laws/fto.htm

What this means for you: a delayed refund or an unfair tax action is not something you must simply endure. One written, sworn complaint within six months can put an independent forum on your side, free of cost.
