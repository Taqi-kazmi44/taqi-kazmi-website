SC: Distant Relatives' DNA Cannot Deny Paternity or Inheritance

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has held that a claim to a biological father's paternity or inheritance cannot be rejected solely on DNA tests from indirect or distant relatives. Without a direct sample, such tests carry low evidentiary weight, and under Article 128 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984, birth during a valid marriage is conclusive proof of legitimacy.

A three-member bench of the Supreme Court (Justice Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Justice Irfan Saadat Khan and Justice Shahid Bilal Hassan) upheld the Peshawar High Court's Abbottabad bench and dismissed the petition of Barkat Khan and others, who claimed the plaintiffs were not the real heirs of Muhammad Ayub Khan (son of Arsala Khan, died 10 October 2006).

The plaintiffs (Banaras Khan and others) filed their suit on 14 December 2007, claiming to be the legal heirs of Muhammad Ayub Khan. The defendants, who joined the case on 15 March 2008, alleged the plaintiffs were in fact the offspring of Faris Khan (son of Haibat Khan). The trial court dismissed the suit on 13 July 2011, the appellate court reversed that decision on 11 December 2012, and the Peshawar High Court dismissed the revision on 22 February 2021.

The petitioners relied on a DNA test conducted on extended family members, arguing it disproved the plaintiffs' lineage. The court rejected that argument: a claim to a biological father's paternity or inheritance cannot be dismissed solely on DNA tests from indirect or distant relatives. Without a direct sample from the deceased father, the report lacks conclusive weight and offers only statistical probabilities, not definitive proof.

The court invoked Article 128 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984: a child's birth during a valid marriage is conclusive proof of legitimacy. The documentary record supported the plaintiffs throughout: NADRA records, the Union Council Dhamtour death certificate, grave photographs and marriage agreements all consistently listed Arsala Khan as the father of Muhammad Ayub Khan.
