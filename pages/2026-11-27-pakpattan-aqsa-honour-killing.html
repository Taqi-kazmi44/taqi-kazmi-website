Pakpattan: Father Allegedly Strangles Teenage Daughter in Honour Killing

A teenage girl was allegedly killed by her own father while she slept, because she wanted to marry a man of her choice.

Aqsa, a teenage girl and the eldest of three children of Naseer Ahmed and Allah Mafi, was allegedly strangled at Mauza Kot Kanoongo in Pakpattan district on Wednesday night, 25 November 2026. According to police, her father allegedly placed a pillow over her face and strangled her while she was asleep. She died on the spot.

Her mother, Allah Mafi, was present in the house, having arrived on 25 November to visit her children. She told police she woke up after hearing her daughter's screams and filed the complaint. The suspect fled after the incident and was arrested after a two-hour search operation, District Police Officer Javed Chadhar told Dawn. Rang Shah Station House Officer Imran reached the scene with a police team, collected forensic evidence and shifted the body to Pakpattan District Headquarters Hospital for a post-mortem examination. Police have registered a murder case on the complaint of the victim's mother.

Police say Aqsa had reportedly developed a relationship with Usman Kharal, a resident of Chak 103/D, and had expressed her desire to marry him. Her father opposed the proposal. About two weeks ago, Aqsa allegedly left home with Usman. After negotiations between the two families through a village panchayat, she was brought back home on assurances that the couple would be allowed to marry. She reportedly stayed with Usman for two to three days. Police say her father remained suspicious of the relationship.

The parents had separated a few months ago after domestic disputes. Naseer had reportedly pronounced talaq twice, while the third divorce was yet to take effect. The children were living with their father.

What the law says: murder is punishable with death or imprisonment for life under Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code. Since the 2016 amendment to Section 311, an honour killing cannot be compounded away by the victim's family waiving qisas. The state must prosecute. The FIR sections in this case have not been published, so no section number is stated here as the charge.

What this means for you: if a woman or girl in your family faces threats over a marriage choice, report it at once to the nearest police station or call police helpline 15, and insist on an FIR. If the police refuse to register or investigate, complain to the District Police Officer or approach the Justice of the Peace under Section 22-A of the Code of Criminal Procedure. The Punjab Women Protection helpline is 1043, and shelters and Violence Against Women Centres can provide protection and support.
