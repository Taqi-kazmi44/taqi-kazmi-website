Islamabad: 13-Year-Old Domestic Worker Fatima Dies After Alleged Torture at Senior Officer's House

A 13-year-old domestic worker, Fatima Asghar, allegedly died after torture at the residence of a senior officer in Islamabad's Police Lines Headquarters. A murder case has been registered, and the officer's wife and a religious teacher have been arrested.

According to The News and ARY News, the incident took place at the residence of a senior officer within the Police Lines Headquarters complex in Islamabad. Fatima Asghar, 13, from Rajanpur, worked there as a domestic worker. She was shifted to Quaid-e-Azam Hospital in critical condition and died during treatment. An initial examination reportedly found marks of violence on her body.

Action taken so far: a murder case has been registered at Shams Colony police station on the complaint of the girl's father, Asghar. Police have arrested the officer's wife and a religious teacher who visited the house to provide religious education to the children. The officer's wife is expected to be produced before a court for remand. Reports say the officer is Muhammad Bin Ashraf, Director of the FIA Gujranwala Zone, who has been directed to report to FIA Headquarters. Police sources have also alleged that efforts were made to persuade the girl's parents not to pursue legal action. The officer has told Geo News that the girl had lived with the family for a considerable time and that the family itself contacted the police. The exact FIR sections have not been publicly reported.

What the law says: murder under Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code carries death or imprisonment for life. Cruelty to a child is an offence under Section 328A of the Code. A 13-year-old should never have been working as a domestic worker at all: Article 11(3) of the Constitution bars the employment of children below 14 in hazardous work, and Pakistan's child protection laws treat the employment of young children for domestic work as child labour. A transfer or a direction to report to headquarters is an administrative step, not a punishment under the law. Only a court can punish an offence after a proper trial. The allegations must be proved in court; the accused are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

What this means for you: if a child domestic worker is being abused, call police helpline 15 at once and get an FIR registered at the local police station. If the police refuse to register an FIR, you can file an application under Section 22-A of the Code of Criminal Procedure before the Justice of the Peace, or complain to the District Police Officer or the Inspector General of Police. Keep any medical record and evidence safe, and do not let anyone pressure the family into a compromise.
