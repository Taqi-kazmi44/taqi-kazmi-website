2026 SCMR 494: The Wife Keeps the Choice Between Gold or Cash Dower

A two-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Justice Amin-ud-Din Khan and Justice Syed Hasan Azhar Rizvi, held in Shafqat Ali vs Mst. Zaib Un Nisa and others, reported as 2026 SCMR 494 (Civil Petition No. 74 of 2025, decided 20 August 2025), that where a family court decree affords alternate modes of satisfying dower, the decree-holder retains the liberty to exercise her choice. If the gold ornaments cannot be returned, the executing court may direct payment of their current market value. The Court must give effect to the true intent underlying the decree and the contract from which it flew.

What was decided

  1. The family court had decreed the wife's dower in alternate modes: seven tolas of gold, or, in the alternative, Rs.100,000. The Supreme Court held that this alternate decree left the choice squarely with the decree-holder wife. Neither the husband nor the executing court could pick the cheaper option on her behalf.
  2. The Court ruled that where the actual gold ornaments cannot be returned, the executing court has the power to direct payment of the current market value of the gold ornaments. This protects the wife against inflation eroding a dower fixed years earlier at a nominal figure.
  3. The judgment lays down a guiding principle of execution: the Court must give effect to the true intent underlying the decree and the contract from which it flew. A decree offering alternate satisfaction must be read to benefit the decree-holder's choice, not to give the judgment-debtor a windfall.

The key legal point

  1. For ordinary people: if your nikahnama or court decree fixes dower as gold ornaments with a cash alternative, the choice is yours. No one can force you to accept an old, outdated cash figure when the gold is worth far more today. The value you receive must reflect current market reality.
  2. For law students, this case is essential reading on execution of alternate decrees, the decree-holder's liberty of choice, and the executing court's power to substitute present market value where specific restitution is impossible.
  3. Practical note: always preserve your nikahnama and the family court decree. The written record of the alternate dower modes is what makes this protection enforceable; oral claims about dower terms rarely survive in execution proceedings.
