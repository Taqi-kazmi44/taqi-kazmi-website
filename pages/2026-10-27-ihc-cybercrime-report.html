IHC Directs PTA, PEMRA and NCCIA to Report on Action Against Cybercrime

Is anyone actually doing anything about online harassment? The Islamabad High Court has now demanded answers from the regulators, and the compliance figures placed before the court show a very uneven picture.

The Islamabad High Court has directed the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA), the Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority (PEMRA) and the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) to submit a comprehensive report on the measures taken to combat cybercrime. Justice Arbab Muhammad Tahir issued the directions in a written order on a petition filed by Dr Raana Ahsan against the federation and others.

The court also directed the Interior Secretary to convene a coordination meeting of the relevant institutions, citing public concerns over online safety, misuse of digital and broadcast media, and enforcement of laws protecting citizens against online harm.

Reviewing the PTA report, the court noted varying compliance rates with requests to block unlawful content. Compliance stood at 40.38 percent in defamation and impersonation cases, 59.01 percent in matters concerning national security and defence, and 98.91 percent in cases involving decency and morality. Platform-wise compliance ranged from 48.42 percent on X to 88.68 percent on TikTok.

The PTA told the court that social media companies had not responded affirmatively to requests to meet registration requirements and appoint local compliance and grievance officers. The court questioned whether existing statutory powers under the Pakistan Telecommunication (Re-organisation) Act, 1996, and the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016, were being effectively exercised, and called for effective complaint handling.

What this means for you: online harassment, blackmailing and the unauthorised sharing of private content are offences under the law, and the court is now pressing the regulators to show what they are actually doing about complaints.
