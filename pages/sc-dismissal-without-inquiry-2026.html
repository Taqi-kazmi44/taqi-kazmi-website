2026 SCMR 576: No Dismissal Without a Regular Inquiry

A two-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Justice Ayesha A. Malik and Justice Aqeel Ahmed Abbasi, held in The Senior General Manager (CEO), Pakistan Railways, Railway HQ, Lahore vs Syed Qaiser Abbas (Civil Petition No. 2761 of 2023), reported as 2026 SCMR 576 and decided on 18 November 2025, that a major penalty of dismissal from service cannot be awarded without a regular inquiry or an opportunity of being heard. Doing so violates natural justice.

What was decided

  1. The Supreme Court held that a regular inquiry is required before awarding any major or minor penalty in departmental proceedings. A dismissal order passed without that inquiry, and without giving the employee a chance to be heard, is a violation of natural justice and cannot stand.
  2. The inquiry may be dispensed with only where sufficient reasons for doing so are disclosed in writing. A silent or unexplained shortcut around the inquiry is not permitted.
  3. The Court read the fair trial and due process guarantee of Article 10-A of the Constitution into every service statute: the right to be heard is not a departmental favour, it is a constitutional right.
  4. In this case the Service Tribunal had already converted the respondent's dismissal into compulsory retirement, and the Supreme Court left that order undisturbed.

The key legal point

  1. For employees, the rule is simple and powerful: no one can be dismissed, or given any other major penalty, without a proper inquiry and a fair hearing. A paper inquiry with no real opportunity to defend yourself is the same as no inquiry at all.
  2. For departments and employers, the message is procedural discipline: record the charge, hold the inquiry, give the employee a hearing, and record written reasons if the inquiry is dispensed with. Shortcuts turn a valid penalty into an illegal one.
  3. For law students, the case is essential reading on the scope of Article 10-A in service jurisprudence, the doctrine of natural justice in departmental proceedings, and the legal effect of dispensing with an inquiry.
