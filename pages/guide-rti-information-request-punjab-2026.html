How to File an RTI Request in Punjab: A Step-by-Step Guide

Want to see a government department's records, a budget file, or the list of officers posted at an office? Under Article 19A of the Constitution and the Punjab Transparency and Right to Information Act 2013, getting that information is your right, not a favour. You do not need to give any reason, and there is no fee for filing the request.

The step-by-step procedure

  1. Identify the Public Information Officer (PIO). Every public body must designate PIOs for its administrative units and offices, and must proactively publish each PIO's name, designation and contact details (Section 7 and Section 4(l)). Look for the RTI or information page on the department's website.
  2. Write your request on plain paper or the prescribed information request form. Describe the information clearly enough that the officer can locate it. You do not have to state any reason for asking (Section 10(1), (2) and (3)). If you are disabled or cannot write, the PIO must reasonably assist you in making the request (Section 10(4)).
  3. Submit the request to the PIO and take a written receipt. If you accidentally give the application to the wrong officer, the law requires it to be transferred immediately to the concerned PIO (Section 11).
  4. Get your answer within 14 working days. The department must respond as soon as possible and in any case within fourteen working days; for very large or complex requests it may add a maximum of fourteen more working days. Where the information concerns the life or liberty of a person, it must be given within 2 working days (Section 10(7)).
  5. There is no fee for filing the request itself. You pay only the Commission's prescribed reproduction or postage costs, if any copies need to be made or posted (Section 10(6)).
  6. If the PIO does not reply or refuses your request, you have two remedies. Seek an internal review by the head of the public body within 60 days of the decision (Section 12), or complain to the Punjab Information Commission on grounds including wrongful denial, non-provision within time, refusal to receive or process the request, false or incomplete information, excess fee, or destruction of records (Section 2(d)); the Commission decides complaints within 30 days (Section 6(2)).

The governing law

The procedure is governed by the Punjab Transparency and Right to Information Act 2013 (Act XXV of 2013), read with Article 19A of the Constitution of Pakistan. Section 3 gives every applicant the right to information in the prescribed manner, and an applicant includes a citizen of Pakistan or a legal person registered or incorporated in Pakistan (Section 2(a)). Section 10 is the core: application on a form or plain paper, acknowledgement of receipt, no reasons required, assistance for those who cannot write, response within fourteen working days (two working days for life or liberty cases), and no fee beyond reproduction or sending costs. Section 11 covers transfer of misdirected applications. Section 12 provides internal review within 60 days of the PIO's decision, decided within 14 days. Section 13 lists the exceptions (national security, privacy, legal privilege, commercial and third-party interests, safety of persons, crime prevention, economic management, policy formulation), and Section 13(5) allows disclosure of information over 50 years old. Section 15 provides a fine of up to two days' salary per day of delay or up to Rs.50,000 against a defaulting officer, and Section 16 makes deliberate destruction of requested records punishable with up to two years' imprisonment or fine.

The key legal point

  1. What this means for you: you do not have to plead or explain. One clear written request starts a legal clock, and if the department stays silent, the law gives you an internal review and the Punjab Information Commission. Government records are your right, not a favour.
