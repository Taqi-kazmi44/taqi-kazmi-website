2026 SCMR 171: Unregistered Rent Agreement Is a Curable Defect

A three-member bench of the Supreme Court of Pakistan, comprising Yahya Afridi, CJ, Muhammad Shafi Siddiqui and Miangul Hassan Aurangzeb, JJ, held in Mrs. Tasneem Abbas vs Additional District Judge, Lahore (C.A. No. 132-L of 2013), reported as 2026 SCMR 171 and decided on 10 September 2025, that non-registration of a tenancy with the rent registrar is a curable defect. The petitioner must be given a chance to pay the fine and register, the case cannot be thrown out on this ground alone.

What was decided

  1. Non-registration of a tenancy with the rent registrar is a curable defect, not a fatal one. The petitioner seeking eviction must be given an opportunity to make up the default on payment of the prescribed fine; the eviction petition cannot be dismissed merely because the tenancy was not registered (Sections 5 and 9, Punjab Rented Premises Act 2007).
  2. A tenant's pending declaration suit claiming ownership of the property is no ground to block or stay an eviction petition. Only a decree obtained in the ownership suit changes the picture.
  3. In a memorable line, the Court warned that comparing handwriting oneself, without an expert, is dangerous and unreliable (Article 84, Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984).

The key legal point

  1. For landlords, the message is practical: write the rent agreement and get it registered with the rent registrar. That registration is your real protection in any eviction dispute.
  2. For tenants, the message is equally clear: a technical defect like non-registration does not end the case, but a mere claim of ownership in a pending suit does not stop eviction either.
  3. For law students, the case is essential reading on the curable-defect doctrine in tenancy law, the distinction between a declaration suit and a decree, and Article 84's rule on handwriting comparison by the court.
