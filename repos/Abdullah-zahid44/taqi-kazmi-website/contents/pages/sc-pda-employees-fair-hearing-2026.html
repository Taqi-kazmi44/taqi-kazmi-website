SC Restores PDA Employees Denied a Fair Hearing

Two government employees lost their jobs over charges that were never properly put to them. The Supreme Court has restored them, holding that the departmental proceedings denied them a fair and meaningful opportunity of defence.

What was reported

  1. Two Peshawar Development Authority (PDA) employees were dismissed over an alleged fraudulent transfer of a two-kanal plot in Hayatabad, Peshawar. The Peshawar High Court converted the dismissal into compulsory retirement but kept the misconduct finding.
  2. A Supreme Court bench comprising Justice Rozi Khan Barrech has now set aside both the Peshawar High Court judgment and the misconduct findings entirely.
  3. The reason: the allegations relied upon were materially different from the original charge sheets, and the employees were never given a chance to cross-examine witnesses during the inquiry.
  4. The court noted that the alleged plot transfer was intercepted before completion, so no actual financial loss was caused to the PDA or the public exchequer.

The key legal point

  1. An employee cannot be punished for allegations that were not part of the charge sheet. The charge sheet must be the same charge the employee is finally punished on.
  2. If the case is altered or enlarged during the proceedings, the employee must get a real chance to respond to the new allegations, including the right to cross-examine witnesses.
  3. Because the defect went to the root of the matter, it could not be cured merely by reducing the punishment, as the Peshawar High Court had done.
  4. Reinstatement after an unlawful dismissal is generally restored with salary, allowances and increments for the intervening period.
  5. Why it matters: in any departmental inquiry, safai ka moqa and gawahon se sawal ka haq are legal rights. Punishment on charges that were never formally put to the employee does not survive in court.
